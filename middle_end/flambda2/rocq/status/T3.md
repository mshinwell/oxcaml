# T3, the aliases of the typing environment: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/T3-aliases`. Nine series are merged: the first
(the structure, its meaning, the queries, `add`, the invariant as a
test, the renaming); the second (sets of aliases, `add_alias_set`, the
structure as a thing that contains names); the third (the extensions
of a typing environment, with their renaming, and two things that
other streams asked of the aliases); the fourth (`follow_aliases` on
the new aliases, which is to take the place of the validator's); the
fifth (for the differential tests); the sixth (the calls replayed
commute with renaming); the seventh (`follow_aliases` knows the
closures that a `Let_static` binds symbols to); and T3's slices of
the rewrites through types (the plan's simplifier streams, by
agreement with T2 and T4), the eighth (the projection of a value
slot) and the ninth (the types of the symbols of a `Let_static`).
What is left of the aliases is the validator's move to the new
`follow_aliases`, which waits for the tip that the integrator names.
On the branch now are a tenth series, a third slice (the environment
in which the body of code is rewritten, with the types of the
symbols), an eleventh (the symbols bound to closures get their
types, so that a call of one is made direct), and a twelfth, for the
validator's move (the new `follow_aliases` keeps scope). The guides are
`middle_end/flambda2/types/env/aliases.ml` and
`middle_end/flambda2/types/env/typing_env_extension.ml`.

## Done

The structure, its meaning, the order, the queries:

- The structure, with the three maps of the OCaml kept
  (`canonical_elements` and its two inverses). The aliases of one
  canonical element are one map from alias to recorded name mode, where
  the OCaml has two fields that say the same.
- `aliases_respected t vars`: every demoted name has the value of its
  canonical element under `vars`, values compared as `eval_simple`
  gives them (a value or none); asked of all three maps, so that the
  statements about values need no invariant.
- The order (`Aliases.defined_earlier`): a constant before any name, a
  symbol before any variable, variables by binding time then by the
  resolver; strict; two symbols not ordered.
- `get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode`: the canonical element, which is
  canonical (under `aliases_consistent`), earlier than every other
  element of its class (under `canonical_elements_earliest`), the
  constant of the class if there is one, never a variable if the class
  has a symbol.
- `get_canonical_element_exn`: the canonical element if its name mode
  suffices; else an alias of the least name mode that suffices, than
  which no other of that mode is earlier; `None` only if no alias may
  be used; the answer may be used at the mode asked for and is in the
  class asked about.
- Alias types: under respect, the alias type of a name and that of its
  canonical element have the same values.
- Respect depends on the environment only through the names the
  structure speaks of (`aliases_mention`).
- The equality test of simples decides equality; the canonical element
  of a canonical element is itself; a canonical element that is a name
  is the name asked about or one the structure speaks of (asked for by
  T5).

`add` and `get_aliases`:

- what `add` answers: the canonical element and the demoted name are
  the two arguments, the later one demoted (if neither is earlier, the
  first); `Bottom` exactly for two different constants; never `None`
  (the model's two fatal errors) for two different canonical arguments
  of a consistent structure;
- the classes after `add`, as an equation on canonical elements and as
  a relation: the two classes are merged, the others are as they were;
- respect is kept whenever the two arguments have the same value, with
  no condition on the structure;
- the invariant, part by part: `aliases_consistent` is kept for two
  canonical arguments; `name_modes_recorded` always;
  `canonical_elements_earliest` only if one argument is earlier than
  the other (`comparable`; false for two symbols, T3-2). The three are
  separate so that the environment's invariant can carry consistency
  and the recorded modes without the order;
- the names spoken of after `add` are among those before and the two
  arguments;
- the invariant, and the recorded name modes alone, read the map only
  on the names the structure speaks of (`well_formed_coincide`,
  `name_modes_recorded_coincide`);
- `get_aliases` under consistency: a simple is in the set exactly when
  it is aliased to the element asked about.

The invariant as a test (`FlambdaEnvAliasesInvariantSpec.v`):

- `aliases_well_formed_b` and its three parts, each answering `true`
  exactly when the proposition holds;
- a consistent structure whose canonical elements are earliest passes
  `Aliases.invariant` (the compiler's check); the converse fails
  (T3-3), shown by a structure;
- where `add` keeps the invariant its result passes
  `Aliases.invariant_add_result`;
- `add` of two different symbols to the empty structure answers and
  its result fails both checks, whatever the environment (T3-2).

Renaming and free variables (`FlambdaEnvAliasesRenamingSpec.v`; D29):

- `rename_aliases` (all three maps, keys and values),
  `rename_binding_times` (the keys of the environment's map),
  `rename_resolver` (the resolver, by the inverse renaming);
  `aliases_free_variables` (every variable written in the structure);
- identity, composition, extensionality; the free variables of the
  renamed structure are the renamed free variables; a renaming that
  fixes the free variables leaves the structure as it is, with `=`;
- the variables the structure speaks of are among its free variables
  (not conversely: the key of an empty set of aliases, a shadowed
  binding; R3's remark), and the names the renamed structure speaks of
  are the renamed names;
- `simple_equal`, `defined_earlier`, `get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode`,
  `get_canonical_element_exn`, `get_aliases` and `add` commute with
  renaming, with no premise; respect and the invariant are kept.
- A name is renamed by T1's `rename_name`
  (`FlambdaTypeRenameSpec.v`) and a map has its keys renamed by the
  map module's `Keyed.map_bindings`: the two definitions that this
  file had of its own, written before those existed, are gone (third
  series).

Third series (merged): `Typing_env_extension`, and
two things asked of the aliases.

- The functions on extensions (`to_map`, `from_map`, `bindings`,
  `fold`, `empty`, `is_empty`, `has_equation`, `one_equation`,
  `add_or_replace_equation`, `replace_equation`, `disjoint_union`,
  `check_equation`, `invariant`) and on extensions with extra variables
  (`fold`, `empty`, `add_definition`, `add_or_replace_equation`,
  `map_types`), on T1's `env_extension`.
- `bindings` has the equations that count, each name once, and is the
  list itself where no name has two equations; `fold` goes through it.
- What each function that makes an extension answers for a name, and
  what satisfies the answer; `add_or_replace_equation` keeps one
  equation for a name; `disjoint_union` answers when no name has an
  equation in both.
- `environment_extension_satisfied`, `level_extension_satisfied`: an
  extension whose equations are types that an environment has is
  satisfied by a valuation that satisfies the environment strictly and
  gives values to the names of the equations. With T2's
  `cut_equations_found` this is what the meet needs of a level that it
  cuts out and keeps in a case of a variant.
- `with_extra_variables_satisfied`: some values of the extra variables
  satisfy the equations; three statements.
- Renaming. `apply_renaming` and `free_names` of an extension are T1's
  `rename_env_extension` and `free_names_of_env_extension`, whose laws
  T1 states. Stated here: `to_map`, `bindings`, `is_empty`,
  `has_equation`, `one_equation`, `add_or_replace_equation` and
  `disjoint_union` commute with renaming, and `fold` for a function
  that commutes with it; `check_equation` and `invariant` do not see
  it. For extensions with extra variables, `apply_renaming` and
  `free_names` as the OCaml has them (the extra variables are renamed,
  and are free names): identity, composition, the free names of a
  renamed extension, a renaming that fixes the free names;
  `add_definition`, `add_or_replace_equation`, `map_types` and `fold`
  commute with renaming (the last two for functions that do); the
  renamed environment satisfies the renamed extension exactly when the
  environment satisfies the extension; and what satisfies an extension
  does not depend on the values of its extra variables
  (`with_extra_variables_bound`), which is the sense in which the
  meaning binds them though `free_names` lists them (T3-8).
- For the stream that states the renaming of typing environments (N3):
  the functions of the aliases read the environment's map for binding
  times and name modes only. For two resolvers that answer alike and
  two maps, over any two types, that give every name the same binding

  other. The proofs of the first three are N3's, moved here at its

Fourth series (merged): `follow_aliases` on the new aliases
(`FlambdaEnvAliasesFollowSpec.v`).

- What is known where a subterm is (`Known.t`): the aliases
  (`Aliases.t`), when each variable was bound, and the closures that
  variables were bound to by a `Let`. Every `Simple` is replaced by the
  canonical element of its class. Under a `Let` of a variable to a
  `Simple` the two are made aliases by `Aliases.add` on their canonical
  elements; the variable has the latest binding time and is the one
  demoted, so the answers are those of today's function.
- Closures, in both forms: the unnamed one of today's function, and
  the closure of a set of several functions (`Closure_of_set`), which
  today's does not know. A variable bound to the projection of a
  function slot from a known closure of a set is known to be the
  closure at the other slot, which is how all the closures of the
  compiler's `Let` of a set are known. A call of a known closure
  becomes a direct call of its code; the projection of a value slot
  becomes what the slot holds, and for `Project_value_slot_from` only
  if the closure is at the function slot named.
- Binders. A binder may bind a variable that is in scope already (the
  validator's own rewrites make such terms, by inlining), so binding a
  variable first forgets it (`Known.forget`), a function of the model
  only: the aliases are made again from the names demoted, leaving the
  variable out, with `Aliases.add`; where the variable is not written
  in them they are left as they are. Stated: what is left is respected
  whatever the variable is then bound to; two simples that were known
  to be equal, neither of them the variable, still are
  (`forget_aliased`; today's function loses that where the variable
  was the canonical element); the aliases stay consistent.
- `follow_aliases_respected`: the result is equivalent to the term in
  every environment that agrees with what is known;
  `follow_aliases_equivalent`: starting from nothing known, in every
  environment, which is the statement that the validator uses.
- It commutes with renaming, with no premise
  (`rename_follow_aliases`: the result for the renamed term, with what
  is known renamed by `rename_known`, is the result renamed;
  `rename_follow_aliases_empty` from nothing known), and so do
  `Known.bind` and `Known.under_let`. Asked for by N3, N1's rule and
  R3.
- Examples (`Examples` in the Proofs file): a constant, a chain of
  aliases, a variable bound again, a set of two closures with a direct
  call and a projection, a projection that names the wrong slot, a
  handler.
- Tried with the validator, on the side and not committed: with
  `FlambdaValidateSpec.v`, `FlambdaValidateProofs.v` and
  `FlambdaExtract.v` moved to the new function, `validate_unit_sound`
  is proved as it stands, and of the 48 examples of
  `middle_end/flambda2/tests/semantics/` all keep their answers but
  two, `set_sibling_projection.fl` and `set_code_in_two_sets.fl`,
  which go from "not validated" to "validated".

Fifth series (merged): what the differential tests asked for, and the
converse of `add_respected`.

- `canonical_element_determined`: two structures that are consistent,
  have their canonical elements earliest and have the same classes
  have the same canonical elements. So where the order decides, the
  order in which aliases were added does not matter.
- `add_alias_set_earliest`: `add_alias_set` keeps the canonical
  elements earliest when one of every two different canonical elements
  that it puts in one class is earlier than the other (no two symbols,
  no two variables bound at one time). With the statement before, the
  canonical elements afterwards are then the same whatever the order
  in which the names of the set are gone through, which the compiler
  and the model do differently.
- `add_respected_equal` and `add_respected_before`: an environment
  that respects the result of `add` gives the two arguments the same
  value, and respects the structure as it was (for a consistent
  structure and two canonical arguments). With `add_respected`, the
  result says what the structure said and that the two are equal, and
  nothing more.
- `Aliases_replay.run` (`FlambdaEnvAliasesReplay.v`), which T7 asked
  for: the calls are a plain type (`Add`, `Add_alias_set`,
  `Get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode`, `Get_canonical_element_exn`,
  `Get_aliases`, `Invariant`), and so are the answers; a fatal error
  of the compiler is the answer `Fatal_error`, after which the
  structure is as it was. Two examples in `FlambdaEnvAliasesSetProofs.v`.
  Extracted on the side to plain OCaml, which compiles.

Seventh series (merged): `follow_aliases` knows the closures that a
`Let_static` binds symbols to.

- `Known.t` has one more list, `static_closures`: for a symbol, the
  closure that a `Let_static` bound it to (`Known.static_closure`: the
  closure as for a variable, and for a closure of a static set the
  symbols that the set names for its function slots).
  `Known.under_let_static` records the symbols that a group binds to
  closures, for the body of the `Let_static`; `follow_aliases` goes
  into the body with it.
- What it does with them: a call of a symbol known to be a closure
  becomes a direct call; the projection of a value slot from it becomes
  what the slot holds, a variable included, where that variable has not
  been bound again; and, new, the projection of a function slot from a
  symbol known to be at the slot named becomes the symbol that the set
  names for the other slot (`canonical_project_function_slot`). A
  variable bound to a symbol is an alias of it, so all this is done for
  the variable too.
- `known_respected known statics vars` now takes the static
  environment: the closure recorded for a symbol is what the static
  environment binds the symbol to, with the values of the `Simple`s of
  its value slots. The statements of the fourth series have the static
  environment added where they had `known_respected`; they are
  otherwise as they were, and `follow_aliases_equivalent` (from nothing
  known, in every environment) is word for word the same.
- New statements: `known_respected_extend` (what is known stays true
  when the static environment binds more: the machine never binds a
  symbol twice), `under_let_static_respected` (the static environment
  that the group makes agrees with what is known in the body),
  `rename_under_let_static` (it commutes with renaming; symbols are
  not renamed, the `Simple`s of the value slots are).
- Where it is known: in the body of the `Let_static`, and in the
  handlers of non-recursive continuations defined there (a handler runs
  under a static environment that has grown since, which is why the
  statement above is needed). Not in code, and not before the binding.
- `Known.forget` forgets a symbol's closure whole when a variable that
  one of its value slots holds is bound again, as for a variable's.
- Three examples in the Proofs file: two static closures of one set
  with a value slot that holds a variable; the same with that variable
  bound again; a use before the binding.
- What it is for: a unit that exports a function which uses a value of
  the module computed when the module is run. Simplify lifts the
  closure with a value slot that holds the variable, and where the
  function is inlined it reads the slot as the variable. (The other
  thing such a unit needs, that the code is bound in the group that
  binds the symbol, is the validator's.)

Ninth series (merged): the types
of the symbols of a `Let_static`, after
`simplify/simplify_static_const.ml`.

- The function that puts the rewrites together defines the symbols of
  a `Let_static` with no types, so a load from a symbol bound to a
  block, or the unboxing of a symbol bound to a boxed number, is not
  answered. `add_static_bindings tenv bindings` is the environment
  that Simplify has in the body: the symbols bound to closures defined
  first, with no type; then each other symbol in the order of the
  group, its type taken in the environment before it is defined
  (`static_const_type_in`), then defined and given the type
  (`define_symbol_with_type`).
- The types: of a block, the type that `Make_block` gives its variable
  (tag of a scanned block, fields of the kind value; the model's
  static block is the compiler's immutable block of the shape
  `Value_only`, and its mutable, unique and mixed static blocks are
  not constants of the model); of a boxed
  number, the type that `Box_number` gives its variable, the kind of
  number read off the kind of the type of the contents; of an immutable
  string, the type of that string. A closure gets none here (that is
  with the closure types, T4's).
- Statements: a symbol that the static environment binds to the value
  of the constant has the type (`static_const_typed`), which speaks of
  variables in scope only; the environment with the symbol defined and
  typed is satisfied (`symbol_typed_env_satisfies`), and so is
  `add_static_bindings tenv bindings` by the static environment that
  the group makes (`add_static_bindings_env_satisfies`); the body of
  the `Let_static` is rewritten under it (`let_static_typed`), with no
  premise on the term. For T2's assembly: which symbols it has defined
  (`add_static_bindings_defined_symbols`) and that it commutes with
  renaming (`add_static_bindings_rename`,
  `static_const_type_in_rename`). An example: the first field of a
  static block loaded from its symbol is the `Simple` that the field
  was given.
- Where the model and the compiler part: for a boxed number whose
  contents are a variable the compiler stops with a fatal error
  (T3-10); the model's constant has a value, and the type given is the
  type of that value.

Tenth series (on the branch, not merged): the environment in which

- Statements: where it answers, it is that environment

  variables (`closure_env_env_satisfies_strictly`); the environment of

## What a differential test of the aliases may compare (for T7)

The compiler's `Aliases.t` is abstract, so a test replays the same
calls on both sides, from `empty`, and compares answers.

The inputs on which the two are to agree:

- no coercions: every `Simple` has the identity coercion (the model has
  none);
- all the variables are of one compilation unit: the model leaves out
  the comparison of compilation units in `name_defined_earlier`;
- the same total `binding_time_resolver` on both sides, and maps that
  give each name the same binding time and name mode (what else the
  map holds is not read: `add_alike` and the statements beside it);
- the compiler's invariant checks off, but where the test is of the
  checks themselves (the last row);
- `add` is given what `aliases.mli` asks: two elements that are
  different and are canonical on both sides (as
  `get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode` answers them on each).

How far a replay is compared:

- Only as far as the first fatal error of the compiler. The model's
  replay goes on from there with the structure as it was
  (`Fatal_error`), and the compiler's side has no structure to go on
  with: the answers after it are not compared.
- The rules below that say "exactly" are for a replay in which the two
  sides have had the same canonical elements so far. They have, from
  `empty`, until an `add_alias_set` puts in one class two canonical
  elements neither of which is earlier than the other (two symbols, or
  two variables with one binding time): from that call on the two
  sides may have different canonical elements for a class, and then
  the answers of `get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode` and of
  `get_canonical_element_exn` may differ, and what is canonical, and
  so a valid argument of `add`, may differ too. After such a call
  compare the classes only (`get_aliases`, as sets), or stop the
  replay there.
- The model's answers do not depend on which variables a test takes:
  the same calls with the variables permuted, the map and the resolver
  permuted with them, give the same answers permuted
  (`rename_replay`). Nothing like it is stated of the compiler, whose
  choice between equally early names goes by its order on names: a
  test that takes fresh variables at each run meets those choices
  only in the cases above.

What to compare:

- `add`: `Bottom` or not, the name demoted and the canonical element.
  Exactly: both sides demote the later of the two, and the first
  argument if neither is earlier (`add_result`), so a tie between the
  two arguments is no difference.
- `get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode`: the answer, exactly.
- `get_aliases`: the constant, and the names as a set (the model's
  list may have them in another order), always.
- `get_canonical_element_exn`: `Not_found` against `None`, and the
  answer. Exactly, but in one case: the canonical element may not be
  used, and two aliases of the least name mode that will do are
  equally early. Then each side answers one of them (`earliest_alias`),
  the compiler the first in the order of names, the model the first of
  its list. There are no two such when different variables have
  different binding times in the map.
- `Alias_set.inter`, `filter`: as sets, always. `is_empty`,
  `get_singleton`: the answer, always.
- `Alias_set.find_best`: exactly if the set has a constant, or exactly
  one symbol, or no symbol and exactly one variable. Otherwise both
  sides answer a symbol of the set, or both a variable of the set
  (`find_best_has`, `find_best_best`), and which one is not compared.
- `Alias_set.choose_opt`: whether there is an answer, always; the
  answer exactly for a set of one element, and otherwise an element of
  the set on each side. No answer on either side for a set with a
  constant and names (T3-4).
- `add_alias_set`: the classes afterwards (`get_aliases` of each name
  involved, as sets), always (`add_alias_set_aliased`). The canonical
  elements too, when one of every two different canonical elements put
  in one class is earlier than the other: no two symbols, no two
  variables with one binding time (`add_alias_set_earliest`,
  `canonical_element_determined`). Otherwise the two sides go through
  the names in different orders and may end with different canonical
  elements.
- With the compiler's invariant checks on: a fatal error of
  `invariant` or of `invariant_add_result` against the model's
  `Aliases.invariant` or `Aliases.invariant_add_result` answering
  `false` (`FlambdaEnvAliasesInvariantSpec.v`).

Where the compiler stops and the model answers `None`:

- `add`: "already added to [Aliases_of_canonical_element]" (an
  argument that is not canonical) and "Cannot demote a constant" (one
  constant given twice). Neither happens on the inputs above
  (`add_defined`).
- `add_alias_set`: those two, and "Discovered Bottom during alias
  join" (two different constants put in one class; T3-9).
- `get_canonical_element_exn`: `Not_found` is `None`.

What is not to be taken for a difference:

- `add` of two symbols answers on both sides and demotes the first.
  With the invariant checks on the compiler then stops in
  `invariant_add_result` (T3-2), and the model's
  `Aliases.invariant_add_result` answers `false`: the same thing.
- Two variables of the unit being compiled with one binding time:
  through `Typing_env` the compiler's resolver stops with a fatal
  error (T3-1). A test that gives `Aliases.add` a total resolver of its
  own does not meet it, and the two sides agree.
- With light invariant checks on, the compiler's `add` of a simple and
  itself stops ("Cannot alias an element to itself"). With them off,
  for a name given twice it answers the structure as it was, as the
  model does; for a constant given twice it stops ("Cannot demote a
  constant"), which is `None` in the model, as said above.
