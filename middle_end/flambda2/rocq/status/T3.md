# T3, the aliases of the typing environment: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/T3-aliases`. The first series (the structure,
its meaning, the queries, `add`, the invariant as a test, the renaming)
and the second (sets of aliases, `add_alias_set`, the structure as a
thing that contains names) are merged. What is on the branch now is a
third series: the extensions of a typing environment, with their
renaming, and two things that other streams asked of the aliases; and
above it a fourth: `follow_aliases` on the new aliases, which is to
take the place of the validator's. The guides are
`middle_end/flambda2/types/env/aliases.ml` and
`middle_end/flambda2/types/env/typing_env_extension.ml`.

Files, all in `middle_end/flambda2/types/rocq/`:

- `FlambdaEnvAliases.v`: the structure and the functions, as the OCaml
  has them, in a module `Aliases`. Extracts (to plain OCaml, checked on
  the side; not yet in `FlambdaExtract.v`).
- `FlambdaEnvAliasesSpec.v`: what the structure means, the order, what
  the queries and `add` answer. Its header lists every difference from
  the OCaml. 50 statements.
- `FlambdaEnvAliasesProofs.v`: the proofs, and the module `Examples`.
- `FlambdaEnvAliasesInvariantSpec.v` and
  `FlambdaEnvAliasesInvariantProofs.v`: the invariant as a test, and
  what the compiler's two checks answer. 10 statements.
- `FlambdaEnvAliasesRenamingSpec.v` and
  `FlambdaEnvAliasesRenamingProofs.v`: the renaming of the structure,
  its free variables, that the functions commute with renaming, and
  the structure as a thing that contains names. 16 statements.
- `FlambdaEnvAliasesSetSpec.v` and `FlambdaEnvAliasesSetProofs.v`: sets
  of aliases, and making a name the alias of a set; examples. 26
  statements.
- `FlambdaBindingTime.v`: binding times and name modes. Written here
  (the first commit of the branch) because the aliases need the order;
  owned by T2. T3 does not edit it.

- `FlambdaTypingEnvExtension.v`, `FlambdaTypingEnvExtensionSpec.v` and
  `FlambdaTypingEnvExtensionProofs.v`: the extensions of a typing
  environment, after `types/env/typing_env_extension.ml` (taken off T2
  by agreement). 39 statements.
- `FlambdaEnvAliasesFollowSpec.v` and `FlambdaEnvAliasesFollowProofs.v`:
  following aliases through a term with the new aliases (fourth
  series). 14 statements.

155 statements, all proved; no axiom, no `Admitted`.

Today's `FlambdaAliasesSpec.v` and `FlambdaAliasesProofs.v` (the
validator's `follow_aliases` on the naive types) are untouched; the
validator still runs them.

## Done

The structure, its meaning, the order, the queries:

- The structure, with the three maps of the OCaml kept
  (`canonical_elements` and its two inverses). The aliases of one
  canonical element are one map from alias to recorded name mode, where
  the OCaml has two fields that say the same.
- `aliases_respected t vars`: every demoted name has the value of its
  canonical element under `vars`, values compared as `eval_simple`
  gives them (a value or none); asked of all three maps, so that the
  statements about values need no invariant.
- The order (`Aliases.defined_earlier`): a constant before any name, a
  symbol before any variable, variables by binding time then by the
  resolver; strict; two symbols not ordered.
- `get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode`: the canonical element, which is
  canonical (under `aliases_consistent`), earlier than every other
  element of its class (under `canonical_elements_earliest`), the
  constant of the class if there is one, never a variable if the class
  has a symbol.
- `get_canonical_element_exn`: the canonical element if its name mode
  suffices; else an alias of the least name mode that suffices, than
  which no other of that mode is earlier; `None` only if no alias may
  be used; the answer may be used at the mode asked for and is in the
  class asked about.
- Alias types: under respect, the alias type of a name and that of its
  canonical element have the same values.
- Respect depends on the environment only through the names the
  structure speaks of (`aliases_mention`).
- The equality test of simples decides equality; the canonical element
  of a canonical element is itself; a canonical element that is a name
  is the name asked about or one the structure speaks of (asked for by
  T5).

`add` and `get_aliases`:

- what `add` answers: the canonical element and the demoted name are
  the two arguments, the later one demoted (if neither is earlier, the
  first); `Bottom` exactly for two different constants; never `None`
  (the model's two fatal errors) for two different canonical arguments
  of a consistent structure;
- the classes after `add`, as an equation on canonical elements and as
  a relation: the two classes are merged, the others are as they were;
- respect is kept whenever the two arguments have the same value, with
  no condition on the structure;
- the invariant, part by part: `aliases_consistent` is kept for two
  canonical arguments; `name_modes_recorded` always;
  `canonical_elements_earliest` only if one argument is earlier than
  the other (`comparable`; false for two symbols, T3-2). The three are
  separate so that the environment's invariant can carry consistency
  and the recorded modes without the order;
- the names spoken of after `add` are among those before and the two
  arguments;
- the invariant, and the recorded name modes alone, read the map only
  on the names the structure speaks of (`well_formed_coincide`,
  `name_modes_recorded_coincide`);
- `get_aliases` under consistency: a simple is in the set exactly when
  it is aliased to the element asked about.

The invariant as a test (`FlambdaEnvAliasesInvariantSpec.v`):

- `aliases_well_formed_b` and its three parts, each answering `true`
  exactly when the proposition holds;
- a consistent structure whose canonical elements are earliest passes
  `Aliases.invariant` (the compiler's check); the converse fails
  (T3-3), shown by a structure;
- where `add` keeps the invariant its result passes
  `Aliases.invariant_add_result`;
- `add` of two different symbols to the empty structure answers and
  its result fails both checks, whatever the environment (T3-2).

Renaming and free variables (`FlambdaEnvAliasesRenamingSpec.v`; D29):

- `rename_aliases` (all three maps, keys and values),
  `rename_binding_times` (the keys of the environment's map),
  `rename_resolver` (the resolver, by the inverse renaming);
  `aliases_free_variables` (every variable written in the structure);
- identity, composition, extensionality; the free variables of the
  renamed structure are the renamed free variables; a renaming that
  fixes the free variables leaves the structure as it is, with `=`;
- the variables the structure speaks of are among its free variables
  (not conversely: the key of an empty set of aliases, a shadowed
  binding; R3's remark), and the names the renamed structure speaks of
  are the renamed names;
- `simple_equal`, `defined_earlier`, `get_canonical_ignoring_name_mode`,
  `get_canonical_element_exn`, `get_aliases` and `add` commute with
  renaming, with no premise; respect and the invariant are kept.
- A name is renamed by T1's `rename_name`
  (`FlambdaTypeRenameSpec.v`) and a map has its keys renamed by the
  map module's `Keyed.map_bindings`: the two definitions that this
  file had of its own, written before those existed, are gone (third
  series).

Second series (merged):

- Four facts that the meet and the join asked for:
  `canonical_element_unmentioned` (a name the structure does not speak
  of is its own canonical element); `add_name_constant` (a canonical
  name and a constant: `add` answers, in either order, and demotes the
  name); `add_is_canonical` (what is canonical after `add` of two
  different canonical elements is what was, but for the name demoted:
  one canonical name fewer); `add_classes_uniform` (whatever is the
  same on each class and on the two arguments is the same on each
  class afterwards: how classes stay of one kind).
- The rest of `Alias_set` (`choose_opt`, `get_singleton`, `inter`,
  `filter`, `find_best`) and `Aliases.add_alias_set`, as the OCaml has
  them. Which simples each set has; `find_best` answers an element
  that none is better than (constant, then symbol, then variable);
  `get_singleton` answers for a set of exactly one; `choose_opt` has
  no answer for a set with a constant and names (T3-4). Of
  `add_alias_set`: the classes afterwards are those before with the
  class of the name and those of the elements merged, and nothing else;
  respect, consistency and the recorded name modes are kept; no fatal
  error when an environment respects the structure and gives the
  elements the value of the name; the names spoken of afterwards.
  The functions on sets and `add_alias_set` commute with renaming
  (`filter` with the test renamed), as R3 asked. Examples: the aliases
  of a variable at two uses, those common to both, the one that the
  join records; the best alias; two constants that meet.
- `aliases_contains_names`, the instance of N1's
  `Nominal.contains_names` for the variables (action
  `rename_aliases (Renaming p [])`, free names
  `aliases_free_variables`, equality `=`), and `aliases_nominal`.

Examples (`Examples` in `FlambdaEnvAliasesProofs.v`): two variables, a
variable and a constant, a variable and a symbol, two constants, two
symbols, a class with a constant and two variables, the canonical
element at a name mode in three scopes, the `Phantom` case;
environments that do and do not respect each; well-formedness by hand

Third series (on the branch, not merged): `Typing_env_extension`, and
two things asked of the aliases.

- The functions on extensions (`to_map`, `from_map`, `bindings`,
  `fold`, `empty`, `is_empty`, `has_equation`, `one_equation`,
  `add_or_replace_equation`, `replace_equation`, `disjoint_union`,
  `check_equation`, `invariant`) and on extensions with extra variables
  (`fold`, `empty`, `add_definition`, `add_or_replace_equation`,
  `map_types`), on T1's `env_extension`.
- `bindings` has the equations that count, each name once, and is the
  list itself where no name has two equations; `fold` goes through it.
- What each function that makes an extension answers for a name, and
  what satisfies the answer; `add_or_replace_equation` keeps one
  equation for a name; `disjoint_union` answers when no name has an
  equation in both.
- `environment_extension_satisfied`, `level_extension_satisfied`: an
  extension whose equations are types that an environment has is
  satisfied by a valuation that satisfies the environment strictly and
  gives values to the names of the equations. With T2's
  `cut_equations_found` this is what the meet needs of a level that it
  cuts out and keeps in a case of a variant.
- `with_extra_variables_satisfied`: some values of the extra variables
  satisfy the equations; three statements.
- Renaming. `apply_renaming` and `free_names` of an extension are T1's
  `rename_env_extension` and `free_names_of_env_extension`, whose laws
  T1 states. Stated here: `to_map`, `bindings`, `is_empty`,
  `has_equation`, `one_equation`, `add_or_replace_equation` and
  `disjoint_union` commute with renaming, and `fold` for a function
  that commutes with it; `check_equation` and `invariant` do not see
  it. For extensions with extra variables, `apply_renaming` and
  `free_names` as the OCaml has them (the extra variables are renamed,
  and are free names): identity, composition, the free names of a
  renamed extension, a renaming that fixes the free names;
  `add_definition`, `add_or_replace_equation`, `map_types` and `fold`
  commute with renaming (the last two for functions that do); the
  renamed environment satisfies the renamed extension exactly when the
  environment satisfies the extension; and what satisfies an extension
  does not depend on the values of its extra variables
  (`with_extra_variables_bound`), which is the sense in which the
  meaning binds them though `free_names` lists them (T3-8).
- For the stream that states the renaming of typing environments (N3):
  the functions of the aliases read the environment's map for binding
  times and name modes only. For two resolvers that answer alike and
  two maps, over any two types, that give every name the same binding
  time and name mode (`binding_times_alike`): `defined_earlier`,
  `add`, `get_canonical_element_exn` and `invariant` answer the same,
  and `aliases_well_formed` holds for the one when it holds for the
  other. The proofs of the first three are N3's, moved here at its
  request.

Fourth series (on the branch above the third, not merged):
`follow_aliases` on the new aliases (`FlambdaEnvAliasesFollowSpec.v`).

- What is known where a subterm is (`Known.t`): the aliases
  (`Aliases.t`), when each variable was bound, and the closures that
  variables were bound to by a `Let`. Every `Simple` is replaced by the
  canonical element of its class. Under a `Let` of a variable to a
  `Simple` the two are made aliases by `Aliases.add` on their canonical
  elements; the variable has the latest binding time and is the one
  demoted, so the answers are those of today's function.
- Closures, in both forms: the unnamed one of today's function, and
  the closure of a set of several functions (`Closure_of_set`), which
  today's does not know. A variable bound to the projection of a
  function slot from a known closure of a set is known to be the
  closure at the other slot, which is how all the closures of the
  compiler's `Let` of a set are known. A call of a known closure
  becomes a direct call of its code; the projection of a value slot
  becomes what the slot holds, and for `Project_value_slot_from` only
  if the closure is at the function slot named.
- Binders. A binder may bind a variable that is in scope already (the
  validator's own rewrites make such terms, by inlining), so binding a
  variable first forgets it (`Known.forget`), a function of the model
  only: the aliases are made again from the names demoted, leaving the
  variable out, with `Aliases.add`; where the variable is not written
  in them they are left as they are. Stated: what is left is respected
  whatever the variable is then bound to; two simples that were known
  to be equal, neither of them the variable, still are
  (`forget_aliased`; today's function loses that where the variable
  was the canonical element); the aliases stay consistent.
- `follow_aliases_respected`: the result is equivalent to the term in
  every environment that agrees with what is known;
  `follow_aliases_equivalent`: starting from nothing known, in every
  environment, which is the statement that the validator uses.
- It commutes with renaming, with no premise
  (`rename_follow_aliases`: the result for the renamed term, with what
  is known renamed by `rename_known`, is the result renamed;
  `rename_follow_aliases_empty` from nothing known), and so do
  `Known.bind` and `Known.under_let`. Asked for by N3, N1's rule and
  R3.
- Examples (`Examples` in the Proofs file): a constant, a chain of
  aliases, a variable bound again, a set of two closures with a direct
  call and a projection, a projection that names the wrong slot, a
  handler.
- Tried with the validator, on the side and not committed: with
  `FlambdaValidateSpec.v`, `FlambdaValidateProofs.v` and
  `FlambdaExtract.v` moved to the new function, `validate_unit_sound`
  is proved as it stands, and of the 48 examples of
  `middle_end/flambda2/tests/semantics/` all keep their answers but
  two, `set_sibling_projection.fl` and `set_code_in_two_sets.fl`,
  which go from "not validated" to "validated".

## Decided overnight (2026-11-11 to 12), for the human to review

- `Alias_set.find_best` and `choose_opt` among several names, and the
  order in which `add_alias_set` goes through the names of a set: the
  compiler uses the order on names. Options: give the model an order on
  names; or take the first of the list and state only what holds of
  either choice. Chosen: the second, as for `find_earliest_alias`
  (T3-7). The statements do not depend on the choice; a differential
  test that compares the element chosen would. To change: add an order
  on names to the model, which the rules for names forbid today.
- A fatal error in `add_alias_set` ("Discovered Bottom during alias
  join", and those of `add0`) is `None`. Proved absent when some
  environment respects the structure and gives the elements of the set
  the value of the name. Nothing weaker was needed.
- `add_alias_set` is not said to keep `canonical_elements_earliest`.
  It would need each pair it adds to be comparable, which fails for two
  symbols (T3-2); stated nowhere rather than with a premise on every
  pair. To change: add the statement with that premise.

- An extension taken from an environment. T1's
  `env_extension_satisfied` asks that every name with an equation has
  a value; T2's satisfaction of an environment asks nothing of a name
  with no value. So the extension of a level cut out of an environment
  is satisfied only if the valuation gives values to the names of the
  level's equations. Options: an extension asks a type only of a name
  that has a value (T1's meaning moves); or every user shows that the
  names have values. Chosen for now: neither meaning is touched and the
  statements have the premise, with a CR
  (`environment_extension_satisfied_statement`). T1, T2, T4 and R3
  were told. T4 can give the premise (its contract of the meet says
  that no equation is added on a name with no value) and needs the
  strong meaning where the meet adds the extension of a case; T1 keeps
  the meaning and recommends that; T2 would have an extension ask a
  type only of a name that has a value. To change: drop the premise if
  the meaning moves.
- `With_extra_variables`: its meaning binds the extra variables (some
  values of them satisfy the equations), though the compiler's
  `free_names` lists them and its interface says they are not treated
  as a binding site. Chosen because `Expand_head` makes them as fresh
  variables for names the target environment lacks. A CR is at
  `with_extra_variables_satisfied`. To change: a meaning in which they
  are free is the plain extension's.
- `add_or_replace_equation` takes the earlier equation of the name out
  (unbind, then cons) where the convention of the maps is to cons: so
  that an extension keeps one equation for a name, which T1's
  well-formedness of types asks. The lookups are the same either way.

- `follow_aliases` on the new aliases is written with the aliases
  alone (`Aliases.t`, a map of binding times, a list of known
  closures), not with T2's typing environment. Options: the whole
  environment, defining each variable in it with its kind and adding
  an alias type; or the aliases alone. Chosen: the aliases alone. The
  validator extracts and runs it, and with the whole environment it
  would extract the grammar of types and the environment for no more
  answers; what a rewrite that goes through a term with the whole
  environment needs (kinds of binders, the join at handlers) is for
  the simplifier streams. To change: that traversal, when it exists,
  takes its place in the validator.
- The closures that it knows are a list of its own (the variable, the
  function slot, the functions and the value slots), not closure
  types read by a prover. Chosen so that the validator knows the sets
  of several functions now; it goes when the provers can answer the
  same from a type.
- A binder that binds a variable in scope: `Known.forget`, which
  remakes the aliases without the variable. Options: ask that the
  binders of a term be all different (the validator's own rewrites
  break that); forget everything at such a binder; forget the
  variable only. Chosen: the last, so that nothing is lost that
  today's function keeps. It is a function of the model only.
- Where `Aliases.add` answers bottom there (a variable bound to a
  constant where it is known to be another constant), the aliases are
  left as they were. The subterm cannot run, and could be replaced by
  `Invalid`; not done, as today's function does not.
- A variable that the term does not bind has the binding time of the
  variables of other compilation units, so that it is earlier than
  every variable the term binds and is never demoted to one.

## Open questions

- For the human: `add` on two symbols. The model follows the compiler
  (the first argument is demoted; the order invariant then fails, and
  `add_two_symbols_invariant_statement` says so). Two different symbols
  are never equal at run time, so `Bottom` would be sound and more
  precise. Recommendation: keep the model as the compiler is, and
  decide for the compiler whether `add` should answer `Bottom` or
  `invariant` should allow two symbols.
- For the human: ties of binding times (the question in the Spec at
  `variable_earlier_statement`): is the comment in
  `Typing_env_level.concat`, that a join may give several variables
  one binding time, still true? If it is, T2's invariant is false of
  the compiler's environments.
