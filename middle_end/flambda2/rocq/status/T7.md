# T7, differential tests of types and the converter: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/T7-difftest`. Everything of mine is in
`middle_end/flambda2/tests/semantics/types/`:

- `model_numbers.ml`, `model_names.ml`, `type_converter.ml`: the
  converter between `Type_grammar.t` and the model's `flambda_type`,
  both ways, with the numbers and the numbering of names. Trusted,
  unverified. Library `flambda2_rocq_types_converter`.
- `model_type.ml` (same library): when two types of the model are the
  same (up to how maps and sets are written), and their printer. Part
  of what the tests are trusted for, not of the conversion.
- `type_generator.ml`: a seeded generator of types of the model, raw
  or keeping what the compiler's creation functions keep, and a
  shrinker.
- `type_comparisons.ml`: how a comparison is run and reported, and
  the comparisons of one type, for both programs below.
- `types_difftest.ml`, `types_difftest.expected`: the comparisons on
  generated and chosen types. Under the `runtest` alias of the
  semantics tests; a few seconds.
- `types_of_compilations.ml`, with `types_of_compilations.units` and
  `types_of_compilations.expected`: the comparisons of one type on
  the types that Simplify computes for the units listed. Under the
  same alias; half a minute.

This file cites no commit: the commits of a series get other hashes
when it is applied.

## The series

The first four series are on the integration branch. The first:
who owns the tests, the lines for the shared lists, the converter,
the generator and the comparisons, and seventeen commits of tests
and repairs, with the two items of the trust ledger. The second: the
examples of the join, the equality of types and the predicate of
well-formed types, the comparisons of one type in a module of their
own, the types that Simplify computes, and two repairs after review.
The third: the examples of the meet run in the compiler, two chosen
meets, and twenty more creation functions with `must_be_singleton`
and `is_alias_of_name`. The fourth: the meet and the join of the
model, extracted (five `Require Import` lines and twenty-one names
in the shared lists; sixteen more extracted modules), each run
beside the n-way one of the compiler on the examples, on chosen
meets and joins and on generated types; and, after review, the
variables that the uses bind among the names asked of a join, with
a control. What their commits did, and what the epochs and the
reviews changed in them, is in the history of this file.

The fifth series, on that, each commit building with the outputs of
the two tests equal to their expected files:

1. the meet and the join of the model on types that are alike;
2. its status;
3. what the generator of such types does, said exactly (after
   review: two commits of comments, and this file; no code);
4. its status.

The sixth series, on the fifth, built in the same way:

1. the meet and the join of the model on types one step from a
   common type;
2. the scenarios of the meet of the model with their variables
   defined in the reverse order;
3. the differences with the binary meet where an extension has an
   equation on a symbol, counted;
4. this status.

Neither changes compiler code or a `.v` file.

With the owner of the grammar and meaning of types:

- `Env_extension` holds a `Keyed.finite_map name flambda_type`, the
  known tags a `Keyed.finite_map Z`. `type_satisfied_b` is a bare
  boolean with its parts callable on their own.
- Told to that owner, for its header: what the converter drops (the
  `rec_info` of a function type, the expression of a `Rec_info` head,
  the string of `Poison`), what it refuses, how numbers are read.
- `free_variables` of a type with a shadowed binding lists the
  variables of the shadowed binding; the compiler has no such binding.
  The comparison is exact on types without shadowed bindings and an
  inclusion otherwise.
- `these_naked_immediates` and `these_tagged_immediates` on a list
  with a repeat give a head with the set, where the compiler, given
  the set, gives the alias to the constant. The two mean the same.

With the owner of the typing environment: a valuation may give no
value to a variable that the environment knows, and nothing is then
asked of it; the obligations of meet and join are stated with no name
exempt. My check of a join follows this.

Rulings I work under: the n-way join is the one to model and the
binary join is an oracle to compare with (D27); a name holding poison
is exempt when types are checked against a run, and counted (D26);
physical equality is not modelled, and the compiler is run with the
structural setting of the flag that removes the dependence (D30);
where the model is coarser than the compiler's equality of types
(`rec_info`, the expression of a `Rec_info` head, the string of
`Poison`, a relation bound to no name), the correspondence is stated
only on types that do not differ there, and such differences are
counted apart.

## Decided overnight

For the sixth series:

- The scenarios with their variables in another order. Options:
  rename the variables by a permutation (it needs the renaming of
  types, which is not extracted); or keep the numbers and move the
  definitions. Chosen: the definitions before every other step, in
  the reverse order. Cost: one other order only, and what is
  permuted is the binding times, not the names.
- The inputs counted for T7-9 are told by the text of the two
  types (an extension has an equation on a symbol), not by what
  the meets do: a difference counted there could have another
  cause. The four shown, one of each run, were looked at with both
  answers printed (not committed), and are of T7-9.
- Three types are made for a pair of types one step from a common
  type, the third being unused by the meets, so that the runs of
  meets and of joins make their types by one function.

For the fifth series:

- How two types with a part in common are made. Options: a
  generator of pairs written for it; or the shrinker, of which one
  step gives, for a type, types whose text differs from it in one
  place. Chosen: the shrinker: a type and, on either side, one of
  the types of the same kind that one step gives for it; the type
  itself where a step gives none, and 15 times in 100 where it
  gives some. A step makes a part unknown (the whole type or its
  head, at times), or removes one thing: a case, a field, a slot,
  a number of a set, a string of a set or a character of a string,
  an equation of an extension, the variable of a relation. It is a
  change of the text, not an order between types: with a case or a
  number removed a type most often says more, with a part unknown
  it says less, with a character removed from a string each of the
  two has values that the other has not; and the second type need
  not keep the structure of the first. "Smaller", in the titles of
  the runs, means no more than that. Cost: nothing is known of how
  the two types compare; the only limit is of the text, the second
  being one step from the first. With a field removed from a block
  of a mixed shape, the fields after it are of another kind than
  their places ask: on those (three inputs, each looked at) the
  compiler stops on the mismatch of kinds and the model has no
  answer.
- At a third use of a join, the type passed is one further step
  of the shrinker from the first type of the pair as it is
  returned, or that first type again where the shrinker gives none
  of its kind. The first of the pair may already be the shrunk
  one.
- The older runs make their types as they did: the types that are
  alike are made only where a run asks for them, so no count of
  the fourth series moved.
- T7-9 is shown on built types. Seven variations of its input
  were run by hand with the three meets (not committed): with a
  variable in place of the symbol the three agree. From OCaml
  source it was tried by another stream, twice, and not
  reproduced; the same stream showed that a meet of two types
  without extensions makes the equation that is lost (a field that
  is an alias of a symbol of unknown type, met with a block).

For the fourth series:

- The variables that a use of a join binds were not asked of at
  first: they have numbers of the tests, so they were neither
  among the names asked nor reached as made variables, and a
  target that differed only in what it says of one would have
  passed. The reviewer found it. They are now asked, on both
  sides, and a control that fails the test if such a difference is
  not seen is run with it.

- Where the extracted meet and join go. Options: a library of their
  own, or the one extraction. Chosen, with the integrator: the one
  `FlambdaExtract.v` and its directory, which gains sixteen modules
  (some 8500 lines). Cost: the library of the semantics tests is
  larger; its split is the integrator's to do.
- How two answers are compared. Options: a converter between the
  typing environments of the model and of the compiler, compared
  whole; or what can be asked of an environment (the type and the
  canonical element of each name, the types of the variables made),
  through the converter of types only. Chosen: the latter, which
  needs no new trusted conversion. Cost: what is not asked is not
  compared (binding times, scopes, name modes, the levels, the
  aliases as sets).
- The variables that a meet or a join makes have other names on the
  two sides. They are renamed in the order in which the print of
  what is seen comes to them. Two answers that differ only in the
  order in which a map lists equations on such variables would be
  reported as different; none was.
- Where neither side answers (the model has `None`, the compiler a
  fatal error or a failed assertion) the two are counted as the
  same, and the two reasons are shown; they are not compared.
  Where the model alone has no answer the case is recorded under a
  label of its own and does not fail the test (T4-10, or the fuel);
  there is none so far.
- The fuel is 200 for each of the three fuels of the model, and the
  depth of joins is read from the compiler's flag (5).
