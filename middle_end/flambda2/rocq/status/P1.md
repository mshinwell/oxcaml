# P1, primitives that stop OCaml programs from being checked: status

What this stream has in the tree, in the order in which it went
there: the primitives up to the identities of arithmetic; the wider
loads and the swap of bytes; `Reinterpret_64_bit_word`; the table of
C functions; the mutable arrays of values, as cells of the heap,
with two statements about arrays and examples asked at the review;
the request for static immutable arrays; the static immutable
arrays of values, in the model and in the conversion; and the
immutable array as a value that a primitive makes, in the model and
in the conversion. Leaf additions to the primitives files
(`FlambdaPrimitive*`, C1's, delegated by the orchestrator), the
conversion of primitives in
`middle_end/flambda2/tests/tools/flsemantics.ml`, and examples; but
the last, which adds a form of value and so has a case in the files
of every stream that goes by cases on values. No change to the
signature of `eval_prim`. The statements of two laws changed for
the static immutable arrays, and those of the same two again for
the array as a value: see the two sections. The lifting of such an
array to a symbol followed, written by the owner of the lifting
after a request of this stream, with one statement of this stream
restated and five added. Last, a pass of the validator that reads
the length and the elements of an immutable array that the term
makes or binds to a symbol, applied in the rounds of the term
before.

## The order of work

1. `Phys_equal`: done.
2. `Num_conv` between the integer kinds: done.
3. The primitives that V2's ranking of what units made from OCaml
   sources need asks for, in its order, where they are leaf work:
   `Boolean_not` (done), `Int_shift` (done), then float arithmetic and
   comparison (not started; see "Floats"). Not P1's: regions and local
   allocation, anything that needs a heap, exceptions.
4. What sensible programs use beyond that list: `String_length` of an
   immutable string (done); the unary `Int_arith` with `Not`, which is
   what Simplify makes of `lnot` (done); the load of one byte from an
   immutable string (done); the mutability of `Block_load`, for a
   load that the source marks as one of a field that may be written
   (done).
5. A pass of the validator for the identities of arithmetic that
   Simplify applies (`x + 0` is `x`, and the like): done, to be wired
   in by V2; see "The identities of arithmetic".

6. What the conversion of a unit still stops on (see "What stops the
   conversion today"): the survey is done; of what it leaves that is
   leaf work, the loads of two, four and eight bytes from a string
   (done) and the swap of bytes (done). Floats are a request
   (`requests/P1-floats.md`), not started.
7. Calls of C functions, which are what a plain program on strings
   meets next (the equality of two strings, a `match` on a string):
   a request (`requests/P1-c-calls.md`), as the change is the
   core's. What does not depend on the core is done: the table of
   the functions (item 9).
8. `Reinterpret_64_bit_word` between a tagged immediate and an int64:
   done. With it the unary and binary primitives of the compiler that
   are on integers alone are all in the model. What is left of the
   compiler's primitives needs the heap (arrays, bytes, bigarrays,
   atomics, the copies of blocks), floats or vectors, regions, or
   addresses (`Int_as_pointer`, `Get_header`, `Peek`, `Poke`).
9. The table of C functions, as leaf definitions that nothing uses
   (the orchestrator's instruction): the comparisons of strings and
   the polymorphic comparisons, in `FlambdaExtcall*`: done. Wiring a
   call into the machine is C3's and the human's.

10. Arrays (the orchestrator's instruction, after a count): the
   mutable arrays of values, as cells of the heap's prototype, with
   `Array_length`, `Array_load`, `Make_array` and `Array_set`: done
   in the model, with examples in the proof files; see "Arrays". The
   conversion refuses them until the machine that the tool runs has a
   heap. Bytes wait for calls of C functions.
11. Static immutable arrays of values, which Simplify makes of a
   match whose arms are constants: done in the model and in the
   conversion, after a request and a measure; the rewrite that would
   validate such a unit is not written. See "Static immutable
   arrays".
12. An immutable array as a value, which is what `[: 1; 2; 3 :]`
   is before Simplify: a first cut is done, in the model and in the
   conversion, after a request. See "An immutable array made when
   the program runs". The lifting of such an array to a symbol,
   which most units need to validate, is done since, by the owner
   of the lifting after a second request. See "The lifting of an
   immutable array to a symbol".
13. The length of an immutable array that the term makes or binds
   to a symbol, and a load from one at a constant index, which
   Simplify computes from the type that it gives the array: a pass
   of the validator, done, and applied in the rounds of the term
   before. See "The length and the elements of a known array".

The branch: the commits of the `.v` files, one or two for each
primitive, and this file; then, last, one commit of the test tool,
which needs the regenerated extracted files, and the commits of the
examples, one for each primitive. The tool is one commit because the
regenerated files have every constructor at once, and its printer
must have a case for each to compile.

## `Phys_equal`

- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: `equality_comparison` (`Phys_eq`,
  `Phys_neq`, for the compiler's `Eq` and `Neq`, which are Rocq's
  names too) and the binary primitive `Phys_equal (op :
  equality_comparison)`. The names are those of C4's prototype of the
  heap.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: `eval_phys_equal`. A value on two tagged
  immediates in range (whether they are the same integer) and on a
  tagged immediate against an allocated value (not the same). No value
  on two allocated values, on null, on poison, on a symbol bound to
  nothing, on a naked number. The result is `naked_bool`, negated for
  `Phys_neq`. Kinds: `(Value, Value)`, result a naked immediate, as
  `args_kind_of_binary_primitive` and `result_kind_of_binary_primitive'`
  of the compiler.
- `eval_binary_primitive` now takes the static environment, which
  `eval_phys_equal` needs for `is_allocated`; `eval_prim` is unchanged
  in its signature. The lemma `eval_prim_binary` of the proofs file
  changes with it; nothing outside the primitives files used it.
- One CR in the `Spec`, at `eval_phys_equal`, for the cases with no
  value. Decision D19.
- Folding: `prim_may_fold` (V1's file) folds it, by its wildcard, and
  that is right: Simplify folds `Phys_equal` of two constants
  (`Provers.prove_physical_equality`).
- The laws, each checked by name; a proof that goes by cases on the
  binary primitives has the new case:
  - `eval_prim_static_values` (and its corollaries
    `eval_prim_same_static_values` and `eval_prim_consts`, which are
    proved from it and needed no change): new lemma
    `eval_binary_primitive_static_values`; `Phys_equal` reads the
    static environment through `is_allocated` only.
  - `eval_prim_statics_extend`: new lemma
    `eval_binary_primitive_statics_extend`.
  - `eval_prim_symbols_bound` (D8): `eval_binary_primitive_symbol_bound`
    replaces `eval_binary_primitive_symbol`, with
    `eval_phys_equal_symbol_bound`: a symbol on which `Phys_equal` has
    a value is allocated, so bound.
  - `eval_prim_kinds`: `eval_phys_equal_kinds`, and the case in
    `eval_binary_primitive_kinds`.
  - `eval_prim_preserves`: the case in
    `eval_binary_primitive_preserves` (the result is a number).
  - `eval_prim_arity` and the equation `eval_prim_binary`: restated
    with the static environment.
  - Seventeen `Example`s in the module `Examples` of the proofs file:
    the defined cases and each undefined one.
- The conversion in `flsemantics.ml` (`convert_binary_primitive`,
  `print_prim`), and five examples in `tests/semantics/`:
  `phys_equal.fl` (on values Simplify does not know and on constants),
  `phys_equal_call.fl` (the shape that integer `=` of the source
  takes, in code that Simplify inlines; validated),
  `phys_equal_immediate_block.fl` (an immediate against a block),
  `phys_equal_blocks.fl` and `phys_equal_null.fl` (undefined in the
  model: a block against itself, which Simplify folds to 1, and null
  against null, which it folds to 1 too).

Agreed with C4 (the heap), 2026-11-11: "a tagged immediate against an
allocated value is unequal" holds under every option of its design
but the one where `Phys_equal` has no value on anything allocated, and
is one of the cases `prove_physical_equality` proves false, so it is
never contradicted. "Allocated" is `is_allocated statics v`. C4's
`eval_phys_equal` also treats null as an immediate (null against null
equal, null against anything else unequal); here null has no value,
which C4 says is not contradicted later either. The case of two
allocated values is C4's. C4's epoch then adds only the cases with a
location.

## `Num_conv`

- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: the unary primitive `Num_conv (src dst :
  standard_int)`. The compiler's is between two
  `Standard_int_or_float.t`; the floats are not here and the
  conversion refuses them.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: an integer of the kind `src`, in range,
  becomes the integer of the kind `dst` that is the same modulo two to
  the width of `dst` (`wrap (int_width dst)`). Kinds:
  `kind_of_standard_int src` and `kind_of_standard_int dst`. No CR:
  nothing is undefined here but a wrong kind and a constant out of
  range (D13, D16).
- What it was read against: `Simplify_unary_primitive`
  (`Make_simplify_int_conv`: the identity where `src` is `dst`, and
  otherwise the `to_immediate`, `to_naked_int8`, ... of the source
  kind's module of `Number_adjuncts`); `Numeric_types.Short_int.of_int`
  (keeps the low bits, extends the sign); `One_bit_fewer.of_int64` and
  friends (`sign_extend`); the backend's
  `Cmm_helpers.Scalar_type.Integral.static_cast` (nothing where the
  destination holds every number of the source, a sign extension of
  the low bits where it does not).
- Folding: `prim_may_fold` folds it; Simplify folds a conversion of a
  constant.
- The laws: the case of `Num_conv` in every proof by cases on the
  unary primitives (`eval_unary_primitive_static_values`,
  `eval_unary_primitive_statics_extend`,
  `eval_unary_primitive_symbol_bound`, `eval_unary_primitive_kinds`,
  `eval_unary_primitive_preserves`). It does not read the static
  environment, and a symbol is not an integer. New lemmas:
  `wrap_of_in_range`, `in_range_wider`, `eval_num_conv` (the result is
  in the range of `dst`: what C1's coming law on ranges needs of it),
  `eval_num_conv_wider`. Ten `Example`s.
- A differential run against Simplify: `requests/P1-num-conv.py`
  generates, for a source kind, a term that converts numbers at and
  around the bounds of every kind to each of the seven kinds and
  compares each result with the number wrapped, computed in Python.
  Run by the test tool, the model must agree with the reference before
  Simplify and Simplify after it, which folds everything. The full
  sets (seven files, 1,470 conversions) agree on both sides; three
  small ones are examples (`num_conv_tagged.fl`, `num_conv_int8.fl`,
  `num_conv_int64.fl`).
- Other examples: `num_conv_int64_of_int.fl` (`Int64.of_int`, an
  addition and `Int64.to_int` as the front end makes them),
  `num_conv_for.fl` (a `for` loop as the front end makes it: a counter
  that is a naked `int64`), `num_conv_wrong_kind.fl` (undefined: a
  tagged immediate given to a conversion from naked immediates, which
  Simplify makes `Invalid`).

## `Boolean_not`

- The unary primitive `Boolean_not`: 1 for the tagged immediate 0 and
  0 for 1; no value on another tagged immediate or on anything else.
  Kinds: `Value` to `Value`.
- One CR in the `Spec`, before `eval_unary_primitive`: Simplify makes
  it `Invalid` where its argument cannot be 0 or 1, the machine flips
  the lowest bit of the integer, and nothing checks that the front end
  gives it a boolean. Decision D38.
- The laws: its case in the five proofs by cases on the unary
  primitives, as for `Num_conv`. Five `Example`s.
- Examples: `boolean_not.fl`, `boolean_not_of_two.fl` (undefined).

## `Int_shift`

- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: `int_shift_op` (`Lsl`, `Lsr`, `Asr`) and
  the binary primitive `Int_shift (kind : standard_int) (op :
  int_shift_op)`, at every integer kind.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: the first argument is an integer of the
  kind, in range; the second, the amount, a naked immediate. A value
  for an amount from 0 up to, and not including, `shift_bound kind`:
  the width of the kind, and 64 for the tagged immediates.
  `eval_int_shift`: `Lsl` shifts left and wraps; `Lsr` shifts the
  unsigned reading right; `Asr` shifts the integer, keeping its sign.
  Kinds: `(kind_of_standard_int kind, naked immediate)` to
  `kind_of_standard_int kind`.
- The bound is that of Simplify (`integer_bit_width` in each module of
  `Number_adjuncts`, used by `with_shift`): at these amounts Simplify
  folds the shift to what the machine computes. Outside them it folds
  to zero while the machine shifts by the amount modulo 64 on x86-64,
  and it says the shift is undefined there. For the naked immediates
  the bound is 63 and for the tagged ones, the same integers, 64: the
  difference is C1-9, which was tried from OCaml source and found to
  be at an amount the library documents as unspecified.
- One CR in the `Spec`, at `eval_int_shift`. Decision D18.
- Folding: `prim_may_fold` folds it; Simplify folds a shift of
  constants.
- The laws: its case in each proof by cases on the binary primitives
  (`eval_binary_primitive_static_values`,
  `eval_binary_primitive_statics_extend`,
  `eval_binary_primitive_symbol_bound`, `eval_binary_primitive_kinds`,
  `eval_binary_primitive_preserves`). It does not read the static
  environment, and a symbol is neither an integer nor an amount. New
  lemmas: `eval_int_shift_in_range` (what C1's coming law on ranges
  needs of it), and that the wraps of the right shifts change nothing
  but an `Lsr` by 0 (`shiftr_in_range`, `eval_int_shift_asr`,
  `unsigned_bounds`, `wrap_unsigned`, `eval_int_shift_lsr`). Thirteen
  `Example`s.
- A differential run against Simplify: `requests/P1-int-shift.py`
  generates, for a kind, a term that shifts numbers that include the
  bounds of the kind by amounts below the bound, each of the three
  ways, and compares each result with a reference computed in Python.
  The full sets (seven files, 3,150 shifts) agree with the model
  before Simplify and with what Simplify folds them to; three small
  ones are examples (`int_shift_tagged.fl`, `int_shift_int8.fl`,
  `int_shift_int64.fl`).
- Other examples: `int_shift.fl` (`lsl`, `lsr` and `asr` of a value
  Simplify does not know, as the front end makes them);
  `int_shift_by_64.fl` and `int_shift_imm_by_63.fl`, undefined in the
  model, which Simplify folds to 0 (the second is the term of C1-9).

## `String_length`

- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: the unary primitive `String_length`. The
  compiler's carries `String` or `Bytes`; the model has no bytes, which
  are mutable, and the conversion refuses `Bytes`.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: the number of bytes of an immutable string
  (`string_of`, which is a symbol bound to one), as a naked immediate.
  No value on anything else: Simplify makes it `Invalid` where its
  argument cannot be a string (`simplify_string_length`). Kinds:
  `Value` to a naked immediate.
- The laws: its case in the five proofs by cases on the unary
  primitives. It reads the static environment through the observation
  `string_of` only (`string_of_static_values`,
  `string_of_statics_extend`); a symbol on which it has a value is
  bound to a string. New lemma `string_of_kind`. Five `Example`s.
- Examples: `string_length.fl` (of a string Simplify does not know and
  of one it does, which it folds), `string_length_of_block.fl`
  (undefined; Simplify gives `Invalid`).

## The unary `Int_arith`

- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: `unary_int_arith_op`, with `Not` and
  `Swap_byte_endianness`, and the unary primitive `Unary_int_arith
  (kind : standard_int) (op : unary_int_arith_op)`. The compiler's is
  called `Int_arith`, as its binary one is; the name here differs
  because the two are in one module.
- Why: the front end makes `lnot x` an exclusive or with -1, and
  Simplify rewrites that into `Not` (`Simplify_binary_primitive`,
  `Bitwise_not_of_the_other_side`). Without `Not` the term after
  Simplify of any unit that uses `lnot` is refused.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: `eval_unary_int_arith`. `Not` of an
  integer of the kind, in range, is `Z.lnot` of it (its opposite less
  one), wrapped to the width, which changes nothing. Kinds:
  `kind_of_standard_int kind` to the same. No CR: nothing is undefined
  but a wrong kind and a constant out of range.
- What it was read against: `Simplify_unary_primitive`
  (`Make_simplify_int_arith`, `I.Num.not_`), and the backend's
  `To_cmm_primitive.unary_int_arith_primitive` (the complement of the
  machine word; for a tagged immediate, an exclusive or of the tagged
  form with -2, which keeps the tag).
- The laws: its case in the five proofs by cases on the unary
  primitives; it does not read the static environment. New lemmas:
  `eval_unary_int_arith_in_range` (what C1's law on ranges needs of
  it), `eval_unary_int_arith_not` (the wrap changes nothing),
  `eval_int_arith_xor_minus_one_r` and `eval_int_arith_xor_minus_one_l`
  (Simplify's rewrite is right: the exclusive or with -1 is `Not`).
  Four `Example`s.
- Examples: `int_not.fl` (at four kinds, of a value Simplify does not
  know and of constants), `int_xor_minus_one.fl` (the term the front
  end makes for `lnot`, which Simplify rewrites into `Not`).
- `Swap_byte_endianness` is what `%bswap16`, `%bswap_int32`,
  `%bswap_int64` and `%bswap_native` of the source become, and so
  what `String.get_uint16_be` and the like use on a little-endian
  target. It is not one operation: `eval_unary_int_arith` now takes
  the kind and not its width.
  - At a tagged or a naked immediate: the two low bytes swapped and
    nothing of the others, so a result from 0 to 65535 whatever the
    argument (`swap_bytes 2`). This is `%bswap16`, which is on an
    `int`.
  - At a naked `int8`: the integer itself.
  - At a naked `int16`, `int32`, `int64` or `nativeint`: the integer
    of the kind whose bytes are those of the argument in the opposite
    order (`wrap` of `swap_bytes` of 2, 4 or 8 bytes), so that the
    last byte of the argument gives the sign.
  Read against `To_cmm_primitive.unary_int_arith_primitive` and
  against what Simplify computes of a constant
  (`Number_adjuncts`: `swap_byte_endianness` of each kind;
  `Target_ocaml_int.get_least_significant_16_bits_then_byte_swap` for
  the immediates; `Numeric_types.Int16`, whose `of_int` wraps;
  `%bswap_int32` and `%bswap_int64` of the host for the others). The
  backend's comments call the primitive "weird" and ask whether the
  small kinds should be there at all; the model follows what the code
  does at each.
- Its lemmas: `swap_bytes_bound` (the swap of `n` bytes is from 0 up
  to `256 ^ n`), `eval_unary_int_arith_in_range` (now at a kind, for
  both operations), `eval_unary_int_arith_swap_int8`. A swap done
  twice gives the integer back at every kind but the immediates
  (`eval_unary_int_arith_swap_twice`), where it gives its two low
  bytes (`eval_unary_int_arith_swap_twice_immediate`). Fifteen
  `Example`s, whose values are those the compiled OCaml gives.
- Differential test against Simplify, which folds a swap of a
  constant: `requests/P1-int-bswap.py` generates, for a kind, a term
  that swaps each of up to 46 integers (the ends of the range, the
  powers of 256 and their neighbours) and compares with a reference on
  Python's integers. At the seven kinds every comparison is 1 before
  Simplify (the model is the reference) and after it (Simplify is the
  reference), and each unit is validated.
- Examples: `int_bswap.fl` (at the seven kinds, of a value Simplify
  does not know and of constants).

## `Reinterpret_64_bit_word` between a tagged immediate and an int64

- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: `reinterpret_64_bit_word`, with
  `Tagged_int63_as_unboxed_int64` and `Unboxed_int64_as_tagged_int63`,
  and the unary primitive `Reinterpret_64_bit_word`. The compiler's
  two others are between an int64 and a float, and the conversion
  refuses them (see "Decided overnight").
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: `eval_reinterpret_64_bit_word`. The word
  of the tagged immediate `i` is `2 * i + 1`: the first gives that as
  an int64; the second gives the tagged immediate whose word is the
  int64 with its lowest bit set, that is the int64 halved towards
  minus infinity. Kinds: `Value` to a naked int64, and back.
- What it was read against: `To_cmm_primitive.unary_primitive` (the
  word as it is; a logical or with 1), and
  `Simplify_unary_primitive` (`2 * i + 1`; a logical shift right by
  one bit read as an integer of 63 bits). The first has no value on a
  value that is not a tagged immediate, where the machine gives the
  address: Simplify makes that `Invalid`.
- The laws: its case in each proof by cases on the unary primitives.
  New lemmas `tagged_word_in_range`, `half_word_in_range`. Ten
  `Example`s.
- Differential test against Simplify, which folds both on a constant:
  `requests/P1-reinterpret.py` generates a term for a direction (25
  tagged immediates, 32 int64s: the ends of the ranges, the powers of
  two and their neighbours, odd and even). Every comparison with the
  reference on Python's integers is 1 before Simplify and after it.
- Examples: `reinterpret_int63_int64.fl`.
- These are `%reinterpret_tagged_int63_as_unboxed_int64` and
  `%reinterpret_unboxed_int64_as_tagged_int63` of the source, which
  the standard library does not use.

## `String_or_bigstring_load` of an integer from a string

- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: `string_accessor_width`, with `Eight`,
  `Eight_signed`, `Sixteen`, `Sixteen_signed`, `Thirty_two` and
  `Sixty_four`, and the binary primitive `String_or_bigstring_load
  (width : string_accessor_width)`. The compiler's also carries a
  `string_like_value`; here it is always `String`, and the conversion
  refuses `Bytes` and `Bigstring` (both mutable), the load of a float
  (`Single`), those of vectors and `Mask`.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: `eval_string_load`. The first argument is
  an immutable string (`string_of`), the second, the index, a naked
  nativeint. A value where the string has a byte at the index, the
  first at 0: with `Eight` the byte as a naked immediate, from 0 to
  255; with `Eight_signed` the naked `int8` of the same eight bits.
  No value on an index that the string has not got: the machine would
  read whatever is there. Kinds: `(Value, naked nativeint)` to a naked
  immediate, or to a naked `int8` for `Eight_signed`, as
  `args_kind_of_binary_primitive` and `result_kind_of_binary_primitive'`
  of the compiler.
- Simplify does not compute it
  (`Simplify_binary_primitive.simplify_string_or_bigstring_load` gives
  the result an unknown type), so there is nothing of Simplify's to
  compare with; the backend is `To_cmm_primitive.string_like_load`.
- One CR in the `Spec`, at `eval_string_load`: the erased conversions
  (X5-3). The compiler erases `Bytes.unsafe_to_string` and
  `Bytes.unsafe_of_string` before Flambda 2
  (`from_lambda/lambda_to_lambda_transforms.ml`: `Pbytes_to_string`
  and `Pbytes_of_string` applied to an argument become the argument,
  and so do `Parray_to_iarray` and `Parray_of_iarray`). So a string of
  the compiler's can be the block of bytes that are written later, and
  the backend moves a load from a string, which it takes to have no
  effect and no coeffect, past such a write. In the model a string is
  an immutable value and there are no bytes: such a string is outside
  it. See "A limit on what validated means" below.
- Folding: `prim_may_fold` folds it by its wildcard. That is allowed,
  as a fold is a step the model takes too, and it is not something
  Simplify does today.
- The laws: its case in each proof by cases on the binary primitives.
  It reads the static environment through the observation `string_of`
  only; a symbol on which it has a value is bound to a string, and an
  index is not a symbol. Seven `Example`s.
- Examples: `string_load.fl` (the byte of a string Simplify does not
  know, as the front end makes `String.unsafe_get`, and of one it
  does; the signed access), `string_load_past_the_end.fl` (undefined).
- The safe access of the source (`s.[i]`) is this load after a test of
  the index against the length, with a raise where the test fails.
  With C5's exceptions the tool accepts and runs such a unit, before
  and after Simplify, with the same result. It is not validated yet:
  see "What stops the conversion today".
- The loads of two, four and eight bytes (the second commit of this
  primitive). They are what `%caml_string_get16`, `get32` and `get64`
  become, that is `String.get_uint16_ne`, `get_int32_ne`,
  `get_int64_ne` and, with a swap or without, the `_le` and `_be`
  ones. `eval_string_load` reads as many bytes as the width has
  (`byte_width_of_string_accessor_width`) from the index on, the
  least significant first (`little_endian`), and gives a naked
  immediate without a sign for `Sixteen` (0 to 65535), a naked
  `int16` for `Sixteen_signed`, a naked `int32` for `Thirty_two` and

## `Block_load` with its mutability

- Why: a read of a field that the source declares as one that may be
  written (`Reads_vary`: `%field0`, `%field1`, the `!` of a reference)
  reaches Flambda 2 as a `Block_load` marked `Mutable`, with an access
  kind that gives neither tag nor size
  (`Lambda_to_flambda_primitives`, `Pfield`). The model's load had no
  mutability and the conversion refused every load not marked
  `Immutable`, so a unit that declares `%field0` and applies it to a
  tuple was not checked. `fst` and `snd` of the standard library are
  not such loads: they are `%field0_immut` and `%field1_immut`
  (`Reads_agree`), marked `Immutable`, and were accepted already (V2
  corrected the first account of this, which had them marked
  `Mutable`). So what this adds for programs today is small; it is
  the shape that C4's heap extends, where `!` is such a load.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v`: `mutability` (`Mutable`, `Immutable`,
  `Immutable_unique`: the compiler's `Mutability.t`) and `Block_load
  (kind) (mut : mutability) (field)`. `Make_block` is unchanged: it
  makes immutable blocks, and the conversion refuses the others, so
  every block of the model is immutable.
- `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v`: the evaluation does not look at the mark.
  The load is what it was, under the checks of D15, whatever it is
  marked. The mark says what the compiler may take of the load, not
  what the block is: Simplify keeps a load marked `Mutable` as it is
  (`simplify_mutable_block_load`), and makes it `Invalid` on a
  constant, where the model has no value either. Nothing becomes
  undefined, so there is no CR.
- A load from a block that can be written (`!` on a `ref`, a field of
  a record with a mutable field) needs the heap: its `Make_block` is
  refused. That is C4's.
- `FlambdaBlockLoadSpec.v` (V2's, changed here with V2's agreement so
  that the branch builds): the pass for loads from known blocks folds
  a load only where it is not marked `Mutable`, as Simplify does; its
  proof has the case.
- The laws: no law changes; the proofs by cases on the unary
  primitives name the new argument. Two more `Example`s.
- Examples: `block_load_mut.fl` (the loads that `%field0` and
  `%field1` make of a pair), `block_load_mut_of_constant.fl`
  (undefined: a load marked
  `Mutable` from a constant, which Simplify makes `Invalid`).
- Agreed with C4, 2026-11-11: this is decision 11 of its request and
  the final shape of its section 8.1. All three marks are accepted on
  an immutable block. Its epoch adds the cases on a location, and
  there the effects of a load go by its mark, not by the block. Its
  prototype drops its own `mutability` and `Block_load_mutable` when
  this is on the integration branch.

## The identities of arithmetic (a pass of the validator)

- Why: Simplify applies an identity of an operation where it knows one
  argument only (`x + 0` is `x`), and the validator computed a
  primitive on constants only, so a unit where that happens was "the
  same, not validated". In V2's count over generated units it was the
  cause, alone or with another, for 9 of the 59 that were not
  validated.
- `FlambdaIdentitiesSpec.v` (new, P1's): `apply_identities`, a
  function on terms for the term before Simplify, and
  `identity_named` for one defining expression. It has Simplify's
  table, with its names (`symmetric_op_one_side_unknown`,
  `op_lhs_unknown`, `op_rhs_unknown`, and the same of the shifts),
  read from `Simplify_binary_primitive` (`Int_ops_for_binary_arith`,
  `Int_ops_for_binary_shift`): the result is the other argument, a
  constant, `0 - x`, or the complement of `x`. It fires where one
  argument is a constant of the kind of the operation and the other is
  not a constant; an operation on two constants is left to the
  folding of constants. It also has `Phys_equal` of a variable and
  itself, which Simplify computes (`Provers.prove_physical_equality`).
- Not in it: the divisions and remainders by 0, which Simplify makes
  `Invalid`; `Phys_equal` of a symbol and itself or of two symbols,
  which Simplify computes and on which the model has no value (D19);
  and what Simplify has no identity for (`x - x`, `x lxor x`,
  `x lor x`).
- `FlambdaIdentitiesProofs.v`: `apply_identities_refines`, which is
  `∀ e, expr_refines e (apply_identities e)`. Each identity is proved
  of every integer in the range of the kind, wrapping included
  (`symmetric_op_sound_r`, `op_lhs_unknown_sound`,
  `op_rhs_unknown_sound`, `shift_lhs_unknown_sound`,
  `shift_rhs_unknown_sound`); that the other argument is in range
  comes from the operation having a value. It is a refinement and not
  an equivalence: the operation has no value on what is not an integer
  of the kind, and what replaces it may have one. Twenty-eight
  `Example`s of what the pass makes of an operation.
- The pass commutes with renaming
  (`apply_identities_equivariant_statement`,
  `identity_named_equivariant_statement`, in the form N1 gave: an
  equation with no premise, for `rename_expr` and `rename_named`). It
  looks at a name in one place, to see that the two arguments of
  `Phys_equal` are the same variable (`rename_variable_eqb`). The
  header of the `Spec` says that nothing runs the pass yet.
- Wiring: V2's, in the validator's files, as a separate commit (where
  the pass goes among the normalisations of the term before, and the
  line of `FlambdaExtract.v`). Agreed with V2, 2026-11-11. Until then
  nothing runs the pass, and no example here depends on it.

## A limit on what "validated" means for a unit made from OCaml

The test tool is given the term that Flambda 2 has. The conversions
`Pbytes_to_string`, `Pbytes_of_string`, `Parray_to_iarray` and
`Parray_of_iarray` are erased before that, so the tool cannot see
that a string was made from bytes, or an immutable array from a
mutable one. It refuses a unit in which a primitive on bytes arrives
(a load from bytes, a write to them, `String_length` of bytes), so a
unit that itself makes or writes bytes is not validated. A unit that
only reads strings is validated on the reading that a string is
immutable. The verdict says nothing of a string that is the block of
bytes that are written while the string is read, which
`Bytes.unsafe_to_string` allows a caller to make (X5-3). The same
holds of immutable arrays, now that the model has loads from them:
the tool refuses a unit in which a mutable array is made or
written, and a unit that only reads immutable arrays is validated
on the reading that nothing writes them.

## Floats

Not done, and not leaf work as the model stands: a request,
`requests/P1-floats.md`, for the human. The constants of the floats
are bit patterns (integers), with no operation on them. Needed for
`Float_arith`, `Float_comp` and the conversions of `Num_conv` to and
from floats: IEEE 754 at 64 and 32 bits on bit patterns, which the
`SpecFloat` of Rocq's library has at any precision with no axiom; a
decision on which NaN an operation returns (Simplify folds with the
host's, and the target's may differ); and a decision on a conversion
from a float that is NaN or out of the range of the integer kind,
which OCaml leaves unspecified and the targets compute differently.
R2 read the request as a design and its points are in it; nothing of
floats is in the model, and no addition to the trust ledger is asked.
Reading Simplify for it gave three suspected differences from the
machine on NaN (P1-6 to P1-8, below); two are demonstrated. Whether
they are mistakes depends on what a NaN is: the request gives the two
options (the bit pattern of a NaN is kept; floats up to the payload
and sign of NaNs), what `eval_prim` would return and which identities
of Simplify survive under each, and a CR for the human. It does not
choose, and no float arithmetic is in the model.

## Decided overnight

- `String_length` of a string whose length is not a naked immediate (a
  string of more than two to the 62 bytes) has no value. The options:
  give the length whatever it is, or no value. Chosen: no value, so
  that the result is a constant in the range of its kind by its form,
  which C1's law on ranges needs of every primitive, without a bound
  on the lengths of strings in well-formedness. It is about terms that
  only the model has: the compiler's strings are far shorter. To
  change it: drop the test in `eval_unary_primitive` and ask a bound
  of static strings in the check of ranges.

- A byte of a static string is an integer of the model with no bound
  (`Static_string (bytes : list Z)`); the conversion gives bytes from
  0 to 255. The load takes the low eight bits of what the string holds
  (`byte mod 256`, or wrapped to eight bits for the signed access), so
  that its result is in range by its form. The options: that, or no
  value on a byte outside 0 to 255, or a bound on bytes in
  well-formedness. It is about terms that only the model has: C1's
  check of ranges asks that the bytes of a static string are from 0
  to 255 (`byte_in_range`), and in a unit that passes it the choice is
  not seen. To change it: test the byte in `eval_string_load`.
- The pass for the identities of arithmetic fires on a constant of
  the term only, where Simplify goes by the type of the argument; it
  leaves out the divisions by 0 that Simplify makes `Invalid`, and
  `Phys_equal` on symbols. The options were to do more (a pass that
  makes a term `Invalid`; identities on symbols that hold only because
  the model has no value there). Chosen: only what is true by
  computation in the model. To change it: add outcomes to
  `identity_of_binary`.
- The unary `Int_arith` is named `Unary_int_arith` in the model.
- `Swap_byte_endianness` at a naked `int8` is the integer itself, as
  in the backend and in Simplify, and at the immediates it is the
  swap of the two low bytes only, whatever the others. The option was
  to leave out the kinds that the backend's comment doubts (the small
  ones); chosen: every kind, as the code has a meaning at each and
  Simplify and the backend agree on it (the differential test). To
  change it: refuse those kinds in the conversion.
- The table of C functions gives the polymorphic comparison a value
  on two tagged immediates and on two strings only. The options: that;
  or also immutable blocks by structure and boxed integers (what
  five of the eighteen units of the survey need); or nothing beyond
  what Simplify specialises. Chosen: immediates and strings, which
  are what the instruction asked for and what answers at once in the
  C; the rest is a CR, as it needs a raise on closures and a decision
  on what a call outside the table is. To change it: extend
  `compare_values`.
- `Reinterpret_64_bit_word` has its two forms between integers only.
  The two between an int64 and a float (`Int64.bits_of_float`,
  `Int64.float_of_bits`) would be the identity on the bit pattern,

## Discrepancies recorded

- P1-1: Simplify folds `Phys_equal` of a poison constant against
  another constant to 0, and the backend makes a poison of kind `Value`
  a tagged immediate that may be that constant.
- P1-2: nothing checks that `Boolean_not` is given 0 or 1.
- P1-3: shifts by an amount outside the bound (the general case of
  C1-9).
- P1-4: the fexpr parser reads `%bytes_length` as `String_length
  String`.
- P1-5: an unsafe load of several bytes at the end of a string reads
  the byte after the last, which is 0 on the machine and which the
  model has not got (the model is stricter; CR at `eval_string_load`).
- P1-6: Simplify makes `x *. 1.` and `x /. 1.` into `x`, which for a
  signalling NaN is not the machine's result. Demonstrated on the
  compiler built from the tree.
- P1-7: Simplify makes `x *. -1.` and `x /. -1.` into the negation of
  `x`, which flips the sign of a NaN where the machine keeps it.
  Demonstrated.
- P1-8: which NaN a folded operation returns is the host's. Tried on
  x86-64 for x86-64 and not reproduced; open for cross compilation.

- P1-9: the fexpr printer writes an `Array_load` and an `Array_set`
  with their array kind only, so the load kind and the set kind of a
  term that Simplify has specialised (`Immediates` with a load kind
  `Values`) are lost when it is printed and read back. Demonstrated
  with the round trip of the compiler's test tools.
- P1-10: Simplify stops with a fatal error on an `Array_set` whose
  set kind is not `Values` and whose array kind is still `Values`
  after it has tried to specialise it (where the type of the array
  says immediates, the same store goes through), and on a
  `Make_array` that is `Immutable_unique`. Nothing refuses either
  where the term is built. The model's types can write the first;
  the conversion is to refuse it. Demonstrated on two fexpr units.
- P1-11: Simplify looks at the array kind of a load and of a store
  (it gives `Invalid` when the type of the array has another kind of
  element) and not at that of `Array_length`, whose compiled code
  does depend on it.

Not of this stream, and about what it added: T5-11. Two immutable
array literals with the same elements are two symbols in the term
that the compiler's Simplify makes of the term in memory, and one
symbol in the term that it makes of the term read back from the
text, so that the comparison of the two by `==` is folded to false
in the one and to true in the other; two copies of one literal made
by inlining are one symbol. In the model the comparison has no
value, so the term before of such a unit is undefined and no
verdict is given of it.
