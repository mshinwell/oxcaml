# N2: the freshness quantifier

Branch `flambda2-rocq/N2-fresh-quantifier`, on the integration branch
after C2's epoch and N1's step 3b (in which the renaming enters code)
with the statements on the classes that follow it. New files only.
A second piece of work, not about names, is on
`flambda2-rocq/N2-context-where`: rewriting in a handler or in the
body of code from the states that have a predicate (the last entry
under "Done").
The human's question: can the quantifier of the papers on nominal
sets, the reversed N that reads "for all but finitely many names", be
used here, and does it make statements easier? The answer, the survey
and the recommendation are in `requests/N2-fresh-quantifier.md`.

- `FlambdaFreshBindersSpec.v`, `Proofs` (11 statements): a fresh name
  is not free; opening a `Let`, a `Let_cont` and the parameters of a
  handler at fresh names; two binders compared at one fresh name, as
  rules that `alpha_equivalent` obeys, with one name and with the
  quantifier; pairing two names in the comparison is exchanging each
  for a fresh name.
- `FlambdaCompareConverseSpec.v`, `Proofs` (3 statements), at N1's
  request: for terms whose code is closed and whose binders repeat no
  name, the comparison accepts two terms only if they are
  alpha-equivalent (`expr_eqb_alpha_equivalent`), so on such terms the
  comparison and alpha-equivalence are one relation
  (`expr_eqb_iff_alpha_equivalent`); and two `Let`s are
  alpha-equivalent exactly when their defining expressions are the same
  and their bodies are alpha-equivalent at a fresh name
  (`alpha_let_iff`).
- `FlambdaAlphaFreshSpec.v`, `Proofs` (6 statements), at the
  orchestrator's request: the same equivalence for the other binders
  that are not recursive. Two non-recursive `Let_cont`s are
  alpha-equivalent exactly when their handlers agree at fresh
  parameters (`alpha_handlers`) and their bodies at a fresh
  continuation (`alpha_let_cont_iff`; `alpha_let_cont_body_iff` and
  `alpha_handler_iff` for each binder alone); two pieces of code bound
  in the same place, exactly when their bodies agree at fresh variables
  for `my_closure` and the parameters and two fresh continuations
  (`alpha_code_iff`); two `Let_static`s, exactly when their bindings
  agree one by one and their bodies are alpha-equivalent
  (`alpha_let_static_iff`). From right to left the proofs use closed
  code and that the lists compared repeat no name; from left to right,
  both conditions of well-formedness on both terms. The premise on the
  lists cannot be left out: a handler that repeats a parameter agrees
  at fresh names with one that does not, and the two `Let_cont`s are
  not alpha-equivalent (`alpha_handlers_repeated_parameter`).
- `FlambdaAlphaGroupSpec.v`, `Proofs` (6 statements), at N1's request,
  who moved it here from N3: the continuations of a recursive
  `Let_cont`. Two recursive `Let_cont`s are alpha-equivalent exactly
  when they have as many handlers and, with the continuations of both
  groups exchanged for the same fresh ones in the handlers and the
  body, the handlers agree in order (`alpha_handlers`) and the bodies
  are alpha-equivalent (`alpha_let_cont_recursive_iff`, on terms with
  closed code and distinct binders). And the group is a name
  abstraction of N1's interface (`group_abstraction`): bound names the
  continuations of the group, scope the handlers and the body, a
  handler being an abstraction of its parameters; equal abstractions
  give alpha-equivalent terms, with no premise of distinct names. The
  scope has the laws (`group_continuations_nominal`,
  `handler_continuations_nominal`), by a list instance for any things
  that contain names (`list_names`, `list_nominal`), which N1 lifts
  into the interface if another instance needs it. For groups whose
  binders repeat no name the two accounts are one relation: the
  abstractions are equal exactly when the terms are alpha-equivalent
  (`group_abstraction_iff`), at the orchestrator's request; this
  statement came after the review of the others and is a separate
  commit.
- `FlambdaInlineFreshSpec.v`, `Proofs` (7 statements), second in N1's
  order: inlining a call at fresh variables. `open_code fresh c` is
  the code with `my_closure` and its parameters exchanged for `fresh`.
  A call, with the binding of its code in sight, refines the opened

## Decided overnight

- The names: `for_fresh` for the definition, `И` for the notation,
  `supports` for "a finite set of names supports a predicate",
  `propositions` for `Prop` as a type that contains no names,
  `concretion` for the scope of an abstraction opened at a name. To
  change one is a renaming in files that nothing else imports.
- The explicit forms stay primary and the quantifier is a derived
  layer (the recommendation; N1 agrees for its interface; D39).
- Lists of bound names are treated when they repeat no name, which is
  row 5 of well-formedness (`FlambdaBindersSpec.binders_distinct`).
- The new statements about free names use the lists of
  `FlambdaBindersSpec.v` (`among z (free_variables_of_expr body) =
  false`), not the older relations, since fresh names are made from
  lists.
- The converse is stated with the two conditions of well-formedness as
  premises on both terms, as N1 gave it: it is for the terms that
  well-formedness admits. That neither can be left out is N1's two
  statements in `FlambdaCompareAlphaSpec.v`, which the header cites.
- `fresh_names` and `fresh_names_fresh_statement` are in
  `FlambdaFreshQuantifierSpec.v`, moved from its Proofs file: a
  function that makes names is in a statement of
  `FlambdaInlineFreshSpec.v`, and a Spec file may not import a Proofs
  file. It changes a Spec file that is merged and signed, by one
  definition and one statement; R1 is asked to confirm them. N1
  agreed, and does not want it in its own Spec.
- Two choices of fresh variables in inlining are related by
  alpha-equivalence, not by "the second is the first renamed": the
  variables made are bound in the result, and a fresh variable may
  occur bound in the body of the code. N1 agreed that this is the
  right reading of its fourth form.
- The names `FlambdaAlphaFresh*` and `FlambdaAlphaGroup*` match the
  pattern `FlambdaAlpha*` of N1's section of `OWNERS.md`, and
  `FlambdaCompareConverse*` the pattern `FlambdaCompare*` of V1's. They
  are N2's files, N1 agreeing; they are listed by name in N2's
  section, as `FlambdaCompareAlpha*` is in N1's. To rename them
  instead costs a renaming of files that nothing else imports.
- The equivalences at the binders (`FlambdaAlphaFreshSpec.v`) take the
  same two conditions, on the two terms compared, though the direction
  from right to left uses less (the entry under "Done" says what). One
  form of premise for every statement is easier to use on well-formed
  terms; to split each into its two directions with the least premises
  would add five statements and change none.

- For `FlambdaContextWhere*`, with C0 and T2 agreeing:
  - The hole is asked two things in every state in which it can come
    to run: that it refines from the states that have the predicate,
    and that the two expressions have the predicate together there.
    The second is what the proof needs: it goes through a state with
    an expression of one side under the environments of the other,
    which no run need reach, so that a step keeping the predicate
    says nothing of it. A predicate that does not look at the
    expression has it for free. To change it later is a change of
    two definitions and of two hypotheses of `transfer`.
  - The body of code is given its two continuations as its whole
    continuation environment, and the premise on kinds is on the
    values of the arguments, as a call tests them; nothing is asked
    of the kinds that a term gives.
  - Nothing is given about the kinds of the values of a handler's
    parameters, since the machine does not test them, and the
    relations on handlers keep their types; C2's series adds the
    kinds there.
  - The names on the code side that `FlambdaNormaliseProofs.v` uses
    (`binding_related`, `Codes_related`, `statics_related` and the
    like) stay, as the instance at a relation that ignores what the
    code binds; the general inductives are `codes_related_where`,
    `binding_related_where` and `statics_related_where`.
  - `FlambdaContextProofs.v` does not import the new Spec: its
    lemmas are general in the relations, and
    `FlambdaContextWhereProofs.v` takes them at the definitions of
    the Spec. The pair is N2's, listed by name in `OWNERS.md` though
    it matches the pattern of C0's section.

## Decided overnight, second part (2026-11-12)

- **Where the generic lemmas about scope live.** The question: N3
  asked for the lemmas about `scoped` in a file that needs nothing of
  V2's series. Options: keep them in `FlambdaKeepsScopeProofs.v` and
  let N3 copy what it needs; or split them out. Choice: split, into
  `FlambdaScopedProofs.v`, with no statement changed. Why: the proofs
  for V3's and V4's rewrites can then be written before V2's last
  rebase, so the statement about `validate_unit` need not become
  conditional again.
  Cost of changing: none to a statement; the file can be folded back.
- **A Proofs file with no Spec.** `FlambdaScopedProofs.v` has no
  statement of its own, so there is nothing of it for `make claims`
  and nothing to review but that it is used. Why not a Spec: every
  fact in it is a step of a proof of a statement stated elsewhere.
  Cost of changing: a Spec with `expr_eqb_scoped` as a statement could
  be added later; it would duplicate `expr_eqb_keeps_scope_statement`.
- **The form of the repair of `thread_arm`.** Options: ask that the
  arm of the handler has no trap action at all, or that it does not
  name the continuation being bound. Choice: the second
  (`among k (apply_cont_continuations ac')`), which is what capture
  needs and keeps the rewrite acting on arms that pop or push another
  handler. Cost of changing: one line and two proofs.
- **What the statements of `FlambdaKeepsScopeSpec.v` are stated for.**
  Each rewrite as the validation runs it: `follow_aliases []`,
  `load_known_blocks`, `unbox_known_numbers` and
  `share_known_primitives` with nothing known to begin with;
  `remove_unused_defined_lets vs` for every `vs` (and, in the pair to
  come, `inline_calls kept []` for every `kept`). The forms with
  something known are lemmas of the proofs, under a condition on what
  is known. Cost of changing: a statement more for each, if one is
  wanted.
- **Which statements go on the integration branch ahead of the change
  that renames binders apart.** The question: the integrator asked
  whether the statements could land before V2's change of the
  validation. Options: wait and land all 20 after it; land all 20 now,
  stated for the definitions as they are, and have that change edit
  this stream's files; land the 17 that the change does not touch, and
  state the other three on top of it. Choice: the third. Why: a
  statement about one rewrite does not change when the round does, and
  the proofs for the rewrites of lifting and of loops use these lemmas
  now; a statement about the round as a whole changes each time a
  rewrite is added to it, so it belongs in a file that sits on the
  round. Cost of changing: none to a statement; the three can be put
  in `FlambdaKeepsScopeSpec.v` later if one file is wanted.
- **The rounds stated now, by a proof that survives a change of the
  round.** The question: whether to state that a round keeps scope
  before V2's change of the validation, which adds rewrites to it.
  Options: wait; or state now what that change does not alter in text
  (`normalise_expr_before`, `normalise_code_before`) and prove it so
  that a rewrite added to the round does not break the proof. Choice:
  the second, with a tactic that takes the chain apart and a set of
  hints, one lemma to a rewrite. Why: the statement then guards the
  round on the integration branch from now on, and whoever adds a
  rewrite with a lemma has nothing of this stream's to edit. What it
  asks of others: a rewrite that is put in a round must have its lemma
  among the hints first. Cost of changing: the proofs can be written
  out as a chain of `scoped_trans` at any time.
- **A test in place of a proof that a rewrite keeps scope.** The
  question (T2's): its rewrites by types put for a `Simple` the
  canonical element that a typing environment answers; that the
  element is in scope needs the environment to be well formed, which
  the rewrite does not test everywhere and which is not proved of the
  environments it builds. Options: prove it (new tests inside merged
  definitions, or a proof about those environments; neither is short);
  or run the rewrite under a test of what `keeps_scope` says, which
  leaves the term as it was where the test fails. Choice: the test,
  `guard_scope`, written for any rewrite. Why: nothing is trusted, the
  statement about the validation and renaming stays without a premise
  on the rewrite, and where the rewrite does keep scope the test
  changes no result (`guard_scope_unchanged`). What it costs at each
  use: whether the code of each of the two terms is closed, the lists
  of their free variables and continuations, and a search of a list
  for each name of the result, quadratic at worst; and a result that
  does not keep scope is dropped without a word, so a unit may stop
  being validated where the rewrite was needed. Of a term whose code
  is not closed the test is not made (`CR mshinwell` at the
  definition).
  Cost of changing: prove `keeps_scope` of the rewrite and take the
  test away; by `guard_scope_unchanged` no verdict changes.
