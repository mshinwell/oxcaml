# C4, the heap: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/C4-heap`, rebased on the integration branch at
1160b780f0 (C3's epoch, C2's kinds, C1's law on symbols bound to
nothing, C0's well-formedness, N1's names and the types, T1's to
T6's, are in the prototype's base; what the rebase cost is in
`requests/C4-heap-fallout.md`). C4 is a design for the human; nothing
of it is to be applied before a reviewer has approved the request and
the human has decided. The request is `requests/C4-heap.md`, third
version.

## Review

five blocking findings (its review log). The third version answers
them; "What changed since the second version" in the request says

## Decided overnight

The human is away and has said: "If there are considerations to be
made, document them, take a sensible decision and proceed; and
tomorrow morning we can review them." The orchestrator has taken the
twelve decisions of the request as recommended there, as amended by
R2's reviews, for the human to review. Each: the question; the
options; the choice; why; what it costs to change later. The numbers
are those of "Decisions for the human" in the request.

1. **What has an identity.** Options: only blocks made `Mutable` or
   `Immutable_unique`; every allocation; the first plus closures.
   Choice: the first. Why: the compiler shares and duplicates
   immutable blocks, boxed numbers and closures (CSE, lifting,
   unboxing), so a model that told them apart would refute rewrites
   the compiler makes. To change: every block of every statement goes
   in the heap; a redesign.
2. **What a location is.** Options: a name with a fresh one chosen by
   a function; the next number. Choice: a name. Why: D29, and a
   bijection between runs is needed either way. To change: nothing in
   statements; `fresh_location` becomes transparent.
3. **`Phys_equal`.** Options: (a) no value on anything allocated; (b)
   defined but on two immutable allocated values; (c) (b) and false
   by shape; (d) by contents; (e) an oracle. Choice: (b), on top of
   what P1 lands for immediates. Why: it gives `==` its meaning where
   something has an identity and on immediates; (d) is not preserved
   by Simplify (R2-21). It is weaker coverage: nothing is proved of a
   program after it compares two immutable allocated values. To
   change to (c): leaf work in the primitives' files. To (e): `state`
   and `refines` change, an epoch.
4. **A load marked `Immutable` from a block made `Mutable`.** Options:
   (a) no value; (b) field stability (the load makes the field
   stable, a store into a stable field has no value); (c) mutability
   by field from the compiler. Choice: (a) for epochs 1 to 3, then
   (b). Why: (a) is the simplest sound start and (b) is exactly what
   Simplify assumes, with no change to the compiler. (a) leaves
   records with both sorts of field undefined, so it must not outlast
   the first epochs. To change from (a) to (b): the heap's files only
   (a cell remembers stable fields; `no_effects` and `no_coeffects`
   restated; the relation orders the marks). Outside the model by the
   human's rulings, and refused by the conversion: tail-modulo-cons,
   and `Duplicate_block` followed by stores. Recursive values: X5
   found nothing wrong; they are calls of C functions, which the
   model does not have. Recursive modules (X5-2): outside what any
   stage covers, since a field may be read before the run-time
   library overwrites it.
5. **`Block_set` into what was not made `Mutable`.** Options: no
   value; write anyway. Choice: no value. Why: the front end makes no
   such store, and Simplify keeps what it knows of the block. To
   change: one case of `exec_prim`.
6. **What an exit observes.** Options, cumulative: (i) values and
   heap as they are; (ii) up to a bijection on locations; (iii)
   immutable values by contents, through symbols (D6); (iv) poison of
   the source is anything of its kind (D26). Choice: (i) for epoch 2,
   (ii) and (iii) in epoch 3, (iv) after. Why: each step is sound by
   itself and the proofs come before the epoch that needs them. To
   change: `refines` is restated, an epoch, with the lemma that
   carries the old theorems over.
7. **Closures at exit.** Options: the whole value; less, by a
   relation on code ids. Choice: the whole value (D1 as it is). To
   change: a third component of the correspondence, S4's.
8. **How the heap enters the statements.** Options: a fifth field of
   the state; one record for the static environment and the heap.
   Choice: a fifth field. Why: less to change now, and nothing
   planned adds to the memory. To change: a second sweep of the same
   size.
9. **`expr_refines` over closed memories only.** Options: yes, in the
   definition; no. Choice: yes. Why: the lemma on related states
   needs it and every state reached from a term is closed. To change:
   the premise moves to each theorem.
10. **Scope.** In: mutable and unique blocks of values on the heap.
    Not modelled: local allocation and regions (the conversion drops
    regions and reads a local allocation as one on the heap, D37).
    Out: atomics, bigarrays, lazy
    blocks, off-heap memory; static blocks that are not `Immutable`
    until symbols are addresses; `Duplicate_block` by ruling. Later,
    as leaf work: arrays, bytes, float and mixed blocks,
    `Duplicate_array`, `Obj_dup`. To change: each is a form of cell
    and its primitives, in the heap's files.
11. **A load marked `Mutable` from an immutable block.** Choice:
    defined, as the immutable load. Why: the mark is a licence for
    the compiler, not a test. It arises only where `%field0` or
    `%field1` is applied to an immutable block by an external that a
    program declares for itself (V2's probe, corrected: the standard
    library's `fst` and `snd` are immutable loads), so little rests
    on the choice. To change: one case.
12. **A primitive applied to a name bound to nothing** (a symbol or a
    location). Choice: no value, for every primitive and argument.
    Why: D8, taken for locations too. To change: one test in
    `exec_prim`.

The CRs for 3, 4 and 5 are at the conditions in `FlambdaHeapSpec.v`.

## Done

- The request, third version: what the compiler has (with what the
  front end makes: every store and which loads are marked immutable,
  by file and line); the store; what is observed at exit and how
  refinement is restated; what Simplify needs, as statements; names;
  well-formedness; the fallout; the `Spec` diff, the statements that
  change and the epochs; trust; what the prototype proves. Twelve
  decisions for the human, three with the CR that is in the `Spec`
  file.
- The prototype, on this branch. Edits to `FlambdaValueSpec.v`
  (`location`, `Value_location`, `cell`, `heap`, `cell_of`),
  `FlambdaPrimitiveModel.v` and `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.v` (four
  primitives of the heap under one constructor `Heap`), with their
  repairs (three `Proofs` files, a dozen lines). New:
  `FlambdaHeapSpec.v` and `FlambdaHeapProofs.v`: `exec_prim`, which
  has no value on a name bound to nothing; effects and coeffects with
  four laws; a machine with a heap; the relation on outcomes;
  closedness; the relation on states; thirty-six statements proved,
  no conjecture.
- The machine respects the relation (`heap_run_related`,
  `machine_respects_relation`): related states have related
  behaviours, for the relation without poison. From it: a closed
  state is related to itself and refined by itself; a binding that is
  not used and has at most generative effects may be deleted
  (`delete_unused_let`); a load from a block that is known may be
  replaced by its value (`load_known_field`).
- A step keeps a state closed (`heap_step_closed`). With it: two
  bindings may be exchanged when the first has neither effects nor
  coeffects (`exchange_lets`), and the second of two applications of
  such a primitive, or of an immutable allocation, is the first
  (`cse_adjacent`).
- The relation at exit is redefined: the correspondence relates
  locations and values (a bisimulation), where it related locations
  and symbols. The earlier one was not transitive, through cyclic
  static data (the request, section 3.2, has the counterexample) and,
  with poison, through boxed numbers (R2). `outcome_related_transitive`
  is proved, with and without poison.
- Four discrepancies with the compiler, C4-1 to C4-4 (the team's
  record); the first three each with an fexpr term given to the real
  Simplify, the fourth by reading.
- Lifting a block to a symbol is proved (`lift_block`). The relation
  on states is widened for it (`states_related_except`): the second
  state may bind symbols that the first neither binds nor names. The
  machine respects it from a state whose expression, handlers and code
  neither name nor bind such a symbol
  (`machine_respects_relation_except`, with the invariant
  `state_code_free_of` and `heap_step_code_free_of`); with no symbol
  left out it is the relation of before (`states_related_nothing`).
  No invariant is needed on the values of the first state: a value
  related to one of the second is not a symbol left out. The
  statement lost a premise that the proof does not use (that the
  fields are constants or symbols). Closures are lifted in the same
  way: the closure of one function (`lift_closure`), and a closure of
  a set of several, whose symbols are all bound at once and all left
  out of the relation (`lift_closure_of_set`).
- The rule of the relation for closures is rewritten for C3's sets:
  the closures at the other function slots of two related closures
  are related (`related_projections`), and a closure made by a `Let`
  is related to a symbol of a static set that is bound in full
  (`set_bound`). The first form, made in the rebase, compared the
  other slots by whether they were symbols: it related two symbols of
  a static set whose other symbols were bound to different things,
  which a term can tell apart by a projection, and it did not relate
  a closure to its lifted symbol. R2 and V3 asked for the present
  form. The relation without poison is symmetric
  (`outcome_equivalent_symmetric`).
- Validation, at the last commit of the branch: `make -C rocq -j8
  all` (every statement of the `Spec` files proved, thirty-six of
  them the prototype's) and `make -C rocq assumptions` pass; `make -s
  boot-compiler`, the tests of `tests/semantics/` and `make -s fmt`
  pass, with no expected output changed. One OCaml file is changed,
  the test tool, which prints a location. The extracted files are
  regenerated and not committed.

## Not done

- The heap is not in `state`: the prototype's machine is a copy
  beside it. The sweep is counted in the request (section 7.1); a
  script, not committed, does its mechanical part. C0 has asked C4 to
  produce the sweep as one commit that adds the component and nothing
  else, every file building, for C0 to replay: on the tip that has
  C5's epoch and C0's own preparatory commit (the statements about
  the run of a unit name the initial state through one definition),
  with the heap last among the components of a state and last in an
  exit.
- The lemma on related states is for the relation without poison and
  for the same expression on both sides; related code and
  `plug_refines` are the core's.
- Owed at the epoch of the primitives (R2's reminder): C3's
  `state_valid` (a well-formed unit holds valid values only) must
  reach the fields of cells, with its two new cases of preservation
  (an allocation, a `Block_set`). It is this design's to do, as one
  more instance of the traversal of the values of a state. The
  prototype's machine does not carry `state_valid`. The same for
  kinds: C2's `state_well_kinded` must visit cells, and
  `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.prim_args_well_kinded` is false on every
  primitive under `Heap`, so the primitives of the heap must be given
  their argument kinds there and `exec_prim` the law that `eval_prim`
  has (R2's note; the request, section 7.2).
- Decision 4 is at its first stage in the prototype: field stability
  (the request, section 8.4) is not written.
- Lifting a boxed number is not stated, and no statement moves a
  `Let_static` to toplevel.
- `states_related` and `states_related_except` are two relations with
  one proof. At the epoch that puts the relation in the core they

## Agreed with other streams

- N1 (names), 2026-11-11:
  - Locations are a sort of name of their own, compared by equality
    only. The action on values, cells, heaps and states is a separate
    family of functions taking a `FlambdaNominalSpec.permutation`;
    `renaming` gains no field. N1's generic interface
    (`FlambdaNameAbstractionSpec.v`) takes one sort at a time, so
    locations are an instance with no change to it.
  - An instance gives `apply_renaming`, `free_names` and `equal` (for
    a heap, `map_equal`), with: `equal` is an equivalence; identity;
    composition; the action depends on `permute p` only and preserves
    `equal`; free names are permuted; equal things have the same free
    names; a permutation that fixes the free names of a thing fixes
    the thing.
  - `step` stays a function. `fresh_location` is N1's `fresh_name` on
    the domain of the heap, with the one lemma that it is not in it,
    and is the only function that looks at numbers. `step` is
    equivariant for locations up to a permutation that extends the
    given one; proved once for `step`, lifted to `run`, and stated on
    behaviours observed up to a permutation.
  - The pure case of the relation at exit is the orbit relation,
    `∃ π, equal (π · (vs, h)) (vs', h')`; the relation of the request,
    indexed by a correspondence, has it as an instance, to be linked
    by a theorem.
  - Symbols and code ids are global and get no action. An address is
    a location or a symbol, and only its location part is renamed.
  - Variables and continuations stay out of cells and of
    `Value_location`, and N1's `apply_renaming_state` leaves the heap
    alone.
  - N1's interface is on the integration branch
    (`FlambdaNameAbstractionSpec.v`: `contains_names T` with
    `apply_renaming`, `free_names`, `equal`, and the laws `nominal`;
    `fresh_name` with `fresh_name_fresh_statement`). The prototype's

  - Confirmed to N1 for the prototype's machine: a location is not a
