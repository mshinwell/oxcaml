## What a user of OCaml gets from the sweep

Nothing to see from the sweep alone: it puts the heap into `state`
and into every exit, and no term makes a cell yet. What it buys is
the epoch after it, which is short when nothing else moves under it:
the heap's primitives run by `step`, and V2's conversion of
`Block_set` and of a `Make_block` made mutable. Today the tool
refuses each of the ten sources with mutable state that are staged
with V2. After that epoch, as far as can be said before it is done
(none of this is measured): nine of the ten are converted and run,
before and after Simplify, and eight of them give the `result` that
OCaml computes. The tenth, the array filled and read, waits for
arrays on `step` (P1 has them on the heap's own machine) and for
C5's imported symbols, since its bounds checks raise
`Invalid_argument`. Validated then, that is, the term after Simplify
proved to refine the term before: the two names for one `ref`; and
the loop summing into a `ref` as soon as `tail_loop.ml` is, since it
needs the same of loops and lifting and nothing more of the heap.
Run with the same result on both sides and reported as not
validated: the five where Simplify replaces the result of a store by
the unit constant (the counter, the record updated, the two fields
swapped, the two counters, the accumulator given to an `iter`),
until the validator has that rewrite with its proof; and the `ref`
that Simplify removes, until two exits are compared up to a relation
between their heaps and not exactly. Reported as undefined before and
after, and not as a pass: the record with fields of both kinds, until
field stability, which is written and proved, is not in this tree
and waits for the human's word.

## Decided overnight

The human is away and has said: "If there are considerations to be
made, document them, take a sensible decision and proceed; and
tomorrow morning we can review them." The orchestrator has taken the
twelve decisions of the request as recommended there, as amended by
R2's reviews, for the human to review. Each: the question; the
options; the choice; why; what it costs to change later. The numbers
are those of "Decisions for the human" in the request.

1. **What has an identity.** Options: only blocks made `Mutable` or
   `Immutable_unique`; every allocation; the first plus closures.
   Choice: the first. Why: the compiler shares and duplicates
   immutable blocks, boxed numbers and closures (CSE, lifting,
   unboxing), so a model that told them apart would refute rewrites
   the compiler makes. To change: every block of every statement goes
   in the heap; a redesign.
2. **What a location is.** Options: a name with a fresh one chosen by
   a function; the next number. Choice: a name. Why: D29, and a
   bijection between runs is needed either way. To change: nothing in
   statements; `fresh_location` becomes transparent.
3. **`Phys_equal`.** Options: (a) no value on anything allocated; (b)
   defined but on two immutable allocated values; (c) (b) and false
   by shape; (d) by contents; (e) an oracle. Choice: (b), on top of
   what P1 landed for immediates, which has no value on null or on
   poison either; the prototype follows it. Why: it gives `==` its
   meaning where something has an identity and on immediates; (d) is
   not preserved by Simplify (R2-21). It is weaker coverage: nothing
   is proved of a program after it compares two immutable allocated
   values. To change to (c): leaf work in the primitives' files. To
   (e): `state` and `refines` change, an epoch.
4. **A load marked `Immutable` from a block made `Mutable`.** Options:
   (a) no value; (b) field stability (the load makes the field
   stable, a store into a stable field has no value); (c) mutability
   by field from the compiler. Choice: (a) for epochs 1 to 3, then
   (b). Why: (a) is the simplest sound start and (b) is exactly what
   Simplify assumes, with no change to the compiler. (a) leaves
   records with both sorts of field undefined, so it must not outlast
   the first epochs. To change from (a) to (b): the heap's files only
   (a cell remembers stable fields; `no_effects` and `no_coeffects`
   restated; the relation orders the marks). Outside the model by the
   human's rulings, and refused by the conversion: tail-modulo-cons,
   and `Duplicate_block` followed by stores. Recursive values: X5
   found nothing wrong; they are calls of C functions, which the
   model does not have. Recursive modules (X5-2): outside what any
   stage covers, since a field may be read before the run-time
   library overwrites it.
5. **`Block_set` into what was not made `Mutable`.** Options: no
   value; write anyway. Choice: no value. Why: the front end makes no
   such store, and Simplify keeps what it knows of the block. To
   change: one case of `exec_prim`.
6. **What an exit observes.** Options, cumulative: (i) values and
   heap as they are; (ii) up to a bijection on locations; (iii)
   immutable values by contents, through symbols (D6); (iv) poison of
   the source is anything of its kind (D26). Choice: (i) for epoch 2,
   (ii) and (iii) in epoch 3, (iv) after. Why: each step is sound by
   itself and the proofs come before the epoch that needs them. To
   change: `refines` is restated, an epoch, with the lemma that
   carries the old theorems over.
7. **Closures at exit.** Options: the whole value; less, by a
   relation on code ids. Choice: the whole value (D1 as it is). To
   change: a third component of the correspondence, S4's.
8. **How the heap enters the statements.** Options: a fifth field of
   the state; one record for the static environment and the heap.
   Choice: a fifth field. Why: less to change now, and nothing
   planned adds to the memory. To change: a second sweep of the same
   size.
9. **`expr_refines` over closed memories only.** Options: yes, in the
   definition; no. Choice: yes. Why: the lemma on related states
   needs it and every state reached from a term is closed. To change:
   the premise moves to each theorem.
10. **Scope.** In: mutable and unique blocks of values on the heap.
    Not modelled: local allocation and regions (the conversion drops
    regions and reads a local allocation as one on the heap, D37).
    Out: atomics, bigarrays, lazy
    blocks, off-heap memory; static blocks that are not `Immutable`
    until symbols are addresses; `Duplicate_block` by ruling. Later,
    as leaf work: arrays, bytes, float and mixed blocks,
    `Duplicate_array`, `Obj_dup`. To change: each is a form of cell
    and its primitives, in the heap's files.
11. **A load marked `Mutable` from an immutable block.** Choice:
    defined, as the immutable load. Why: the mark is a licence for
    the compiler, not a test. It arises only where `%field0` or
    `%field1` is applied to an immutable block by an external that a
    program declares for itself (V2's probe, corrected: the standard
    library's `fst` and `snd` are immutable loads), so little rests
    on the choice. To change: one case.
12. **A primitive applied to a name bound to nothing** (a symbol or a
    location). Choice: no value, for every primitive and argument.
    Why: D8, taken for locations too. To change: one test in
    `exec_prim`.

The CRs for 4 and 5 are at the conditions in `FlambdaHeapSpec.v`.
The CR for 3 is P1's, before `FlambdaPrimitiveSpec.eval_phys_equal`.

## Decided in the rehearsal of the sweep

Where the request and the agreement with C0 did not say, C4 decided,
for C0 and the human to review. The fallout note has the list in
full; in short:

1. The heap is named `hp` in statements (`h` is a handler in the
   proofs).
2. One binder for a statement, after the binder of the static
   environment, shared by the two states of the statement: exits are
   compared by their heaps, so two heaps would make the statement
   false.
3. `Exits` carries the heap, as `Exit` does; `refines` and
   `equivalent` compare it exactly.
4. An example's state has `empty_heap`, and so has its exit.
5. Where a proofs file compares two results, it compares the heaps of
   two exits.
6. C5's machine with a stack carries the heap in `Stack_exit` and in
   its five functions.
7. N1's `rename_state` leaves the heap as it is.
8. The heap's own machine stays beside `step` until the primitives
   run on `step`.
9. A section over a static environment gains a variable for the heap.
10. `state_values_all` gains the cells with the primitives, not in
    the sweep.
11. A quantifier inside a statement that binds a static environment
    of its own binds a heap of its own.
12. Where a branch of a match on an exit compares another result
    with that exit, the exit has a heap of its own and the other side
    keeps the heap of the statement.
13. A lemma that says that a call does not exit says it of an exit
    with any heap.
14. Where a statement runs from the empty heap and an existential
    binds the static environment of a state that the run reaches,
    that state has `empty_heap`, and the existential binds no heap.

To change any of these but the tenth later is to run the script again
with another rule; the tenth is a matter of which commit carries it.

- A unit run under a continuation environment
  (`FlambdaRunUnderSpec.v`, `FlambdaRunUnderProofs.v`; new files,
  nothing that exists changes). A unit leaves by applying a way out, a
  continuation that its environment does not bind. Run on its own,
  from no continuation, that is an exit. Run from a continuation
  environment `base` it is the application of what `base` binds the
  name to. Four statements, proved, of a term whose code is closed
  (`code_closed`), from any variables, heap and static environment
  whose code is closed (`static_code_closed`: the empty one, and that
  of a unit's imports, which bind no code): a state that the unit
  reaches on its own is reached from `base` in as many steps, the same
  but for its continuations (`run_under_statement`); where the unit
  exits, the step of that state from `base` is what `base` binds the
  continuation of the exit to, applied to the values of the exit under
  the static environment and the heap that the unit left
  (`exit_under_statement`); where it goes wrong, so does the run from
  `base` (`wrong_under_statement`); so the behaviours from `base` are
  those of the unit, with an exit replaced by the behaviours of that
  application (`behaves_under_statement`). Nothing is asked of `base`.
  Without closed code the statements are false: the body of code runs
  under the two continuations of its call, so a continuation that it
  leaves free is a way out whatever `base` binds. The files are for
  a comparison of what two units leave by what is run next on it.
  Asked of R4.
