## Agreed with other streams

With T5 (join):

- The parameter is `join_scoped_envs : meet_expanded_head -> Scope.t ->
  Meet_env.t -> Typing_env.t -> list Typing_env.t -> option
  Meet_env.t`: `Join_env.cut_and_n_way_join` with `n_way_join_type`
  inside. `None` is a fatal error or no fuel.
- Rely and guarantee: the join's contract supposes
  `meet_expanded_head_sound` and `meet_expanded_head_keeps` of the
  meet it is given.
- J1 is per valuation: it satisfies the environment of the meet and
  the scoped one. Agreement is exact on the variables known (the same
  value, or none in both). `no_bottom_types` (in `typing_env_fits`)
  is asked because the compiler's join loses that agreement without
  it. It is not enough for the compiler's join to have J1 (T5-10,
  below).
  The scoped environments are kinded; the result is kinded (T5's
  `join_keeps_kinded`, whose premises `scoped_env_of` gives).
- The default strategy (`Dfs`) only.
- T5's join calls my `Meet_env.add_variable_definition`,
  `add_env_extension` (the one that is not strict), `make_bottom` and
  `enter_scope`.
- T4-1 is read the same way by both.
- T5-10, decided: the compiler's n-way join has J1 only of the runs
  that have the values that it reads. `recover_inverse_relations`
  gives a variable `r` the type "`Is_int` of `x`" where `x` may have
  no value in the run; and the join of the types of a name passes
  over a type that it finds to have no value, which is an obviously
  bottom type or a head of naked immediates that its relations reduce
  to nothing, and then gives the name what it is at the other uses.
  `no_bottom_types` rules out the obviously bottom type only: R1 and
  T7 ran a case with a stored type "0 or 2, `Is_int` of `p`" where
  `p` is a tagged immediate, and both joins of the compiler make the
  name the constant that it is at the other use. T5 states three
  conditions on a run (`scrutinees_have_values`,
  `joined_variables_have_values`, `imported_variables_have_values`,
  in `FlambdaJoinSpec.v`). J1 stays strict: weakening its conclusion
  is not sound for a meet that follows. The instance of the meet for
  the compiler's own join is owed. Its obligation, which nothing
  discharges yet: every join that a meet makes is made on runs that
  have the three conditions. They are hypotheses of that instance
  wherever the meet is not shown to establish them, with a `CR
  mshinwell` (nothing in the compiler is known to establish the first
  and the third). X2 has an OCaml program on which the first fails:
  compiled with the n-way join it answers a wrong number (an arm of a
  switch is deleted by the `Get_tag` reduction, on a run in which the
  scrutinee of the recovered relation has no value).
