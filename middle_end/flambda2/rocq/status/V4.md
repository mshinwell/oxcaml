# V4: loops

The aim: units made from OCaml sources that have a loop (a `while`, a
`for`, a tail-recursive function) are validated by
`FlambdaValidateSpec.validate_unit`, and not only found the same on
one run.

## Done

Each is a function on terms with its statement in a `Spec` file and the
proof in the `Proofs` file beside it; no axiom, nothing admitted.

- **`rewrite_rec_handlers`** (`FlambdaRecHandlersSpec.v`): a rewrite
  that refines, or is an equivalence, under all environments may be
  applied to the handlers of recursive continuations. It is what lets
  the existing passes into a loop; they need no change.
- **`sink_let_conts`** (`FlambdaSinkContsSpec.v`): an equivalence that
  moves a non-recursive `Let_cont` into the handler that alone uses
  it, where `inline_tail_conts` then inlines it.
  `FlambdaContAlikeProofs.v` is the tool of proof: continuations that
  apply alike may stand for each other.
- **`drop_unused_params`** (`FlambdaUnusedParamsSpec.v`): a refinement
  that removes the parameters of a continuation that its handler does
  not use, at the binder and at every application in scope. For a
  recursive continuation it is the parameters that are not free in the
  handler as it stands.
- **`shortcut_switches`** (`FlambdaShortcutSpec.v`): an equivalence; an
  arm of a `Switch` that applies a continuation whose handler only
  applies another applies that other.
- **`follow_aliases_in_loops`** (`FlambdaLoopAliasesSpec.v`): an
  equivalence; inside the handlers of recursive continuations, a
  variable bound outside to another name or to a constant is replaced
  by it.
- **`loopify_unit`** (`FlambdaLoopifySpec.v`): a refinement; the self
  tail calls of the code named become jumps to a recursive
  continuation around its body, as Simplify's loopify makes them. The
  proof is a simulation in which a step of the first run is answered
  by one or more of the second (`FlambdaSimulationProofs.v`).
- **The wiring** in `FlambdaValidateSpec.v`: the passes above are in
  the normal form of the term before, in the order the file gives,
  and the normal form is applied in the handlers of recursive
  continuations too (`normalise_code_before`); the term after has its
  aliases followed and its constants folded there as well.
  `validate_unit_sound` is proved as before, its statement unchanged.

- **Renaming.** The passes commute with renaming:
  `rewrite_rec_handlers f` for an `f` that does, `sink_let_conts`,
  `shortcut_switches`, `drop_params` with the keys of its masks
  renamed, `follow_aliases_in_loops` with its typing environment
  renamed, `loopify_unit` with the continuations of its list renamed.
  The proofs are a section of `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`
  (N3's file, by agreement), where the normalisations of the
  validation use them; they are lemmas there, in the form of that
  file's `Spec`, until N3 states them.
- **Exceptions.** Since terms have trap actions, extra arguments of
  exception continuations and a flag on exception handlers:
  - `loopify`: a self tail call has no extra arguments for its
    exception continuation (Simplify's third test, which the model
    needs too); the loop is not an exception handler and the jumps
    have no trap action;
  - `drop_unused_params`: the trap action of an application stays;
    the first parameter of an exception handler is kept whether used
    or not; a handler that loses parameters stays an exception
    handler if it was one;
  - `shortcut_switches`: no shortcut of an exception handler, nor
    through an `Apply_cont` with a trap action; an arm with a trap
    action stays;
  - `sink_let_conts`: a continuation that a trap action names is
    free where the trap action is.

`ocaml/while_loop.ml` of `middle_end/flambda2/tests/semantics/` goes
from "not validated" to "validated"; no other example changes.

### Second series

- **`remove_unused_defined_lets_in_loops`** (`FlambdaLoopLetsSpec.v`):
  an equivalence; `remove_unused_defined_lets` made to go into the
  handlers of recursive continuations, with the parameters and the
  variables in scope at the `Let_cont` in scope. For the term after.
- **`bind_aliased_params`** (`FlambdaParamAliasesSpec.v`): a
  refinement; a parameter that every application gives the same
  constant, symbol or variable is bound to it by a `Let` at the start
  of the handler, as Simplify's `lets_to_introduce`; for a recursive
  continuation alone in its `Let_cont` the applications inside the
  handler may also pass the parameter itself. `drop_unused_params`
  then removes the parameter. Together they remove from a loop the
  mutable variables that its body does not assign.
- **`forward_applications`** (`FlambdaForwardSpec.v`): a refinement; an
  application of a non-recursive continuation whose handler is one
  `Apply_cont` becomes that `Apply_cont`, the arguments put for the
  parameters, wherever it is (Simplify's `Continuation_shortcut`); a
  goto arm of a `Switch` takes it with its trap action
  (`rebuild_arm`). `FlambdaRoundsProofs.v` is the form of simulation
  it needs: one or more steps of each run to a round.
- **`inline_tail_conts_kinded`** (`FlambdaInlineTailSpec.v`): an
  equivalence, for the term after; `inline_tail_conts` of
  `FlambdaNormaliseSpec.v` where the handler gives one kind for each
  parameter. The term after has it since (`inline_once_applied_after`,
  V2's).
- **The wiring.** The term before is normalised in two phases
  (`normalise_before`). The first (`normalise_params_round`) is the
  round for an expression with `forward_applications` after
  `shortcut_switches`, then `remove_unused_conts`, and
  `bind_aliased_params` before `drop_unused_params`; it inlines no
  call, and it normalises the handlers of loops before what is around
  them (`normalise_code_params`). The second is the rounds as they
  were. Term after:
  `remove_unused_defined_lets_in_loops` where
  `remove_unused_defined_lets` was. `validate_unit_sound` is proved as
  before, its statement unchanged.
- **Renaming.** Lemmas for the three new passes in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`, and the lemma for
  `drop_params` proved again.

Measured with `flsemantics.exe` on units made from OCaml sources, not
in the tree (V2 adds such units to `ocaml/`): validated now, and not
before the series, are a `while` in a `while`, an `if` inside a
`while`, two `while`s in sequence, a `while` followed by a use of a
constant, the sum of the first integers, a countdown, and `for` loops
(a sum, two references, a `for` in a `for`, an `if` inside, `downto`).
No expected file of the tree changes.

### Loopify in the validation

- **`validate_unit` takes the code to loopify** (`G`, each code id
  with a name for its loop), before the liftings. The two terms are
  first compared as they are given; after that the term before is
  `loopify_unit G` of the term given, in every check. `G` is a
  guess, as the other arguments are: `loopify_unit` refines for every
  `G` and leaves alone the code it may not change. The two statements
  quantify over `G` and are otherwise unchanged;
  `validate_unit_equivariant_statement` renames the names of the
  loops with the terms.
- **The test tool** notes, as it converts a unit, the code that the
  front end marks for loopify (`Loopify_attribute.should_loopify`), and
  gives it to `validate_unit`, each with a continuation name that
  nothing has; its other guesses are made of the loopified term.

Measured on the units with tail-recursive functions made from OCaml
sources, with the list of code whose calls are left (V2's) under it.
Of the tree, `tail_loop.ml`, `list_length.ml` and `list_rev.ml` are
validated (their expected files change with the tool). Of eight more
that are not in the tree, seven are: a count of bits, a factorial
and a sum with an accumulator, Fibonacci with two, a gcd, a
tail-recursive function local to an expression, and a recursive
function with no tail call (no loop is made). In each the loop that
the validation makes of the code is the one Simplify made. Not
validated: `string_sum.ml` of the tree and a unit with two
tail-recursive functions, each for one line (below), and
`pair_loop.ml`, where Simplify also unboxes a parameter of the loop.

### Third series

- **`drop_dead_params`** (`FlambdaDeadParamsSpec.v`): a refinement;
  `drop_unused_params` with two things more. It removes the `Let`s
  whose variables are left unused (where
  `FlambdaNormaliseSpec.named_may_go` lets them go), and at a recursive
  `Let_cont` it searches for the masks that remove the most: those
  under which the parameters removed are not free in the handlers as
  rewritten under the same masks. So a parameter that a loop only
  passes round to itself goes, with the `Let` that computes its next
  value: what Simplify's data flow analysis removes. The proof is the
  simulation of `FlambdaUnusedParamsProofs.v`, whose relation now also
  lets the second term lack a `Let` whose variable it does not use;
  the simulation is by rounds (`FlambdaRoundsProofs.v`). Nothing is
  proved of the search but that its answer is left as it is by one
  more round, which is all the statement needs.
- **The wiring.** `drop_dead_params` is where `drop_unused_params` was
  in the first phase of the term before (`normalise_expr_params`); the
  second phase keeps `drop_unused_params`. `validate_unit_sound` is
  proved as before, its statement unchanged.
- **Renaming.** `drop_dead_params_equivariant` in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`.

Measured as above: validated now, and not before this series, are a
`while` that increments a counter nothing reads, and a `for` with two
references that only feed each other. Of twenty-five units with loops
tried, twenty-three are validated; the two that are not keep a boolean
untagged in a parameter of the loop (S6). Two examples are in the tree
(`while_counter_unread.fl`, `for_two_unread.fl`), and of the units
made from OCaml sources that were there, `while_unused_counter.ml` is
validated now; its expected file changes with the examples.

### A function with a loop

- **`forward_applications` left one more continuation alone**, by a
  guard on the syntax: one whose applications all give a parameter

  analysis of aliases rebinds such a parameter by a `Let` at the head
  of the handler before it looks for a shortcut, so it records none,
  and the term after keeps the continuation. It is how a `while` loop
  in a function leaves for the return continuation with what it
  computed. The guard is gone: the term after has those applications
  forwarded too ("Applications with arguments, in the term after",
  below).

Measured: a function whose body is a `while` loop, exported, is now
validated (`function_with_while.fl`, in the tree). No unit that was
validated is lost, of the units with loops tried and of the tree. A
function with a loop that the unit calls is validated where Simplify
inlines the call, where the function is exported with a `for` loop,
and, with the list of code whose calls are left, where Simplify
leaves the calls (a function with a `while` loop called twice).

### The parameters of code

- **`remove_unused_defined_lets_in_code`** (`FlambdaCodeLetsSpec.v`):
  an equivalence; `remove_unused_defined_lets_in_loops` in the body of
  every piece of code, with the variables that a call binds in scope:
  `my_closure` at the kind `Value`, and the parameters at the kinds of
  the arity of the code. The new body and the old refine each other
  where the code runs, which is at a call, and a call tests those
  kinds (`FlambdaContextProofs.let_static_code_refines_where`).
- **The wiring.** It is the last rewrite of a round for the term
  after (`normalise_after_round`). `validate_unit` and its statements
  are unchanged.
- **Renaming.** `remove_unused_defined_lets_in_code_equivariant` in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`.

What it is for: a tail-recursive function with a parameter that every
self call passes on unchanged (the string of `string_sum.ml`).
Simplify removes that parameter from the loop and leaves `let x = p`
at the head of the loop, unused, for the parameter `p` of the code.
The term before loses it by a refinement; the term after may lose it
only by an equivalence, which needs the kind of `p`.

All told, of sixty-four units with loops or tail-recursive functions
tried, fifty-eight are validated; the six that are not are under "Not

### The jumps that the fold makes in the term after

- **`forward_jumps`** (`FlambdaForwardJumpsSpec.v`): an equivalence;
  `forward_applications` with entries only for the continuations of
  no parameters whose handler gives no kinds. An application of one
  whose handler is one `Apply_cont`, with no trap action on either,
  becomes that `Apply_cont`, and an arm of a `Switch` in the same
  way. It is made of `forwarding` and `forward_apply_cont` of
  `FlambdaForwardSpec.v`.
- **The proof.** The relation between the two runs in
  `FlambdaForwardProofs.v` is taken with a flag: as it was, for the
  refinement; or strict, where every entry is for a continuation of
  no parameters and no kinds. Then the second run is undefined where
  the first is, and the rounds are read both ways (`fwd_round`,
  `fwd_round_back`). `forward_applications`, its statement and its
  theorem are as they were.
- **The wiring.** In `inline_once_applied_after`, after
  `inline_tail_conts_kinded` and before a `remove_unused_conts`; so
  in every round of the term after, in the bodies of code and the
  handlers of loops too. `validate_unit` and its statements are
  unchanged. (`forward_kinded` is there now in its place: see
  "Applications with arguments, in the term after".)
- **Renaming.** `forward_jumps_equivariant` in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`. The statements of scope are
  of the rounds of the term before, so there is none to make.

What it is for (found by T6 and V2): where every application of a
continuation gives a parameter the same constant, Simplify removes
the parameter and binds it at the head of the handler, once the
handler is rebuilt. A `Switch` on it in the handler stays, so the
handler is not one `Apply_cont`, and Simplify records no shortcut.
The validation folds that `Switch` in both terms; the term before
then has the applications forwarded, and the term after kept them.
The parameters that the unboxing of a variant adds are often such.

Measured: of the 600 generated units, 588 are validated. The pass
turns four of them, three with functions and one with scalars that
are not known (on the tree before the merging of the arms of a
`Switch`: 586 with the pass, 582 without, the other verdicts the
same). No unit that was validated is lost, of those, of the units
with loops tried and of the tree, where no expected file changes.

### The place of a continuation among the `Let`s

- **`sink_conts_to_uses`** (`FlambdaSinkUsesSpec.v`): an equivalence;
  a non-recursive `Let_cont` goes down past a `Let` whose variable
  its handler does not mention, past a `Let_static`, and past another
  non-recursive `Let_cont` where neither names the other (the three
  moves of `FlambdaSinkContsProofs.v`), and stops above the first
  thing that it cannot pass. (As first landed it moved a binding only
  if it could reach an `Apply` or a `Switch` that names its
  continuation, and left it where it was otherwise. Two more shapes
  need the move: the way out of a loop, bound before a `Let` in one
  term and after it in the other, which only the handler of the
  recursive continuation names; and, where a function with a test is
  inlined, the continuation that follows the call, which an arm and
  the continuation of the other arm apply. Of the 240 generated units
  with exceptions and with loops 198 are validated where 196 were,
  one of each shape; no other verdict changes, of those, of the 600
  or of the tree. `ocaml/loop_exit_inlined.ml` has the first shape
  and is validated at the comparison of the two normalised terms
  where it was at the second asking; `ocaml/inlined_test_join.ml` has
  the second and was not validated.)
- **`placed`** (`FlambdaValidateSpec.v`): that pass in a term, in the
  handlers of its recursive continuations and in the bodies of its
  code. `validate_unit` applies it once to each term that it
  compares after the first comparison (which is of the two terms as
  they are given), when the rounds are over: to the term before
  normalised, to the term after normalised, and to the term before
  with the liftings made. `validate_unit_sound` and
  `validate_unit_sound_everywhere` are proved as before, their
  statements unchanged in their text.
- **Scope and renaming.** `sink_conts_to_uses_keeps_scope` in
  `FlambdaLoopsScopeProofs.v`; `sink_conts_to_uses_equivariant`,
  `placed_equivariant` and `placed_keeps_scope` in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`, where
  `validate_unit_renamed_alike` uses them.
- **The test tool** shows the terms with their continuations placed,
  as they are compared; it guesses the liftings from the terms as the
  rounds leave them, as before.

What it is for: the front end binds the continuation that a call
returns to above the `Let`s of the arguments of the call. Where
Simplify has changed the parameter of that continuation (a result
that is not used, which is no longer passed; a boolean result that a
test consumes, which is untagged), it makes the call return to a new
continuation, bound at the call, under those `Let`s
(`Expr_builder.rewrite_fixed_arity_continuation`, the case
`New_wrapper`), and the old one is inlined in it. In the handler, the
`Let` of the untagging then stands before the bindings of the
continuations of the test. The two terms have the same bindings in
another order, and the comparison is of the bindings in their order.

Why once, and not in a round: run in every round, the pass lost a
unit that is validated (the continuation of a call is moved, and the
call is then inlined), and a `Let_cont` moved between rounds changes
what a rewrite of the next round finds where it looks
(`bind_aliased_params`), which follows Simplify on the term as it
was.

Measured: of the 600 generated units, 592 are validated where 591
were, and one that was validated at the second asking is at the
first. Of 240 more generated units, with exceptions and with loops,
189 are validated where 133 were: all 56 are among the 120 with
loops, of which 72 are validated where 16 were. No unit that was
validated is lost, of those and of the tree, where no expected file
changes.

### A call that returns further on

- **The forwarding takes a call too**
  (`FlambdaForwardSpec.forward_apply`): an `Apply` that returns to a
  continuation with an entry returns to the continuation that the
  handler applies, if the handler passes its parameters on as they
  are, in their order, with no trap action (`passes_on`,
  `alias_of`). It is what Simplify does where it rebuilds a call
  (`Expr_builder.apply_continuation_aliases`, with
  `Continuation_shortcut.to_alias`). The exception continuation of
  the call is left. (As first landed it was in
  `forward_applications`, in every round of the term before; where
  it is applied now is in "The calls of the term before, where
  Simplify left one" below.) The result refines the term. Nothing is
  asked of the kinds
  that the two continuations give their parameters: the term tests
  the results against both, the result of the pass against the second
  only, so the term may be undefined where the result is not, and
  never the reverse.
- **The proof** (`FlambdaForwardProofs.v`): the relation between what
  a continuation name stands for in the two runs has one more case,
  for what a callee is given to return to (`Alias_fwd`): in the first
  run a continuation related to the closure of an entry, in the
  second what that closure applies. Results given to the first reach,
  after the steps through the handlers, what answers the same results
  given to the second; so a round at a call, or at an application of
  related continuations, is some steps of the first run to one of the
  second. The relation has a flag for whether such a pair is allowed:
  a name with an entry never stands for one. `forward_jumps` is as it
  was: it leaves every call, and its relation has no such pair.
- **Scope and renaming.** The proofs that the pass keeps scope
  (`FlambdaLoopsScopeProofs.v`) and commutes with renaming
  (`FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`) have the case.

What it is for: a function that returns what a call returns after
work on it that comes to nothing (a record made of the result and the
field read back, in `ocaml/call_result_passed_on.ml`). The validation
removes the work, and the call is left returning to a continuation
whose handler only passes the result on to the return continuation of
the function; Simplify makes the call return there.

Measured: of the 240 generated units with exceptions and with loops,
196 are validated where 189 were: the seven are among the 120 with
loops, of which 79 are validated where 72 were. Of the 600 generated
units 592 are validated, as before. No unit that was validated is
lost, of those, of the units with loops tried and of the tree, where
no expected file changes.

### Applications with arguments, in the term after

- **`forward_kinded`** (`FlambdaForwardKindedSpec.v`): an equivalence;
  an application of a non-recursive continuation whose handler is one
  `Apply_cont` becomes that `Apply_cont`, where each argument is
  known, by the `Let` or the handler that binds it in the term, to
  have the kind that the handler gives the parameter it is given to
  (a constant has its own kind, a symbol the kind `Value`), the
  handler gives as many kinds as it has parameters, and neither
  `Apply_cont` has a trap action. An arm of a `Switch` in the same
  way. A call is left. It is made of `forwarding` and `forwarded` of
  `FlambdaForwardSpec.v` and of the kinds in scope of
  `FlambdaNormaliseSpec.v`. `forward_jumps` is its case of no
  parameters; the validation does not call it any more. The pass,
  its statement and its proofs are kept; removing them would take
  their lines out of the shared lists and of the files on renaming.
- **`forward_kinded_in_code`**: the same pass in the body of each
  piece of code, started with the kinds of what a call binds
  (`FlambdaCodeLetsSpec.code_in_scope`), so that an argument that is
  a parameter of the function is known.
- **`forward_applications` no longer has the guard** that guessed at
  what Simplify's analysis of aliases left
  (`given_one_inner_variable` and `forward_entry` are gone; the entry
  of a continuation is that of `forwarding`, whose tests on names
  stay). The three statements of the pass, that its result refines
  the term, that it keeps scope and that it commutes with a renaming,
  are of `forward_applications` on every term and do not name the
  entry: their text is unchanged, and they are now said of a pass
  that forwards more.
- **The proof.** The strict relation of `FlambdaForwardProofs.v`
  carries the kinds of the variables bound around a point and those
  that the handlers of the continuations bound around it give their
  parameters, and the two states have variables of those kinds
  (`FlambdaNormaliseProofs.kinds_hold`). An application that goes on
  has arguments with values of the kinds of the parameters, so the
  first run takes its step there. `forward_jumps` is proved by the
  same relation, with no argument to know. In code, as
  `FlambdaCodeLetsProofs.v` does.
- **The wiring.** `forward_kinded` is where `forward_jumps` was in
  `inline_once_applied_after`. `forward_kinded_in_code` comes in a
  round of the term after, before
  `remove_unused_defined_lets_in_code`. `validate_unit` and its
  statements are unchanged.
- **Scope and renaming.** `forward_kinded_equivariant` and
  `forward_kinded_in_code_equivariant` in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`.
  `forward_applications_keeps_scope` (`FlambdaLoopsScopeProofs.v`)
  and `forward_applications_equivariant` are proved without the
  guard; their statements are unchanged.

What it is for (asked by P1 and V2, and found again by T2 and T4): a
function with a region that ends where two ways through the function
meet: one that matches on the result of a `try`, or on a block that
it builds on one way of a test only and that does not leave it. The
continuation where the two ways meet ends the region before it passes
its parameter on. Simplify keeps the region, so that handler is not
one `Apply_cont` for it, and it records no shortcut. The model has no
regions: in both terms the handler is one `Apply_cont`; the term
before had its applications forwarded, and the term after kept them.
In the tree: `ocaml/local_block_matched.ml`, and
`ocaml/local_block_matched_argument.ml` where an arm of the match
returns an argument of the function (the pass in the bodies of code);
`ocaml/try_result_matched.ml`, which names a predefined exception and
so is run with its import: it is validated with nothing imported, its
verdict, with closures lifted, being outside what is proved of runs
with imports.

Why the guard went: with the pass in the term after, a continuation
that Simplify left for the reason the guard guessed at is forwarded
there, and must be in the term before. Without the guard both terms
are forwarded whatever Simplify's analysis found. The guess was wrong
where the variable given is in one cycle with the parameter: Simplify
finds no alias there and records the shortcut. It is the result of a
loop inside a tail-recursive function, where the loop may leave the
accumulator of the function and its result is the accumulator of the
tail call (`ocaml/loop_result_passed_round.ml`, which was not
validated).

Measured: of the 240 generated units with exceptions and with loops,
199 are validated where 198 were (the one with that loop). Of the 600
generated units 592 are validated, as before, every verdict the same.
No unit that was validated is lost, of those, of the units with loops
tried and of the tree, where no expected file changes.

### The calls of the term before, where Simplify left one

- **The pair that was lost** (found by X5):
  `ocaml/stack_argument_result_returned.ml`, a function that
  allocates a pair on its stack, gives it to another function and
  returns what that function returns. It was validated before "A
  call that returns further on" above, and was not after. The front
  end binds, for the result of the call, a continuation whose handler
  ends the region of the stack block and then applies the return
  continuation of the code. The conversion drops regions, so in the
  model that handler passes its parameter on and does nothing else.
  For Simplify it was not a single `Apply_cont`: the term after has
  the call returning to it. `forward_applications`, in the rounds of
  the term before, made the call return to the return continuation
  of the code, and the term after cannot be given the same by an
  equivalence: the handler's test of the kinds of the results would
  go with the continuation (`forward_kinded` takes on an
  `Apply_cont` whose arguments have known kinds, not a call).

## Not done

- `drop_dead_params` and `drop_unused_params` leave the parameters
  of a continuation that an `Apply` returns to (Simplify makes a
  wrapper continuation there). `drop_dead_params` removes a `Let`
  only where `named_may_go` allows, as `remove_unused_lets` does: a
  parameter that only feeds a primitive with an effect stays.
- A loop parameter whose representation Simplify changes is not
  validated: Simplify gives the loop one more parameter for the
  untagged or unboxed value and computes it at each application. Four
  of the units tried have it: a boolean reference that a `while`
  tests, a boolean accumulator of a tail-recursive function, the
  bound of a `for` inside one, a pair passed round one
  (`pair_loop.ml`). Unboxing of continuation parameters is S6, with
  T6; its passes are
  for a non-recursive continuation whose applications give something
  that the term shows. What a loop needs besides:
  - the recursive case, with the applications that are in the
    handler itself;
  - the read taken from anywhere in the handler: for a recursive
    continuation Simplify decides by the subkind of the parameter
    alone (`simplify_single_recursive_handler` of
    `simplify_let_cont_expr.ml`), and unboxes at every application;
  - a `Let` of the unboxing primitive at an application whose
    argument the term does not show to be boxed. Where the argument
    is the result of a primitive that always gives a tagged
    immediate, a law of the primitives is enough. Where it is a
    parameter, of the code (the entry of every loop made of a
    function) or of a continuation, the model has nothing: the
    source untags the parameter on some paths only, Simplify on
    every call, and `Untag_immediate` has a value on a tagged
    immediate only. On a call that gives the parameter another value
    the term after is undefined where the term before is not, so the
    term after does not refine the term before, and no rewrite of
    the validation can make it. The test tool shows it on a unit
    that passes a string through `%obj_magic`.

  What the model would have to carry: the subkind of a parameter of
  code and of a parameter of a continuation, the recursive ones
  included, with no step where a call or an application binds a
  value that is not of it. Two subkinds are enough for these units: a
  tagged immediate, and a block of one tag and size. Nothing is
  needed of a `Let`, nor of the result of a call. The loop that
  `loopify_unit` makes of a function would give its parameters the
  subkinds of the parameters of the code, so that the jump tests what
  the call tested.
- `bind_aliased_params` binds a parameter that Simplify keeps: one
  of a continuation in a loop that is always given a parameter of
  the loop, where some path gives it back to the loop unchanged (a
  `while` with two conditions joined whose body leaves a variable as
  it is on one path). Simplify's analysis of aliases finds no alias
  among the parameters of one cycle
  (`simplify/flow/dominator_graph.ml`, `extract_doms`). Such a unit
  also has the boolean of the condition untagged, so it waits on the
  unboxing first. What it would take: a guard on the pass, which
  asks that the variable given does not come from the parameter, by
  what the applications of the term pass to what.
- `shortcut_switches` makes an arm of a `Switch` go where its
  continuation goes only where the `Switch` is directly under the
  `Let_cont`. Where `Let`s are between the two once a call is inlined
  (met by T6), the rounds of the first phase that now end the
  normalisation of the term before (`demote_and_tidy`) forward the
  arm. A pass that looks past such `Let`s is written and proved (an
  equivalence, with its statements of scope and of renaming) and is
  not in the tree: with those rounds it changes the verdict of no
  unit, of the tree, of the generated ones or of those with loops.
- The fold makes a second difference at a handler whose `Switch` is
  on a parameter that Simplify removed. An arm of that `Switch` that
  is never taken may be the one application that gives a parameter of
  the next continuation another value (`switch x | 0 -> k (p)
  | 1 -> k (0)`, with `k (p)` everywhere else). Simplify, whose
  analysis of aliases sees that arm, keeps the parameter of `k`; the
  term before, folded first, has every application give `p`, and
  `bind_aliased_params` removes the parameter. Such a unit is not
  validated (seen by making every other application give `p` in a
  unit that `forward_jumps` validates). What it would take: for the
  term after, an equivalence that binds a parameter that every
  application gives the same variable, where the kind of the
  parameter is that of the variable.
- A unit that assigns a reference that is not local to a loop is not
  converted (the assignment of the heap, C4).
- `bind_aliased_params` does one step of Simplify's analysis for each
  round, for a recursive continuation alone in its `Let_cont`. A value
  that goes round through several continuations defined together is
  not found.
- `follow_aliases_in_loops` is written with the first
  `follow_aliases` and its list of what is known. Its successor, on
  the aliases of the typing environment, is T3's; the pass moves to it
  when the validator does.
- Kinds. The machine tests the kind of a value at a call, at a `Let`
  and where a handler is applied. `drop_unused_params` removes the
  kind of a removed parameter from the metadata of its handler; the
  loop that `looped_code` makes has the kinds of the parameters of
  the code, which must be distinct (`loopable`); `shortcut_switches`
  asks that a handler of no parameters lists no kinds; the `Let`s of
  `bind_aliased_params` have the kinds that the handler gives its
  parameters. `inline_tail_conts` is an equivalence only where the
  handler gives one kind for each parameter, which is why
  `inline_tail_conts_kinded` asks it.
- A local function with value slots that Simplify makes a loop and
  inlines at its one call is not validated with the tool's third
  guess either. (Found by T2.)

## Decided overnight

1. **The name of the loop is given by the caller of `loopify_unit`.**
   Options: take it from `FlambdaNameAbstractionSpec.fresh_name`, over
   the continuations of the body; or a name given by the caller and
   checked. Chosen: given and checked
   (`FlambdaLoopifySpec.loopable` asks that it does not occur in the
   body); code that fails the check is left as it is, so a bad name
   costs a validation and never soundness. To change: compute the
   name with `fresh_name` where the caller gives it now; the proof
   uses only what `loopable` checks.
2. **`sink_let_conts` moves a `Let_cont` only where the inlining then
   happens, and never into a recursive `Let_cont`.** Options: move it
   as far as it will go (an equivalence all the same); or follow
   Simplify, which inlines a continuation used once but not from
   inside a loop. Chosen: follow Simplify, since the comparison is of
   the bindings in their order. To change: the conditions of
   `sink_into_handler`; the proof of the move itself does not depend
   on them.
3. **`loopify_unit` asks nothing of the call kind, the arity or the
   code id of a self tail call**, as Simplify asks nothing: a call of
   `my_closure` that names other code, or has the wrong number of
   arguments, is undefined in the model, so the jump refines it. It
   does not go into the handlers of recursive continuations or into
   code to find `Let_static`s: none there is executed.
4. **Regions.** Simplify's loopify does not compare the region of the
   call with that of the function, and the model has no regions. The
   statement says nothing about them; a `CR mshinwell` at
   `FlambdaLoopifySpec.is_self_tail_call` records it. A trial from
   source found no self tail call whose region is not the function's
   own (discrepancy V4-1).

5. **Where the proofs of renaming are.** In N3's Proofs file, as one
   section, and not in a file of this stream: that file's lemmas on
   the normal forms need them, and they need its lemmas. To change:
   move the section once the statements are in N3's `Spec`.
6. **Exception handlers.** The machine does not execute trap actions
   and does not read the flag, so a shortcut of an exception handler,
   or removing an unused exception parameter, would be right in the
   model. The passes follow Simplify, which does neither (discrepancy
   V4-3). To change: two tests, in `shortcut_switch` and
   `used_params`.

7. **One step of the analysis of aliases for each round.** Simplify
   finds what a parameter is always given by a fixpoint over the graph
   of all continuations (`simplify/flow/dominator_graph.ml`). Options:
   model that analysis; or look at the applications of one
   continuation and let the rounds of the validation repeat it.
   Chosen: the second (`FlambdaParamAliasesSpec.aliases_of`), which is
   a fact about one `Let_cont` and has a proof by simulation. It finds
   what the front end makes for mutable variables in `while` and `for`
   loops. To change: a new analysis; `bind_aliases` and the proof of
   the `Let`s stay.
8. **The masks of a loop are computed on the handler as it stands.**
   `drop_unused_params` removes from a recursive continuation the
   parameters not free in its handler before anything is removed from
   it, so a parameter that the handler only passes on is kept.
   Options: a fixpoint inside the pass; or what stands, repeated by
   the rounds, with `bind_aliased_params` hiding a parameter first.
   Chosen: the second; the fixpoint is the data flow analysis that is
   not done (above). Later the same night: decision 14.
9. **`forward_applications` follows Simplify at a goto arm of a
   `Switch`**: it takes the `Apply_cont` of the handler with its trap
   action (`simplify_switch_expr.ml`, `rebuild_arm`). The header of
   `FlambdaShortcutSpec.v` said that Simplify makes no shortcut there;
   that was wrong for a goto arm and is corrected. `shortcut_switches`
   itself is left stricter than Simplify, as reviewed: it only makes
   fewer validations, and `forward_applications` runs after it. To
   change: drop the test of the trap action in `shortcut_switch`, with
   the guard on the names of the trap action that `forwarding` has.
10. **The names of a trap action are guarded against capture** in
   `FlambdaForwardSpec.forwarding`, though the machine of
   `FlambdaSemanticsSpec.v` does not read trap actions and the
   refinement holds without: the machine with a stack
   (`FlambdaTrapSpec.v`) does read them. N2 asked for it.
11. **The work on parameters is a phase before the calls are
   inlined.** Put in the rounds that inline calls, it stopped
   `raise_in_call.fl` from validating: the validation inlines a call
   that Simplify left, on both sides; the continuation the call
   returned to becomes one that the inlined body applies; the term
   before then lost its parameter and had its application forwarded,
   and the term after, which has only equivalences, kept both.
   Options: the same rewrites for the term after, as equivalences
   (removing a parameter is one only where every argument is sure to
   have a value of its kind; not written); `validate_unit` tries the
   normal form with and without the new rewrites; or the new rewrites
   run first, on the term as Simplify saw it, and the rounds that
   inline calls stay as they were. Chosen: the last. What it costs: a
   parameter that is always given the same thing only once a call is
   inlined is not removed. To change: move the three rewrites into
   `normalise_expr_before` when the term after has their
   equivalents, or when the validation is told which calls Simplify
   inlined (V2's list of calls kept).
12. **In the first phase the handlers of loops come before what is
   around them.** `forward_applications` goes into the handler of a
   loop, for the continuations bound outside it. Run before the round
   for the handler, it forwarded, inside the loop, an application to a
   continuation that `sink_let_conts` and `inline_tail_conts` would
   have inlined there, as Simplify does (it makes no shortcut to a
   continuation that it inlines); three of the units with an `if` or a
   loop in a loop then failed. Options: `forward_applications` not
   going into loops (it would then miss the continuations bound
   outside, such as the raise of a failed test); or the handlers
   first. Chosen: the handlers first, in the first phase only; the
   second phase keeps its order.
13. **A self tail call is a full call** once the machine has partial
   applications and over-applications (C3's epoch): a call of
   `my_closure` with fewer or more arguments is then not undefined,
   and the jump would not refine it. `is_self_tail_call` asks it
   there, as Simplify decides loopify for full calls only; the repair
   of the proofs is written and goes with that epoch. Decision 3
   above is about the machine before it.

14. **The masks of a loop are searched for, and only their being
   stable is used.** `drop_dead_params` needs, for a loop, masks under
   which the parameters removed are not free in the handlers rewritten
   under those masks. Options: Simplify's analysis transcribed (a
   graph of dependencies over the whole body of a function, and its
   proof); or a search inside the pass, from the masks that remove
   everything, keeping at each round what the rewritten handlers use,
   until a round changes nothing. Chosen: the search. The statement
   does not depend on it: the proof uses only that one more round
   leaves the answer as it is, which the function tests, and keeps
   every parameter of the loop if the search runs out of rounds (it
   cannot: each round keeps more). It finds the same parameters as
   Simplify on the units tried; where Simplify's analysis knows more
   (a parameter needed only by a primitive it deletes for another
   reason) the unit is not validated, which is the safe side. A second
   choice, in the same pass: it removes every unused `Let` that
   `named_may_go` lets go, everywhere and not only in loops, as
   `remove_unused_lets` already does in the same round. To change:
   another way of finding the masks changes one function and no
   proof but its last lemma.

15. **`forward_applications` follows Simplify's order of work, not
   only its rule.** Simplify records a shortcut for a handler that is
   one `Apply_cont` when it rebuilds it, after its analysis of
   aliases has put `Let`s at its head. The validation goes round many
   times, so a handler that Simplify saw with such a `Let` is one
   `Apply_cont` a round later, and was forwarded where Simplify had
   not. Options: an equivalence on the side after that forwards too
   (it must know the kinds of the arguments, since the machine tests
   kinds where a handler is applied: a pass with what is in scope, and
   a proof in both directions); or the test above, which says when
   Simplify's analysis rebinds a parameter. Chosen: the test. It is a
   guess at what Simplify did, and where it is wrong a unit is not
   validated; it cannot make a wrong unit validate. To change: the
   equivalence, which would also serve where the validation inlines a
   call that Simplify left.
