# V4: loops

Branch `flambda2-rocq/V4-loops`. The aim: units made from OCaml sources
that have a loop (a `while`, a `for`, a tail-recursive function) are
validated by `FlambdaValidateSpec.validate_unit`, and not only found
the same on one run.

## Done

Each is a function on terms with its statement in a `Spec` file and the
proof in the `Proofs` file beside it; no axiom, nothing admitted.

- **`rewrite_rec_handlers`** (`FlambdaRecHandlersSpec.v`): a rewrite
  that refines, or is an equivalence, under all environments may be
  applied to the handlers of recursive continuations. It is what lets
  the existing passes into a loop; they need no change.
- **`sink_let_conts`** (`FlambdaSinkContsSpec.v`): an equivalence that
  moves a non-recursive `Let_cont` into the handler that alone uses
  it, where `inline_tail_conts` then inlines it.
  `FlambdaContAlikeProofs.v` is the tool of proof: continuations that
  apply alike may stand for each other.
- **`drop_unused_params`** (`FlambdaUnusedParamsSpec.v`): a refinement
  that removes the parameters of a continuation that its handler does
  not use, at the binder and at every application in scope. For a
  recursive continuation it is the parameters that are not free in the
  handler as it stands.
- **`shortcut_switches`** (`FlambdaShortcutSpec.v`): an equivalence; an
  arm of a `Switch` that applies a continuation whose handler only
  applies another applies that other.
- **`follow_aliases_in_loops`** (`FlambdaLoopAliasesSpec.v`): an
  equivalence; inside the handlers of recursive continuations, a
  variable bound outside to another name or to a constant is replaced
  by it.
- **`loopify_unit`** (`FlambdaLoopifySpec.v`): a refinement; the self
  tail calls of the code named become jumps to a recursive
  continuation around its body, as Simplify's loopify makes them. The
  proof is a simulation in which a step of the first run is answered
  by one or more of the second (`FlambdaSimulationProofs.v`).
- **The wiring** in `FlambdaValidateSpec.v`: the passes above are in
  the normal form of the term before, in the order the file gives,
  and the normal form is applied in the handlers of recursive
  continuations too (`normalise_code_before`); the term after has its
  aliases followed and its constants folded there as well.
  `validate_unit_sound` is proved as before, its statement unchanged.

- **Renaming.** The passes commute with renaming:
  `rewrite_rec_handlers f` for an `f` that does, `sink_let_conts`,
  `shortcut_switches`, `drop_params` with the keys of its masks
  renamed, `follow_aliases_in_loops` with its typing environment
  renamed, `loopify_unit` with the continuations of its list renamed.
  The proofs are a section of `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`
  (N3's file, by agreement), where the normalisations of the
  validation use them; they are lemmas there, in the form of that
  file's `Spec`, until N3 states them.
- **Exceptions.** Since terms have trap actions, extra arguments of
  exception continuations and a flag on exception handlers:
  - `loopify`: a self tail call has no extra arguments for its
    exception continuation (Simplify's third test, which the model
    needs too); the loop is not an exception handler and the jumps
    have no trap action;
  - `drop_unused_params`: the trap action of an application stays;
    the first parameter of an exception handler is kept whether used
    or not; a handler that loses parameters stays an exception
    handler if it was one;
  - `shortcut_switches`: no shortcut of an exception handler, nor
    through an `Apply_cont` with a trap action; an arm with a trap
    action stays;
  - `sink_let_conts`: a continuation that a trap action names is
    free where the trap action is.

`ocaml/while_loop.ml` of `middle_end/flambda2/tests/semantics/` goes
from "not validated" to "validated"; no other example changes.

### Second series

- **`remove_unused_defined_lets_in_loops`** (`FlambdaLoopLetsSpec.v`):
  an equivalence; `remove_unused_defined_lets` made to go into the
  handlers of recursive continuations, with the parameters and the
  variables in scope at the `Let_cont` in scope. For the term after.
- **`bind_aliased_params`** (`FlambdaParamAliasesSpec.v`): a
  refinement; a parameter that every application gives the same
  constant, symbol or variable is bound to it by a `Let` at the start
  of the handler, as Simplify's `lets_to_introduce`; for a recursive
  continuation alone in its `Let_cont` the applications inside the
  handler may also pass the parameter itself. `drop_unused_params`
  then removes the parameter. Together they remove from a loop the
  mutable variables that its body does not assign.
- **`forward_applications`** (`FlambdaForwardSpec.v`): a refinement; an
  application of a non-recursive continuation whose handler is one
  `Apply_cont` becomes that `Apply_cont`, the arguments put for the
  parameters, wherever it is (Simplify's `Continuation_shortcut`); a
  goto arm of a `Switch` takes it with its trap action
  (`rebuild_arm`). `FlambdaRoundsProofs.v` is the form of simulation
  it needs: one or more steps of each run to a round.
- **`inline_tail_conts_kinded`** (`FlambdaInlineTailSpec.v`): an
  equivalence, for whoever needs it on the term after;
  `inline_tail_conts` of `FlambdaNormaliseSpec.v` where the handler
  gives one kind for each parameter. Not used by `validate_unit` yet.
- **The wiring.** The term before is normalised in two phases
  (`normalise_before`). The first (`normalise_params_round`) is the
  round for an expression with `forward_applications` after
  `shortcut_switches`, then `remove_unused_conts`, and
  `bind_aliased_params` before `drop_unused_params`; it inlines no
  call, and it normalises the handlers of loops before what is around
  them (`normalise_code_params`). The second is the rounds as they
  were. Term after:
  `remove_unused_defined_lets_in_loops` where
  `remove_unused_defined_lets` was. `validate_unit_sound` is proved as
  before, its statement unchanged.
- **Renaming.** Lemmas for the three new passes in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v`, and the lemma for
  `drop_params` proved again.

Measured with `flsemantics.exe` on units made from OCaml sources, not
in the tree (V2 adds such units to `ocaml/`): validated now, and not
before the series, are a `while` in a `while`, an `if` inside a
`while`, two `while`s in sequence, a `while` followed by a use of a
constant, the sum of the first integers, a countdown, and `for` loops
(a sum, two references, a `for` in a `for`, an `if` inside, `downto`).
No expected file of the tree changes.

## Tried, not merged

On a trial branch with V3's lifting and V2's list of calls that are
not inlined, before terms carried kinds, `validate_unit` was given
`loopify_unit G before` in place of the term before, and the test tool
gave the code that has the loopify attribute. Then `tail_loop.ml` and,
of V2's examples, `list_length.ml` and `list_rev.ml` are validated (a
self tail call made a jump), and so are `factorial.ml` and
`list_sum.ml` (no loop: the lifting, the calls kept, and
`sink_let_conts`). `mutual_recursion.ml` is not: a projection of a
function slot from a static set is not resolved, and the two pieces of
code of the group are bound in the other order; V2 and V3 have both.
Without the list of calls kept, the three tail-recursive units fail on
one thing: the call is inlined on both sides, and the term after is
left with code that nothing uses.

## Not done

- `loopify_unit` is not called by `validate_unit` yet: that function
  takes the lifting of V3 first, and V3 adds the argument (the code to
  loopify, each with a name for its loop) when this is merged. The
  test tool must then say which code has the loopify attribute.
- `drop_unused_params` leaves a parameter of a recursive
  continuation that the handler uses only to pass it round, and those
  of a continuation that an `Apply` returns to. Simplify removes the
  first by its data flow analysis ([decide_param_usage_recursive]); a
  unit that needs it (a counter that a loop increments and nothing
  reads) is not validated. What it would take: remove the argument at
  the back edge when the parameter is used nowhere else, which makes
  the `Let` of the increment unused, and repeat.
- A loop parameter whose representation Simplify changes is not
  validated: a boolean reference that a `while` tests is kept untagged
  by Simplify. Unboxing of continuation parameters is S6, with T6.
- `bind_aliased_params` does one step of Simplify's analysis for each
  round, for a recursive continuation alone in its `Let_cont`. A value
  that goes round through several continuations defined together is
  not found.
- `follow_aliases_in_loops` is written with the first
  `follow_aliases` and its list of what is known. Its successor, on
  the aliases of the typing environment, is T3's; the pass moves to it
  when the validator does.
- `inline_tail_conts_kinded` is an equivalence
  (`FlambdaInlineTailSpec.v`) but the term after does not use it yet.
  Where the validator inlines a call in the term after, the
  continuation the call returned to is left applied once, and only the
  term before has it inlined. V2 wires it when a unit needs it.
- Kinds. The machine tests the kind of a value at a call, at a `Let`
  and where a handler is applied. `drop_unused_params` removes the
  kind of a removed parameter from the metadata of its handler; the
  loop that `looped_code` makes has the kinds of the parameters of
  the code, which must be distinct (`loopable`); `shortcut_switches`
  asks that a handler of no parameters lists no kinds; the `Let`s of
  `bind_aliased_params` have the kinds that the handler gives its
  parameters. `inline_tail_conts` is an equivalence only where the
  handler gives one kind for each parameter, which is why
  `inline_tail_conts_kinded` asks it.

## Decided overnight

1. **The name of the loop is given by the caller of `loopify_unit`.**
   Options: take it from `FlambdaNameAbstractionSpec.fresh_name`, over
   the continuations of the body; or a name given by the caller and
   checked. Chosen: given and checked
   (`FlambdaLoopifySpec.loopable` asks that it does not occur in the
   body); code that fails the check is left as it is, so a bad name
   costs a validation and never soundness. To change: compute the
   name with `fresh_name` where the caller gives it now; the proof
   uses only what `loopable` checks.
2. **`sink_let_conts` moves a `Let_cont` only where the inlining then
   happens, and never into a recursive `Let_cont`.** Options: move it
   as far as it will go (an equivalence all the same); or follow
   Simplify, which inlines a continuation used once but not from
   inside a loop. Chosen: follow Simplify, since the comparison is of
   the bindings in their order. To change: the conditions of
   `sink_into_handler`; the proof of the move itself does not depend
   on them.
3. **`loopify_unit` asks nothing of the call kind, the arity or the
   code id of a self tail call**, as Simplify asks nothing: a call of
   `my_closure` that names other code, or has the wrong number of
   arguments, is undefined in the model, so the jump refines it. It
   does not go into the handlers of recursive continuations or into
   code to find `Let_static`s: none there is executed.
4. **Regions.** Simplify's loopify does not compare the region of the
   call with that of the function, and the model has no regions. The
   statement says nothing about them; a `CR mshinwell` at
   `FlambdaLoopifySpec.is_self_tail_call` records it. A trial from
   source found no self tail call whose region is not the function's
   own (discrepancy V4-1).

5. **Where the proofs of renaming are.** In N3's Proofs file, as one
   section, and not in a file of this stream: that file's lemmas on
   the normal forms need them, and they need its lemmas. To change:
   move the section once the statements are in N3's `Spec`.
6. **Exception handlers.** The machine does not execute trap actions
   and does not read the flag, so a shortcut of an exception handler,
   or removing an unused exception parameter, would be right in the
   model. The passes follow Simplify, which does neither (discrepancy
   V4-3). To change: two tests, in `shortcut_switch` and
   `used_params`.

7. **One step of the analysis of aliases for each round.** Simplify
   finds what a parameter is always given by a fixpoint over the graph
   of all continuations (`simplify/flow/dominator_graph.ml`). Options:
   model that analysis; or look at the applications of one
   continuation and let the rounds of the validation repeat it.
   Chosen: the second (`FlambdaParamAliasesSpec.aliases_of`), which is
   a fact about one `Let_cont` and has a proof by simulation. It finds
   what the front end makes for mutable variables in `while` and `for`
   loops. To change: a new analysis; `bind_aliases` and the proof of
   the `Let`s stay.
8. **The masks of a loop are computed on the handler as it stands.**
   `drop_unused_params` removes from a recursive continuation the
   parameters not free in its handler before anything is removed from
   it, so a parameter that the handler only passes on is kept.
   Options: a fixpoint inside the pass; or what stands, repeated by
   the rounds, with `bind_aliased_params` hiding a parameter first.
   Chosen: the second; the fixpoint is the data flow analysis that is
   not done (above).
9. **`forward_applications` follows Simplify at a goto arm of a
   `Switch`**: it takes the `Apply_cont` of the handler with its trap
   action (`simplify_switch_expr.ml`, `rebuild_arm`). The header of
   `FlambdaShortcutSpec.v` said that Simplify makes no shortcut there;
   that was wrong for a goto arm and is corrected. `shortcut_switches`
   itself is left stricter than Simplify, as reviewed: it only makes
   fewer validations, and `forward_applications` runs after it. To
   change: drop the test of the trap action in `shortcut_switch`, with
   the guard on the names of the trap action that `forwarding` has.
10. **The names of a trap action are guarded against capture** in
   `FlambdaForwardSpec.forwarding`, though the machine of
   `FlambdaSemanticsSpec.v` does not read trap actions and the
   refinement holds without: the machine with a stack
   (`FlambdaTrapSpec.v`) does read them. N2 asked for it.
11. **The work on parameters is a phase before the calls are
   inlined.** Put in the rounds that inline calls, it stopped
   `raise_in_call.fl` from validating: the validation inlines a call
   that Simplify left, on both sides; the continuation the call
   returned to becomes one that the inlined body applies; the term
   before then lost its parameter and had its application forwarded,
   and the term after, which has only equivalences, kept both.
   Options: the same rewrites for the term after, as equivalences
   (removing a parameter is one only where every argument is sure to
   have a value of its kind; not written); `validate_unit` tries the
   normal form with and without the new rewrites; or the new rewrites
   run first, on the term as Simplify saw it, and the rounds that
   inline calls stay as they were. Chosen: the last. What it costs: a
   parameter that is always given the same thing only once a call is
   inlined is not removed. To change: move the three rewrites into
   `normalise_expr_before` when the term after has their
   equivalents, or when the validation is told which calls Simplify
   inlined (V2's list of calls kept).
12. **In the first phase the handlers of loops come before what is
   around them.** `forward_applications` goes into the handler of a
   loop, for the continuations bound outside it. Run before the round
   for the handler, it forwarded, inside the loop, an application to a
   continuation that `sink_let_conts` and `inline_tail_conts` would
   have inlined there, as Simplify does (it makes no shortcut to a
   continuation that it inlines); three of the units with an `if` or a
   loop in a loop then failed. Options: `forward_applications` not
   going into loops (it would then miss the continuations bound
   outside, such as the raise of a failed test); or the handlers
   first. Chosen: the handlers first, in the first phase only; the
   second phase keeps its order.
13. **A self tail call is a full call** once the machine has partial
   applications and over-applications (C3's epoch): a call of
   `my_closure` with fewer or more arguments is then not undefined,
   and the jump would not refine it. `is_self_tail_call` asks it
   there, as Simplify decides loopify for full calls only; the repair
   of the proofs is written and goes with that epoch. Decision 3
   above is about the machine before it.
