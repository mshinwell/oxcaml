## The machine with a stack of handlers

Merged: two commits on top of the series for the epoch, in
`FlambdaTrapSpec.v` and `FlambdaTrapProofs.v` only (and a sentence in
two headers). Done:

- A machine that goes by a stack of handlers and of calls
  (`stack_step`): a `Push` installs what its continuation stands for, a
  `Pop` removes the entry on top, a raise goes to the entry on top, a
  call puts the caller's return continuation and the extra arguments on
  the stack and runs the body of the code with its two continuations
  standing for nothing, so that leaving by one is a return and leaving
  by the other a raise in the caller. Everything else is `step`.
- Since C3's epoch (partial applications and over-applications), in
  the commit of the calls: a stack entry `Call_then`, for a call with
  more arguments than the code has parameters. The stack keeps the
  arguments that remain; when the body returns, its result is called
  with them under the stack as it was at the call, and a raise in
  either call goes to the caller's handler with the extra arguments
  of the whole call. A partial application returns its value and
  leaves the stack as it is. The statements are as they were and all
  proved; R1 read the change to `FlambdaTrapSpec.v`. What it models
  is the backend's path for an indirect call of unknown arity (one
  generic application); a direct call or a call of known arity that
  is not full is C3-9, and nothing is said of the compiled program
  there.
- `stack_agrees_statement`, proved: a unit with
  `binders_distinct e = true` and `traps_well_formed unit_exn e = true`,
  run from empty environments, has the same behaviours on the two
  machines. Both premises are conditions of `well_formed_unit`.
- `stack_needs_distinct_binders_statement`, proved: the first premise
  is needed. A unit that passes `traps_well_formed` and calls code
  whose return and exception continuations are one name, the body of
  which raises, leaves with 1 on the machine that goes by names and by
  its exception continuation on the one with the stack.
- `stack_differs_statement`, proved: a term whose binders are distinct
  and where a raise names a handler other than the one installed last
  exits with 2 on the machine that goes by names and with 1 on the one
  with the stack (C5-1).
- The proof is a simulation, one step for one step, with a step index
  for the handlers of recursive continuations. It is written with a
  copy of the check that tests the two conditions on binders where it
  uses them (`full_demands`, in the proofs file); a lemma says that
  the copy and `trap_demands` are one on a term whose binders are
  distinct. Of the additions to the checks, the proof needed the test
  after the passes over a recursive group (decision 8) and the two
  conditions on binders (decision 9). The condition C5-9 was found
  while the machine was designed, and the theorem does not assume the
  check that has it.

What the statements are not:

- They are of two machines of the model. That the code the backend
  makes behaves as the second is not stated and not proved.
- Which `Apply_cont` is a raise is said by the term (`is_raise`), not
  by whether the continuation applied is an exception handler, as
  to_cmm has it (C5-2). Since, in a third commit:
  `raises_as_marked_statement`, proved. `raises_as_marked` is the
  model's account of to_cmm's test (the continuation applied is one of
  the exception handlers in scope: the exception continuation of the
  function or the unit, and the marked handlers bound since), and in a
  unit that passes `unit_exn_handlers_well_formed` the two readings
  take the same `Apply_cont`s for raises; without the check they need
  not (`raises_not_as_marked_statement`). So each of the two checks
  has a theorem. What to_cmm then makes of a raise, a call or a trap
  action is not modelled.
- Before the replay `stack_agrees_statement` had `traps_well_formed`
  alone as its premise, the check then asking the two conditions on
  binders itself. The check as it is now accepts more terms than that
  one did, all with binders that are not distinct; nothing is said of
  the two machines on those but the one example.
- Equivariance, in the same third commit, as agreed with N1: the
  action of renamings on a stack renames the continuation values and
  the two continuations of each call in progress, and a step, a run
  and the behaviours of the machine with the stack commute with it
  (`stack_step_equivariant_statement` and three more, proved from the
  lemmas of `FlambdaEquivarianceProofs.v`). Not stated: that a stack
  has a finite support.

## The demotion of exception handlers

`FlambdaDemoteSpec.v` and `FlambdaDemoteProofs.v`, with
`FlambdaDemoteScopeSpec.v` and its proofs, merged. For V2's units
made from OCaml sources:
Simplify makes the handler of a `try` whose raises it can all see
into an ordinary continuation and removes the trap actions that name
it, and the validator has no rewrite that does the same. Reviewer:
R1.

- `demote_handlers scope e`: at a non-recursive `Let_cont` of `k`
  whose handler is marked, if no call of the body has `k` as its
  exception continuation, `k` is not a name in scope and the body
  does not bind `k` again (`demotable`), the handler loses its mark
  (`clear_exn_handler`) and the body its trap actions that name `k`
  (`strip_traps`). It goes into handlers and code. `scope` is the
  names in scope where the term stands: the return and exception
  continuations of a unit.
- Proved, all nine statements:
  - `demote_handlers_equivalent_statement`: the term and the result
    are equivalent under all environments, on the machine that goes
    by names. That machine executes no trap action and reads no mark,
    so this needs no condition; it is what the validator uses.
  - `demote_handlers_keeps_exn_handlers_statement` and
    `demote_handlers_keeps_traps_statement`: a unit that passes the
    check of exception handlers, or (with distinct binders) the check
    of trap actions, passes it after the rewrite. So the result is a
    unit of which `stack_agrees_statement` speaks.
  - `demote_handlers_stack_statement`: on the machine with a stack of
    handlers, a unit with distinct binders that passes the check of
    trap actions has the same behaviours after the rewrite.
  - `demote_handlers_binders_statement`: `binders_distinct` is kept.
  - `demote_handlers_equivariant_statement`: the rewrite commutes
    with renaming, the names in scope being renamed with the term (the
    form N1 and N3 asked for).
  - `demote_handlers_keeps_scope_statement`
    (`FlambdaDemoteScopeSpec.v`): for every scope the rewrite keeps
    scope (`FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceSpec.keeps_scope`), which the
    validator asks of a rewrite it applies. It is in a file of its
    own because `keeps_scope` is defined after the validator, which
    is to import `FlambdaDemoteSpec.v`. Proved with N2's lemmas
    (`FlambdaScopedProofs.v`).
  - Two examples: `demote_handlers_try_raise_statement` (the `try`
    around a raise as the front end makes it becomes what Simplify
    leaves) and `demotion_under_call_statement` (a `try` around a
    call is left alone; demoted by hand, the unit leaves by its
    exception continuation on the machine with a stack, and no longer
    with 2).
- How the check of trap actions is kept: the demands of the result
  are those of the term with `k` taken off every stack
  (`trap_demands_demote` in the proofs, by a relation between demands
  under a map of stacks that the passes over a recursive group
  respect).
- Not done:
  - The argument of a raise that nothing reads, which Simplify
    replaces (`patch_unused_exn_bucket`), is another rewrite.
  - The statement about the stack is from the empty static
    environment, as `stack_agrees_statement` is.
- Asynchronous exceptions were looked at for this (a handler demoted
  where the body has no call must not be the target of an exception
  that no raise of the term raises). `stdlib/sys.mli` documents that
  an asynchronous `Break` or `Stack_overflow` is raised from the call
  site of `Sys.with_async_exns`, or goes to the toplevel handler of
  uncaught exceptions. A `try` around that call may catch it, and the
  rewrite leaves such a `try` alone, its body having a call whose
  exception continuation is the handler. No discrepancy for those
  two; other events of the runtime are outside the model.

## Decided overnight

The human is away and has asked that work go on: where a decision is
his, the consideration is written down, a sensible choice is taken,
and he reviews it in the morning. These are the four decisions of the
request, section 8. Each can be overruled; the cost says what that
would undo. The CRs stay where they are.

1. **Is the stack of exception handlers part of the machine?**
   Options: (a) no: the machine goes by the names, a raise and the
   exception of a call go to the continuation the term names, and the
   discipline under which the backend's stack agrees is a check of the
   term, with a theorem to follow; (b) yes: the state has the stack,
   and a `Pop`, a raise or a call that does not name the handler on
   top is undefined.
   Choice: (a), as recommended; R1 supports it.
   Why: it is how Simplify reads a term, so its rewrites of exceptions
   can be stated without a premise; it adds no field to the state; and
   a stack in the machine needs a notion of which handler is which,
   which continuation values do not have.
   Cost of changing: the state gains a field, to be swept through every
   statement that builds a state, with the heap's; each mismatch with
   the stack becomes an undefined case with its CR; the leaf theorem
   (the two machines agree on well-formed units) becomes the proof that
   a well-formed unit never meets those cases. The terms, the
   conversion and the check of the term stay as they are.
2. **Are backtraces observed?**
   Options: (a) no: the kind of a raise is in the term, the validator
   compares it, and it changes nothing in a run; (b) yes, in some
   form.
   Choice: (a), as recommended; R1 supports it.
   Why: nothing else in the model observes more than the values at an
   exit. The model then allows any raise to become a jump, where
   Simplify does so only for a raise without a trace or when debugging
   information is off (C5-4).
   Cost of changing: an observation of the trace in `Exit` or in the
   state, and a premise on the raise kind in the statement that
   demotes a handler. Nothing written tonight depends on the choice
   except that statement, which is not written yet.
3. **Is the discipline a condition of well-formedness, though the
   compiler checks less?**
   Options: (a) adopt the two checks (`traps_well_formed`,
   `exn_handlers_well_formed` with `ways_out_well_formed`), with the
   comment that D32 asks for; (b) only what the backend checks: one
   stack for each continuation, and no `Pop` on an empty stack.
   Choice: (a), as recommended. A unit of the compiler that fails is
   then a finding.
   Why: D32; the term of C5-1, on which Simplify and the backend
   disagree, passes (b).
   Cost of changing: two conjuncts go, or become one weaker one; the
   leaf theorem then has the checks as its own premise.
4. **Must the first parameter of an exception handler be declared of
   kind `Value`?** (After C2.)
   Options: (a) ask for it; (b) do not.
   Choice: (b), though (a) is what I recommend: R1's reading is that
   D32, which excludes kinds, does not authorise it, so it waits for a
   ruling. The compiler does not test it (C5-7).
   Cost of changing: one line of the check and its comment.

For the series of the epoch, also decided while the human is away:

5. **What the series was built on.** C2's series as it stood, before
   its rebase over N1's change, so as not to wait; it has since been
   replayed on the integration branch.
6. **A handler not marked as an exception handler is not used as
   one.** New with the flag; nothing in the compiler checks it
   (C5-8). Options: (a) ask for it; (b) ask only what the prototype
   did, of handlers that are used as exception handlers. Choice: (a):
   with the flag in the term, (b) would let the flag and the uses
   disagree, and `handler_at_toplevel` reads the flag. Cost of
   changing: one branch of `exn_handlers_well_formed`, and
   `handler_at_toplevel` goes back to reading the uses.
7. **The conjuncts are in the series**, as a commit of their own that
   C0 can apply or replace, rather than left for C0 to write.
8. **Two things added to the checks** while the machine with a stack
   of handlers was designed:
   - the exception continuation of code, or of the unit, is named by
     calls and by the trap actions of raises only (to_cmm stops on any
     other trap action that names it; C5-9);
   - after its passes over a recursive group, the check tests that
     every handler that is entered has been checked.
   Cost of changing: each is one line of a check.
9. **The checks of trap actions are for a term whose binders are
   distinct.** For the stack to mean what it says, the return and
   exception continuations of code must be two names and the
   continuations of a recursive `Let_cont` distinct. Options: (a) the
   check of trap actions asks them itself, and stands alone; (b) it
   leaves them to `binders_distinct` (row 5), which a well-formed unit
   is asked first. Choice: (b), as C0 asked, so that the one
   definition asks each condition once. `traps_well_formed` alone then
   says less than it did: it accepts code of one continuation that
   raises, and a recursive group that binds a name twice, and what it
   says of them is not what the machine does (two pairs of examples
   in `FlambdaTrapProofs.v`). Any statement about it takes
   `binders_distinct` too. Cost of changing: two lines of the check.

For the demotion of exception handlers, decided while the human is
away:

14. **The rewrite takes the names in scope.** Options: (a)
    `demote_handlers e`, with the theorems about the two checks under
    a premise that no handler demoted has the name of a continuation
    in scope; (b) `demote_handlers scope e`, which leaves such a
    handler alone. Choice: (b). The checks go by names and do not
    follow rebinding, so a handler demoted under a name that is also
    the exception continuation of the unit would break them; with the
    scope the theorems need only that the exception continuation of
    the unit is in the scope given. The compiler's terms bind each
    name once and lose nothing. Cost of changing: one argument and
    one conjunct of `demotable`.
15. **The rewrite does not look at the kind of a raise.** With
    debugging information Simplify keeps a handler that is reached by
    a raise that records a backtrace; the machines observe no
    backtrace (decision 2; C5-4), and the rewrite demotes. On a unit
    compiled so, that can leave the two terms different in structure.
    Nothing is claimed of backtraces or of the native code of a
    handler that is demoted. Cost of changing: one more disjunct in
    `demotable`, and nothing in the proofs of the checks.
16. **The statements about the discipline take `binders_distinct`**,
    as every statement about `traps_well_formed` does (decision 9).

17. **The validator demotes after its two phases, not in a round.**
    Options: (a) at the start of each round of the term before; (b)
    once, after the two phases, followed by the rounds again. A
    round first inlines a continuation that is applied once and then
    takes an arm of a `Switch` to where its continuation goes; with
    (a), a handler demoted before the raises to it have been taken to
    the arms is inlined in the continuation that raises, and is then
    bound elsewhere than in the term after. Simplify does not inline
    an application with a trap action; it takes the arm to the
    handler and leaves the handler where it is bound, which is what
    the rounds do while the handler has its mark. Measured with
    decision 19 in place: (a) 4 of the 13 units with exceptions and
    `try_raise.fl`; (b) 12 of the 13 and `try_raise.fl`; no verdict is
    lost either way. Choice: (b). Cost of changing: where
    `demote_and_tidy` is called, and the lemmas that compose it.
