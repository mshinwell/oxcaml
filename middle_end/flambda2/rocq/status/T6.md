## Decided overnight

Each is for the human to confirm or overrule.

1. **The base.** The branch is cut over T1's branch, not over the
   integration branch. Why: the statements need T1's `relation_holds`
   as amended (`Is_int` and `Get_tag` hold of no poison), T1's
   accessors of the grammar and `type_well_formed`, none of which is
   merged. Cost of changing: a rebase.
2. **T4's `expand_head` is a stand-in.** Options: wait for T4's merge;
   cut the branch over T4's (which is not over the present integration
   branch); copy T4's definitions and take the soundness as a premise.
   Chosen: the copy, in a file of its own
   (`FlambdaProversStandIn.v`), with the premise
   `expand_head_sound_assumed` in each statement. Why: T6 does not
   wait, the provers run and extract, and the premise is T4's proved
   statement. Cost of changing: delete the file, change the imports,
   drop the premise from each statement.
3. **A stop of the compiler is `None`.** A prover answers an `option`;
   nothing is stated of `None`. As T4's `expand_head`.
4. **`wrong_kind`** answers its default (`Unknown` for a proof,
   `Invalid` for a meet).
5. **Statements are for strict satisfaction first**, with a separate
   statement of what a prover answers on a value that is poison
   (`prover_silent_on_poison`). The form for the weaker notion (names
   that hold poison exempt) comes after, with the premise that the
   names expanded hold no poison. One case is known to be wrong there
   (the `Is_null` relation on a name that holds poison and has the type
   of `Null`); it is a discrepancy entry, not demonstrated.
6. **Allocation modes.** The provers that only answer an allocation
   mode are transcribed and nothing is stated of them; where an answer
   carries one beside something else, the statement is of the rest.
7. **No machine width.** `Target_ocaml_int.bool` is 1 or 0; a tag is
   its number. An answer that is a set is a list, compared as a set.
