# T6, the provers and reification: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/T6-provers`, over the integration branch (after
T1's third request, which has the functions through which the provers
read a type, and the third epoch, of kinds and arities). The guides
are
`middle_end/flambda2/types/provers.ml` and
`middle_end/flambda2/types/reify.ml`. Reviewers: R3, then R4 from the
provers of allocation modes on.

The plan, the table of the provers and the shape of the statements are
`middle_end/flambda2/rocq/requests/T6-provers.md`.

Files, all in `middle_end/flambda2/types/rocq/`:

- `FlambdaProvers.v`: the provers, in a module `Provers`, as the OCaml
  has them. Definitions only; extracts (checked on the side, not in
  `FlambdaExtract.v`).
- `FlambdaProversSpec.v`: the two forms of statement, what each answer
  says, the domains, the statements. Its header says what is and is
  not claimed.
- `FlambdaProversProofs.v`: the proofs.
- `FlambdaProversExamplesSpec.v` and `FlambdaProversExamplesProofs.v`:
  what the provers compute on small types, and an environment that
  shows why the statements are for strict satisfaction.
- `FlambdaReify.v`: reification, in a module `Reify`, as `reify.ml`
  has it, without arrays. `FlambdaReifySpec.v`: what an answer says of
  a value, the statement, examples. `FlambdaReifyProofs.v`: the
  proofs.
- `FlambdaProversRenameSpec.v` and `FlambdaProversRenameProofs.v`:
  the provers and reification commute with renaming.
- `FlambdaProversPoisonSpec.v` and `FlambdaProversPoisonProofs.v`:
  the provers where the names that hold poison are exempt.

No axiom, no `Admitted`.

T6 also has S6, the unboxing of continuation parameters on the
validator's side: the section "S6" at the end of this file.

## What the statements take as premises

- Strict satisfaction of the environment
  (`typing_env_satisfied_strictly`): all of those of
  `FlambdaProversSpec.v` and of `FlambdaReifySpec.v`. Those of
  `FlambdaProversPoisonSpec.v` take `typing_env_satisfied`, which
  exempts the names that hold poison, and that the value is not
  poison.
- No statement assumes the soundness of `expand_head` any more: the
  provers call `expand_head` of `FlambdaExpandHead.v`, and the proofs
  use `expand_head_sound` of `FlambdaExpandHeadProofs.v`. (Until the
  meet's files were merged, each statement had it as a premise,
  `expand_head_sound_assumed`, over a copy of the definitions.)
- `well_formed_input` (the type asked about and the types of the
  environment are well formed: T1's `type_well_formed` and T2's
  `types_well_formed`, which replaced a copy here): the four
  provers that read the numbers of a naked immediate
  (`meet_naked_immediates` and the three of tagged immediates). The
  model's grammar has a type of naked numbers at the kind of the
  immediates that the compiler has not; conditions 2, 3 and 10 of
  well-formedness exclude it, and nothing else of well-formedness is
  used. That expanding a head keeps well-formedness is proved here
  (`expand_head_well_formed`, by the definitions of `expand_head` and
  `Typing_env.find`); it is T4's to take if it wants it.
- `meet_naked_number` is stated at the eleven kinds that are not that
  of the immediates.
- The two provers of tagging take `well_formed_input`, as they read
  the numbers of the immediates of a variant.
  `prove_equals_to_simple_of_kind` needs neither `expand_head` nor
  well-formedness: only that the aliases are respected.
- `prove_variant_like` and `meet_variant_like` take
  `well_formed_input`, for the numbers of the immediates. The other
  provers of blocks do not, but for the second statement of
  `prove_unique_fully_constructed_immutable_heap_block`, which takes
  it for condition 6 (a product has as many types as an exact size).
- `meet_block_field_simple` is stated of the values on which the load
  of the field at the kind asked has a value
  (`block_field_loads`).
- `meet_single_closures_entry` takes `well_formed_input`: it answers
  that a type has no closure where the one case of its closures is
  exact and has no function type for its own slot, which the meaning
  of types does not say (T1-7); condition 8 of well-formedness
  excludes the case. `prove_single_closures_entry` does not need it.
- The two provers of projections are stated of the values on which
  the projection has a value (`function_slot_projects`,
  `value_slot_projects`), from whichever function slot. A value slot
  of the model has no kind, so the kind is an argument of
  `meet_project_value_slot_simple`, and that the contents are of that
  kind is part of its domain (T6-4). That domain is narrower than
  where the projection has a value, the one such domain: the
  statement does not by itself justify the prover's `Invalid` for the
  machine as it is, and the header of the `Spec` file says so. The
  example is a closure whose slot holds a naked float. The answer
  `Known_result` needs no such premise
  (`meet_project_value_slot_simple_known_statement`, on T2's remark):
  the premise on the kind is for `Invalid` alone.
- A code id answered is the very code of the closure: the meaning of
  a function type leaves code age out (a CR at
  `single_closures_entry_holds`).
- `reify` takes `well_formed_input`, for the numbers of immediates.
  Its statement says what the value is, not that a static constant
  made of the answer may take its place (a CR at
  `reification_holds`, decision D6); nothing is stated of the
  conditions under which the compiler lifts a variable
  (`Reify.var_allowed`).
- `prove_physical_equality` takes `well_formed_input` of both types:
  it reads the numbers of the immediates of two variants, and it
  takes two blocks of shapes that are not equal to be two blocks
  (condition 7). Its statement is about values of the model, which
  are not addresses (a CR at the statement, decision D19). What the
  primitive `Phys_equal` computes is tied to the answer where the
  primitive has a value: on two tagged immediates, and on a tagged
  immediate against an allocated value.

Each is for the human to confirm or overrule.

1. **The base.** The branch was cut over T1's branch, for the
   functions through which the provers read a type; they are merged,
   and the branch is over the integration branch.
2. **T4's `expand_head` is a stand-in.** Options: wait for T4's merge;
   cut the branch over T4's (which is not over the present integration
   branch); copy T4's definitions and take the soundness as a premise.
   Chosen: the copy, in a file of its own
   (`FlambdaProversStandIn.v`), with the premise
   `expand_head_sound_assumed` in each statement. Why: T6 does not
   wait, the provers run and extract, and the premise is T4's proved
   statement. Cost of changing: delete the file, change the imports,
   drop the premise from each statement. T4 agreed. Done since: the
   meet's files are merged, and the copy and the premise are gone.
3. **A stop of the compiler is `None`.** A prover answers an `option`;
   nothing is stated of `None`. As T4's `expand_head`.
4. **`wrong_kind`** answers its default (`Unknown` for a proof,
   `Invalid` for a meet).
5. **Statements are for strict satisfaction first**, with a separate
   statement of what a prover answers on a value that is poison. The
   form for the weaker notion (names that hold poison exempt) comes
   after, with the premise that the names expanded hold no poison. One
   case is known to be wrong there (the `Is_null` relation on a name
   that holds poison and has the type of `Null`: T6-1, the same as
   T1-9); not demonstrated.
6. **Well-formedness as a condition on what a prover is asked**
   (`well_formed_input`), where the model's grammar has a type that
   the compiler's has not (T6-2). The alternative, to take the type
   out of the grammar, was T1's to decide: T1 keeps the type, and
   well-formedness excludes it.
7. **Allocation modes.** The provers that only answer an allocation
   mode are to be transcribed and nothing stated of them; where an
   answer carries one beside something else, the statement is of the
   rest.
8. **No machine width.** `Target_ocaml_int.bool` is 1 or 0; a tag is
   its number. An answer that is a set is a list, compared as a set.
9. **The kind of a value slot is an argument** of
   `meet_project_value_slot_simple`, and the domain of its statement
   asks that the contents projected are of that kind (T6-4). Options:
   that; give value slots kinds in the model (C2's or C0's to do);
   leave the comparison of kinds out of the transcription. Why: the
   transcription keeps the compiler's test, and the statement says
   what the test needs. Cost of changing: one argument and one
   conjunct of a domain.
10. **A code id answered is the very code of the closure**, as T1's
    meaning of a function type has it; nothing is stated of a closure
    made with a newer version of the code. It changes with T1's
    meaning when code age comes in.
11. **`prove_physical_equality` is stated of the values of the
    model**: the same value, or not. Options: that; wait for
    `Phys_equal` and state the primitive's answer. Why: the model's
    values are not addresses, the statement is true as it stands, and
    on the tagged immediates, which is where the primitive is to have
    a value first, it is what the primitive computes. Cost of
    changing: one statement more, none less.
12. **Reification is stated as what the value is**, not as the
    soundness of lifting: a block and a symbol bound to an equal
    block are two values of the model (D6), so that a static constant
    may take the place of the value is for the rewrite that lifts.
    The conditions under which the compiler lifts a variable
    (`Reify.var_allowed`) are transcribed and nothing is stated of
    them. The cases of arrays are left out, as the grammar has none.
    Cost of changing: none to what is there; the statement of lifting
    is one more.

13. **Where poison is exempt, the provers are stated of values that
    are not poison.** Options: that; a premise on the names that a
    prover reaches; nothing beyond "where no name holds poison". Why:
    a value that is poison is the value of an exempt name, and of it

    follows an answer owes its own case there, as decision D26 has
    it, and for the primitives that these provers are asked for it is
    that the primitive has no value on poison. For the provers that
    read no relation nothing else is needed. Cost of
    changing: the statements are new, and none of the strict ones
    changed.

Decided overnight (S6):

14. **The side.** Options: an after-side normalisation that puts the
    load of an extra parameter back into the handler (the suggestion
    to test); a before-side rewrite that makes the load at the head of
    the handler a parameter. Chosen: the before side. Why: in the
    common case Simplify has removed the boxed parameter, so the after
    side has nothing to load from and would have to invent the block;
    and the before side, with the passes the validation has, reaches
    Simplify's term. Cost of changing: where the read has a value the
    step is the same rewrite read the other way, and the simulation
    would be reused; in general the rewrite only refines (a static
    block with a field that is not of the kind of values), so the
    other way needs the conditions under which it is an equivalence.
15. **Only what the term shows.** Options: also add a `Let` of the
    load at a use whose argument is not known; only give what is
    already in scope. Chosen: the second, for blocks. Why: Simplify's
    load at such a use names the tag and the size, by the subkind of a
    binder; the model has no subkinds, the term after is then less
    defined than the term before, and no rewrite can be proved. For
    numbers the `Let` at the use is right and is owed (item 2).
16. **The pass goes by what the handler reads first**, not by
    Simplify's decision, and the new parameters are in the order of
    the `Let`s. Why: it needs no hint and makes no name; where it
    differs from Simplify the validation fails and accepts nothing
    more. Cost of changing: a hint of the order, or item 4.
17. **Variants: the constant is given where the read has no value.**
    Options: say that the place of the read is not reached from an
    application that fails the tests on the way (a statement about
    the control flow of the handler, with a simulation of its own);
    copy the handler at each application; give the constant only
    where the term shows that the read has no value on the argument,
    so that the term before is undefined wherever it reads. Chosen:
    the third. Why: it needs no premise, says nothing of poison (so
    nothing of D26), and its proof is the simulation of the first
    pass with the lemmas of contexts; it covers an option, a list and
    a variant with one constructor that holds fields. Cost of
    changing: the first option is needed for a variant with several
    tags and would be added beside this one, not in its place.
18. **Subkinds.** Recommended to the human: binders carry subkinds and
    a value that has not the subkind of its binder makes the binding
    undefined, as the compiler takes it. Not done, not assumed
    anywhere.
19. **`ahead_refines`**, the simulation in which the first run takes
    the more steps, is in `FlambdaUnboxParamsProofs.v`. It could move
    to `FlambdaSimulationProofs.v`, which is C0's.
20. **Under a test, an argument must be certain to be of the kind of
    the read.** The machine tests the kinds of the values that a
    handler is applied to; the term before tests the value only if it
    comes to the read. Options: give whatever the term shows the read
    to give, as at the head of a handler (not a refinement: the term
    after can be undefined where the term before is not); give only
    what is certain to be of the kind. Chosen: the second: a constant
    of the kind, a field of a block that `Make_block` made in scope,
    the number in a box that `Box_number` made in scope, or the
    number that `Tag_immediate` tagged in scope. Cost: a
    field of a static block is not given under a test (item 7 of what
    is owed).
21. **The second pass is a pass of its own**, beside the first, and
    not a change to it. Why: the first has its statements of renaming
    and of scope and is with its reviewer; the second validates the
    two bodies that the first validates and is the one to wire, now
    that it has those statements. It is not the first and more: it
    takes the reads in its own order and stops at the first that it
    cannot make a parameter. Cost: two passes for a while.
22. **The order of the reads.** Options: the order in which the
    handler has them; Simplify's order for a variant. Chosen: the
    second (`next_read`: a tag, a constant constructor, whether the
    value is an immediate, then the loads and the unboxings), and
    within a sort the order of the handler. Why: a variant whose
    constant constructors are told apart is then validated
    (`shape2_heap`); nothing else changes. Cost of changing: one
    definition.
23. **A variant with several tags: the test that an application does
    not come to a read.** The load of a field says nothing of the
    tag, so on a block of another tag it has a value, and Simplify's
    `poison` there rests on the `Switch` on the tag. Options: a
    simulation that follows a run of the handler and shows that the
    continuation of the read is not applied; a test on the term
    (`never_reads`): take the arms that the constants given by the
    application lead to, remove the continuations then unused, and
    compare the handler with the handler without the read. Chosen:
    the second. Why: each of its three steps is an equivalence that
    is already proved or is proved by the lemmas of contexts, so
    there is no new simulation; and it is a decision on the term,
    which the extracted pass runs. Cost: a comparison of two handlers
    at each application; a test of another form than a `Switch` on a
    parameter is not seen.
24. **Where the validation runs the pass.** Options: the first phase
    of the term before; the second; both. Chosen: the first only.
    Why: it is where the other work on parameters is, and V4's
    reason for keeping that work out of the second phase holds of
    this pass too (the term after has only equivalences).
    Measured: with the pass in both phases no verdict changes on the
    units above. Cost of changing: the line; the tactics of the
    proofs and the hint of the law of scope have the pass already.
25. **The proofs of renaming and of scope moved to a file with
    hypotheses.** Options: move the thirteen equations out of
    `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceProofs.v` into a file below both
    (N3's file, a larger change to it); prove the passes' halves
    over hypotheses and give them where they are used. Chosen: the
    second. Why: it touches N3's file by one lemma and one line, and
    no statement moves. Cost: the thirteen statements are written
    twice, as hypotheses and as N3's lemmas; if one of N3's changes,
    the instantiation fails to build and says which.
26. **Symbols in code: by scope, not by the unit.** Options: give the
    pass, everywhere in the unit, every constant block that the unit
    binds (a refinement of a unit run on its own, with an invariant
    on what its symbols are bound to, as
    `FlambdaKnownSymbolsSpec.fold_known_unit` has); give it, in code
    and in a loop, the static blocks of the `Let_static`s in scope.
    Chosen: the second. Why: a `Let_static` binds its group in one
    step and a symbol is bound once, so the symbols in scope are
    bound whenever the code or the loop runs; the rewrite then
    refines under all environments, with the lemmas of contexts and
    no simulation of its own, and it is in the normalisation of the
    term before wherever the validation normalises it, not only where
    something was lifted. Measured:
    both forms validate the same sources. Cost: a symbol bound under
    the code is not known in it; closure conversion binds the
    constants of a unit at its head, so no unit was found that needs
    it.
27. **The facts go into the unboxing where it is in the round**, and
    the round is given to code and to loops by rewrites that take
    what is known; they are not a pass of their own before the
    round. Why: with nothing known the round is the round that was,
    so no unit that has no such symbol can change; measured, none
    does.
28. **The tests that count are those of the box.** Options: every
    constant naked immediate that the application gives (as it was);
    the constants given to the tests of the box read and of the boxes
    above it. Chosen: the second. Why: Simplify gives poison by what
    the argument is, not by the path that the handler takes, and
    three sources of other streams differed by one argument for it.
    Cost: the pass knows the tests that it made of a handler in one
    round; a test that an earlier round made a parameter is not known
    as one. No unit was found that needs it.
29. **A variant is known by its test.** The model has no types, so
    whether an untagging is of the constant constructor of a variant
    or of a number is read off the handler: it is of a variant where
    the handler also reads `Is_int` of the same box. Cost: none
    found; a variant with constant constructors only is a number for
    Simplify too.
30. **The fields from the last, by `first_read`.** Options: sort the
    loads of a handler; take, where a load is found, a load of the
    same box at a greater field found below it. Chosen: the second.
    Why: it is `first_read` with another predicate at the body of the
    `Let`, so every statement about `first_read` keeps its form; the
    loads of the fields of another tag are in other arms, not below.
    Cost: a load that is taken before the load at the head of the
    handler is given arguments that are certain only (item 13).
31. **At a load of two tags the pass stops, and the tags are those
    of the blocks that the applications give.** Options: make, as
    Simplify does, a parameter for each field of each tag and remove
    those that nothing reads; leave the load and go on to the next
    read; stop for that handler, as at any read that cannot be made
    a parameter. Chosen: stop. Why: the first builds what Simplify
    then throws away, and the pass takes one read at a time, in an
    order in which the loads come last, so that going on would need
    a `first_read` that skips a read, and a statement for it. The
    model has no types, so the tags are read off the term: those of
    the arguments that are known blocks, in the applications that
    the tests of the box do not keep from the load. Cost: a read
    that comes after such a load in the order (a load or an
    unboxing, of this box or of another parameter) is not made a
    parameter, where Simplify may make it one; and an argument that
    is not a known block counts for no tag. No term of the measure
    shows either.
32. **A read in scope is kept as the sharing of primitives keeps
    what is computed, and is used at the head only.** Options: say
    of the box, once a `Let` has unboxed it, that it is the wrapping
    of the variable of that `Let` (an entry of what is known of
    wrapped numbers, read by the functions that are there); keep
    the reads apart, as `FlambdaCseSpec.computed`, and look the
    read up. Chosen: the second. Why: the first is not true in the
    model, where what is known of a wrapped number is the form of
    the value of the variable, and a variable that is unboxed may
    hold a symbol of a static box; the second has the lemmas of
    `FlambdaCseProofs.v` for binders and for renaming, and is right
    for every read of a box, not only of a number. Cost: the kind
    of the `Let` that made the read is not kept, so only the read
    that a handler begins with takes it, where no kind is asked.
33. **One parameter for what is read in two places: by the read,
    and by the tag for loads.** Options: a pass after the unboxing
    that makes one of two parameters which every application gives
    the same argument; where a read is made a parameter, make the
    other reads of the same parameter by the same primitive `Let`s
    of it. Chosen: the second. Why: the first needs a simulation of
    its own, and would also make one of two parameters that
    Simplify keeps apart (the two fields of a pair that every arm
    gives the same thing); the second is by what Simplify's
    parameters are made from, and its proof is the lemmas of
    contexts under the fact that the step already has, that the
    new parameter holds what the primitive has on the parameter
    read. For loads the tag counts, as it does for Simplify, and
    it is read off the applications as `loads_of_two_tags` reads
    it. Cost: the `Let`s stay until the next round follows them,
    so a read that comes after one of them at the head of what is
    left of the handler is taken with arguments that are certain,
    or in the next round; and the loads of one field for which
    two tags are known are not shared even where one of the tags
    has two of them.
