# T2, the typing environment: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/T2-env`, on the integration branch, where the
first request (the environment, what satisfies it, lookup) and T3's
aliases are merged. Files, all in
`middle_end/flambda2/types/rocq/`:

- `FlambdaBindingTime.v`: binding times and name modes. Written by T3
  as the first commit of its branch, owned by T2.
- `FlambdaTypingEnv.v`: the environment and its functions, in modules
  with the compiler's names (`Typing_env_level`, `Cached_level`,
  `Typing_env`). Definitions only; it extracts to plain OCaml (tried on
  the side). Its header says, field by field, what the model has of
  `Typing_env.t` and every difference.
- `FlambdaTypingEnvSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypingEnvProofs.v`: what satisfies
  an environment and what looking a name up gives. 32 statements.
- `FlambdaTypingEnvExamplesSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypingEnvExamplesProofs.v`:
  the environment that Simplify builds for a small term, the same in
  the body of a closure, the handler of a continuation after a join, a
  symbol, poison; with machine environments that do and do not satisfy
  each. 24 statements.

- `FlambdaTypingEnvInvariantSpec.v`,
  `FlambdaTypingEnvInvariantProofs.v`: the invariant of an environment,
  as a test and as conditions, with where the compiler has each; what
  it gives to `cut`, to the fold of a level and to the aliases; that
  the functions keep it; beside it, that the types are well formed.
  42 statements.

- `FlambdaTypingEnvOperationsSpec.v`,
  `FlambdaTypingEnvOperationsProofs.v`: what the functions that build
  an environment do to what satisfies it, and what they leave as it
  is. 23 statements.

- `FlambdaTypingEnvKindedSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypingEnvKindedProofs.v`:
  the kinds of the `Simple`s that an environment knows and of those
  that it knows to be equal; premises about kinds, beside the
  invariant and not part of it. 42 statements.
- `FlambdaTypingEnvRefinementSpec.v`,
  `FlambdaTypingEnvRefinementProofs.v`: refinement of terms relative to
  a typing environment (`expr_refines_under`), and going under binders;
  three small terms rewritten with it. 24 statements.

- `FlambdaTypingEnvRangeSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypingEnvRangeProofs.v`:
  the machine satisfies a typing environment through a valuation whose
  values are in range, which gives the join of environments its
  condition on the tags of blocks. 12 statements.

- `FlambdaSimplifyPrimitivesSpec.v`,
  `FlambdaSimplifyPrimitivesProofs.v`: the first slice of the stream
  "primitives through types" (S1 of the plan), which the orchestrator
  gave to T2: a primitive replaced by a constant, or the term by
  `Invalid`, on the answer of a prover about the types of its
  arguments. 16 statements.
- `FlambdaSimplifyProjectionsSpec.v`,
  `FlambdaSimplifyProjectionsProofs.v`: the second slice: a load from
  a block or a projection from a closure replaced by the `Simple` that
  a prover answers, and the type of a block that is made. 12
  statements.
- `FlambdaSimplifyBoxingSpec.v`, `FlambdaSimplifyBoxingProofs.v`: the
  third slice: the types of a number that is tagged or boxed, and the
  unboxing primitive replaced by the `Simple` that a prover answers
  for the number inside. 9 statements.
- `FlambdaSimplifyArithSpec.v`, `FlambdaSimplifyArithProofs.v`: the
  fourth slice: the arithmetic of tagged immediates replaced by a
  constant, by one of its arguments or by `Invalid`, and the types of
  its result. 7 statements.
- `FlambdaSimplifyPassSpec.v`, `FlambdaSimplifyPassProofs.v`: the
  rewrites of the four slices assembled into one function on terms,
  `simplify_by_types`, with the proof that its answer refines the
  term. 17 statements.
- `FlambdaSimplifyPruningSpec.v`, `FlambdaSimplifyPruningProofs.v`: a
  second function, `simplify_by_types_pruning`, which also removes the

- `FlambdaSimplifyUsesSpec.v`, `FlambdaSimplifyUsesProofs.v`: the
  handler of a continuation rewritten under the environment of its
  one application, and under any environment that has the guarantees
  of the join of the environments of its applications. 4 statements.

`FlambdaEnvAliases.v`, `FlambdaEnvAliasesSpec.v`,
`FlambdaEnvAliasesProofs.v` and T3's files on the invariant and the

First merge request: the environment, what satisfies it, lookup.

- The definitions (`FlambdaTypingEnv.v`): levels, the environment,
  `find`, `mem`, `add_variable_definition`, `add_symbol_definition`,

  `cut`, `closure_env`, `type_simple_in_term_exn`,
  `get_canonical_simple_exn` and the functions around them.
- What satisfies an environment, in two layers: a valuation, which may
  give a value to any variable the environment knows, and the machine,
  which binds the variables in scope and agrees on them with a
  valuation that satisfies the environment. The header of the Spec says
  why, and what is asked of a name with no value at run time, of
  symbols, of code and of poison.
- Valuations are partial: a variable that a join made existential
  stands for nothing in a run through a use that does not define it
  (discrepancy T2-6). Nothing is asked of a name with no value, so a
  statement about a name that is not reached through a value in hand
  needs the premise that the name has one (`example_absent_env`: a
  name of type bottom in an environment that is not bottom, which
  `Meet_env` builds for the binary join).
- `typing_env_satisfied_but exempt statics valuation tenv` is the one
  definition; of an exempt name only the kind is asked. Named cases:
  - `typing_env_satisfied_strictly` (no name exempt) and
    `env_satisfies_strictly`: for the statements about types (meet,
    join, the provers) and for Simplify up to variant unboxing;
  - `typing_env_satisfied` and `env_satisfies` (D26, the orchestrator's
    ruling; two `CR mshinwell`): exempt are the names that hold poison
    and, since the ruling was revised and then amended (`Is_int` and
    `Get_tag` are never true of a scrutinee that holds poison; `Is_null`
    relates poison of kind value to 0), the names whose type is an
    `Is_int` or a `Get_tag` relation on a name that holds poison
    (`exempt_by_poison`, `relation_on_poison`;
    `example_relation_env_poison`). Only `find_not_poison` follows from
    it: with an exempt name an alias may not be replaced by the type of
    the name (`example_env_poison_alias_type`). The header's sentence
    that under strict satisfaction a statement that reads an `Is_int`
    or a `Get_tag` relation has no case of poison is true once
    `relation_holds` of `FlambdaTypeMeaningSpec.v` has no poison clause
    for the two, which it has not since T1's second request was merged
    (the header said "until then"; the fourth series drops that).
- Statements: how the notions compare (`satisfied_but_weaken`,
  `satisfied_strictly_satisfied`, `satisfied_no_poison_strictly`,
  `env_satisfies_strictly_env_satisfies`); what satisfaction depends on
  (`satisfied_but_coincide`, `typing_env_satisfied_coincide`,
  `env_satisfies_coincide`, `env_satisfies_strictly_coincide`); lookup

  `find_not_poison`, `find_kind`, `find_cases`, `mem_find`,

- `levels_agree` (the equation of the latest level for a name is the
  type the environment has for it) is a proposition only: it says that
  two types are the same term. So `typing_env_invariant tenv :=
  typing_env_well_formed tenv = true ∧ levels_agree tenv`. It stays so
  (below, "Decided overnight", item 4).
- What it gives: `cut_defined`, `cut_equations_found`,

- The functions keep it: `create_invariant`, `make_bottom_invariant`,

  `add_symbol_definition_invariant`,
  `add_variable_definition_invariant` (the variable is not bound),

## Sixth series: done so far

Above the fifth series; not reviewed yet. Asked for by the
orchestrator, for the join of environments (T5), whose statement for
the compiler's join is for the runs in which every block that a
`Simple` of known variables evaluates to has a tag in range
(`blocks_have_tags`, in the contract of the join).

`FlambdaTypingEnvRangeSpec.v`:

- The difficulty. A state of the run of a well-formed unit is in
  range (`FlambdaRangeSpec.v`), so the values of its variables are.
  But a typing environment is satisfied through a valuation, which
  also gives values to the variables that exist only in types, and of
  those the machine says nothing. So "the machine satisfies the
  environment and is in range" does not give a valuation in range.
- `env_satisfies_strictly_in_range tenv statics vars`: the machine
  satisfies the environment strictly through a valuation all of whose
  values are in range. `satisfies_in_range_strictly` (it gives the
  plain notion).
- `in_range_blocks_have_tags`: under a valuation and a static
  environment in range, every block that a `Simple` evaluates to has a
  tag in range, which is a tag, and fields in range. This is the
  join's condition, for any set of known variables.
- `agrees_in_terms_has_value`: a valuation that agrees with the
  machine gives every variable that may occur in a term the machine's
  value. That is all that the machine gives of the join's other three
  conditions on runs (that certain variables have values): of a
  variable that exists only in types no agreement is asked, and those
  conditions stay premises (discrepancies T5-5 and T5-10).
- Where it holds: `create_satisfies_in_range` (any machine, of the
  environment that knows nothing), `satisfied_strictly_satisfies_in_range`.
- The steps keep it, where the values that the machine binds are in
  range: `increment_scope_`, `add_variable_definition_`,
  `add_definitions_of_params_`, `replace_equation_`,
  `add_alias_satisfies_in_range`, `satisfies_in_range_statics_extend`.
- `example_out_of_range` (the reviewer's): an environment that a
  machine in range satisfies strictly, and through no valuation in
  range. A variable that exists only in types is, by its type, the
  tagged immediate of 2^70, and a variable of the machine is `Is_int`
  of it. The environment has its invariant and its types are well
  formed (the statement says so since the review: the type is written
  so that it is not an alias on a canonical name).
- Owed by others, and said to them: that the witnesses that a join

## Needs recorded

- Replacing a name that may hold poison by a constant or by another
  name needs "the result may be more defined than poison", which the
  core does not have (D26, with D1 and D6). Until it has,
  such statements take the premise that the name is not poison.
- That the machine binds a variable to a value of the kind it is
  defined with was a premise of the statements about binders. It no
  longer is. The machine tests the kind of a value at a `Let`, where
  a handler is applied and at a call, so the statements at the kinds
  that the term gives have no such premise
  (`let_declared_refines_under`, and those of
  `FlambdaSimplifyHandlersSpec.v` and `FlambdaSimplifyCodeEnvSpec.v`).

- Refinement relative to what a run keeps. The statements that go
  into a handler rest on a lemma of the core about handlers that
  refine under any values, continuations and static environment. Two
  things that a run keeps would make more rewrites right inside a
  handler, and each needs that lemma to be about the runs that keep
  it: that the state is in range (the join of environments needs the
  tags of blocks to be tags), and that the values bound are of the
  kinds that the binders give (a handler rewritten at the kinds of its
  parameters). Told to the integrator and the orchestrator. The
  second is met since: the core's lemmas hand the fact that the
  values are of the kinds of the handler, and the handlers are
  rewritten at those kinds.
