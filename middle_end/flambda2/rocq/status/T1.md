# T1, grammar and meaning of Flambda types: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/T1-grammar`. Three requests are merged: the
grammar and the meaning (on the integration branch, the commits from
4100198eb1 to 3ba1702de2), what the other types streams asked for
next (from 9a9762c814 to 827dc6adf1), and the renaming of types with
what the streams of meet, the joins, the provers and the names asked
for (from 265b97dabc to b3676e6f57). The last two are under "After
the first merge", with what the branch has after them. Files, all in
`middle_end/flambda2/types/rocq/`:

- `FlambdaTypeGrammar.v`: the grammar (`flambda_type` and the types
  defined with it), `kind`, and the creation functions of
  `type_grammar.ml` and `more_type_creators.ml` that the statements
  use. Definitions only; reviewed against the OCaml like
  `FlambdaModel.v`. Its header says, constructor by constructor, what
  the model has.
- `FlambdaTypeGrammarProofs.v`: `types_induction`, and `name_eqb_eq`.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningSpec.v`: `type_satisfied statics vars v t`, what it
  reads of a value, and the statements.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningProofs.v`: the proofs, one equation for each
  constructor (hint database `type_satisfied`), and the examples.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningBoolSpec.v`: `type_satisfied_b` and the tests of
  the other functions of the meaning, with the statements that each
  answers `true` exactly when the meaning holds.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningBoolProofs.v`: their proofs.
- `FlambdaTypeWellFormedSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeWellFormedProofs.v`:
  `type_well_formed`, the types' part of well-formedness.
- `FlambdaTypeEqualSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeEqualProofs.v`: `type_eqb`, the
  structural equality of types.
- `FlambdaTypeAliasKindsSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeAliasKindsProofs.v`: what
  `aliases_with_kinds` is of the types the creation functions make.
- `FlambdaTypeRenameSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeRenameProofs.v`:
  `rename_type` and `free_names`.
- `FlambdaTypeNominalSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeNominalProofs.v`: types as
  things that contain names, in the interface of
  `FlambdaNameAbstractionSpec.v`.
- `FlambdaTypeAccessSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeAccessProofs.v`: the
  functions of the grammar through which the provers and reification
  read blocks and closures.

Today's `FlambdaTypesSpec.v` and `FlambdaTypesProofs.v` are untouched
and still build; `FlambdaAliases*` and the validator use them.

After R1's review of the first request (changes required, two blocking
findings, both about the relations), on top of the second request:

- B1, `Get_tag`. A relation of a type is not the execution of its
  primitive. `relation_holds` for `Get_tag` now also relates a tagged
  immediate to any number: unboxing records the relation of a parameter
  that may be an immediate and passes the tag 0 there. The primitive
  stays undefined on an immediate.
- B2, `Is_null`. The two directions disagreed on poison and on a symbol
  that is not bound. `relation_holds` for `Is_null` now relates every
  value of kind `Value` (1 for `Null`, 0 for any other), which is the
  domain the type of the scrutinee admits and the compiler's reading of
  a constant.
- `Is_int` checked for the same: its two directions agree (tagged
  immediates and blocks), and are unchanged.
- `Relation.of_const`, the compiler's reading of a relation on a
  constant, is in the grammar, with `relation_of_const_sound` (where
  the relation holds of a constant and the compiler gives a number, it
  is that number: what T4 needs for
  `reduce_head_of_kind_naked_immediate`) and `is_null_of_const` (for
  `Is_null` it is exact at kind value). For `Is_int` the compiler's
  reading is wider than the relation, and its two creation paths
  disagree on `Null` (discrepancy T1-2).
- New statements `is_null_coherent`, `is_int_coherent`,
  `get_tag_coherent`: the variable of a type holds a number that the
  relation relates to each value of the type. The three statements for
  the relation types spell the relation out.
- `relation_get_tag` is weaker: the value is an immediate or a block
  with one of the tags. So one reduction of the compiler's `Meet`
  (`reduce_inverse_relations`, the `Get_tag` case) is not sound for
  this meaning: it makes the value a block. For T4 and the human, see
  the open questions.
- Examples: the unboxing case; `Is_null` of poison and of an unbound
  symbol, both sides; mixed and float shapes, with rejection by shape
  from an inhabited type; `closure_types`; closures with at least some
  contents (`at_least_this_closure`, with its statement); a second kind
  of naked number and of boxed number. The claim is now "for each
  constructor but `Mutable_block`".
- Found while checking the unboxing case: unboxing gives types to
  parameters that hold poison (a field, unboxed in turn, of a variant
  that is an immediate on that path). They were not true for the
  meaning here, where a poison value has no type that says what it is.
  Examples `unboxed_poison_*`; discrepancy T1-3. Decided since (D26),
  see "After the first epoch".
- Discrepancies recorded (`middle_end/flambda2/rocq/DECISIONS.md` has
  their titles): T1-1 (`Get_tag` in `reduce_inverse_relations`, with a
  run of the compiler's types library), T1-2 (`Is_int` of a constant),
  T1-3 (types of poison parameters in unboxing).

After R3's review of the second request (changes required, one
blocking finding and three notes), on top of the above:

- Blocking: a function type of a set of closures made the function
  exist. `closures_entry_satisfied` now says the code of a function
  only if the set has the function, as the closure types and the value
  slot types already did; what the set has is for the index. New
  statements `closures_case_function_code` (the code is known where
  the index has the slot) and `closures_case_own_code` (and always for
  the slot of the closure itself, with `closure_in_set_slot`): the two
  cases in which a prover reads a function type.
- The compiler has two implementations of meet and join. The default,
  at every optimisation level, is `meet_and_join.ml` (binary join);
  `meet_and_n_way_join.ml` runs only with the n-way join algorithm or
  match-in-match. The reviews and the plan for T4 cite the second. The
  two joins differ on function types (discrepancy T1-4): the n-way one
  keeps a function type that one input has, and is then not an upper
  bound where another input says nothing of the code of the closure's
  own function (`meet_code_ids` answers the code; reproduced with the
  types library; examples `nway_join_*`).
- Notes 1 and 2 (code age, allocation modes and uniqueness): the
  header says what the restriction means for T4, T6 and S4.
- Note 3 (what meet relies on in raw structures): the header lists the
  conditions (product no wider than the index, slots among those of
  the index, shapes written in one way, kinds), and four statements
  say what each gives for one case: `block_case_empty`,
  `closures_case_function_code`, `closures_case_value_slot`,
  `block_shapes_disjoint`. The predicate over a whole type, with the
  creation functions establishing it, is not written yet (see
  "Next").
- The header says what a meet shortcut of the provers answers about
  (not every value of the type).
- Discrepancies recorded: T1-4 (the two joins), T1-5 (what the
  creation functions and raw constructors leave unchecked, and a
  comment on products that the code contradicts).

## Decided overnight (2026-11-11), for review

- **Where the compiler stops, the model's function answers `None`.**
  `Row_like_for_closures.get_single_tag` has a fatal error on an exact
  case whose index lacks the slot of the case. Options: leave the
  function partial with a premise; answer `No_singleton`; answer
  `None`. Chosen: `None`, with the statement that a well-formed type
  never gets there. It keeps the transcription total and the
  compiler's stop visible. To change: one constructor and three
  proofs.
- **`find_function_type ~exact:true` answering bottom is not stated
  as "the type has no value".** The compiler's prover reads it so
  (`prove_single_closures_entry`). Under the meaning here that is
  false of a type that is not well formed (the meaning does not ask
  an entry to have function types). Options: strengthen the meaning
  of an exact case so that it asks for a function type of every
  function; or state the reading for well-formed types only. Chosen:
  the second, since the first would make the meaning depend on the
  shape of an entry and not on the value. It is the kind of place
  that T1-7 already records (nothing in the compiler checks that an
  exact case has a function type for each of its functions).
- **A possible disagreement on `Is_null` and poison is recorded and
  not resolved** (T1-9): a prover answers 1 for `Is_null` of a name
  of type `Null`, where the relation of the model gives 0 if that
  name holds poison. No program is known. It is for T6's statement
  of `meet_naked_immediates` to take the strict environment or a
  premise; the meaning is unchanged. If a program is found, the
  amendment of D26 (`Is_null` relates poison to 0) is what to look
  at again.
- **The revision of D26 and its amendment are one commit** in the
  second request, so that a reader sees only what changed against the
  reviewed text. No statement is affected.
- **What an equation of an environment extension asks of a name with
  no value: unchanged, and recommended to stay.** Found by T3. Here an
  equation `(n, ty)` of an extension asks that `n` have a value, of
  type `ty` (`env_extension_satisfied`). The typing environment (T2)
  asks the type of a name only if the name has a value, since a
  variable that a join made existential stands for nothing on some
  runs. So "a valuation that satisfies an environment satisfies the
  extension made of a level cut out of it" needs the premise that the
  names of the level have values; the meet makes such extensions
  (`meet_variant`, `meet_row_like`). Options: (i) an equation of an
  extension asks the type only of a name that has a value, as in an
  environment (T2's recommendation: an equation then means the same
  in both); (ii) the meaning stays, and whoever makes an extension of
  a level shows that its names have values. Chosen: (ii). Two
  reasons. First, (i) weakens a merged definition and makes
  `type_satisfied_monotone_statement` and
  `env_extension_satisfied_monotone_statement` false as stated.
  Second, and decisive, T4's: the meet is also a consumer of
  extensions. It adds the extension of a case to the scoped
  environment (`add_env_extension_strict` in `meet_case` and
  `meet_variant`) and drops the case if that is bottom; with two
  inputs that each have an equation on `y` in the case, the two types
  are met, and if `y` may have no value nothing ties them, so the
  case can be dropped for a value that is in it. Under (ii) a value
  in the case gives `y` a value of both types. The price of (ii) is
  on the producer: T4's contract of the meet, and of the join it is
  given, now says that no equation is added on a name that has no
  value (`equations_valued` in its file). The meet can prove that of
  its own equations. The compiler's n-way join cannot in general (it
  brings in a variable that only one of the joined environments
  defines, with its type: T5-5, and T5-10 for the consequence), so
  that clause is a hypothesis on the join with its CR, in T4's and
  T5's files. To change to (i) later: the definition,
  `env_extension_lookup_statement`, the two monotone statements, the
  boolean test, and the proofs over them here and in the files of T2
  to T5b.
