# T1, grammar and meaning of Flambda types: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/T1-grammar`. Five requests are merged: the
grammar and the meaning (on the integration branch, the commits from
4100198eb1 to 3ba1702de2), what the other types streams asked for
next (from 9a9762c814 to 827dc6adf1), the renaming of types with
what the streams of meet, the joins, the provers and the names asked
for (from 265b97dabc to b3676e6f57), the creation functions under a
renaming (e85aef0872), and the meaning as an equivariant function
with the other creation functions (from 979ab86338 to a0253c5ccf).
The last four are under "After the first merge". The branch has
after them the first slice of the `Switch`. Files, all in
`middle_end/flambda2/types/rocq/`:

- `FlambdaTypeGrammar.v`: the grammar (`flambda_type` and the types
  defined with it), `kind`, and the creation functions of
  `type_grammar.ml` and `more_type_creators.ml` that the statements
  use. Definitions only; reviewed against the OCaml like
  `FlambdaModel.v`. Its header says, constructor by constructor, what
  the model has.
- `FlambdaTypeGrammarProofs.v`: `types_induction`, and `name_eqb_eq`.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningSpec.v`: `type_satisfied statics vars v t`, what it
  reads of a value, and the statements.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningProofs.v`: the proofs, one equation for each
  constructor (hint database `type_satisfied`), and the examples.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningBoolSpec.v`: `type_satisfied_b` and the tests of
  the other functions of the meaning, with the statements that each
  answers `true` exactly when the meaning holds.
- `FlambdaTypeMeaningBoolProofs.v`: their proofs.
- `FlambdaTypeWellFormedSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeWellFormedProofs.v`:
  `type_well_formed`, the types' part of well-formedness.
- `FlambdaTypeEqualSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeEqualProofs.v`: `type_eqb`, the
  structural equality of types.
- `FlambdaTypeAliasKindsSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeAliasKindsProofs.v`: what
  `aliases_with_kinds` is of the types the creation functions make.
- `FlambdaTypeRenameSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeRenameProofs.v`:
  `rename_type` and `free_names`.
- `FlambdaTypeNominalSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeNominalProofs.v`: types as
  things that contain names, in the interface of
  `FlambdaNameAbstractionSpec.v`.
- `FlambdaTypeAccessSpec.v`, `FlambdaTypeAccessProofs.v`: the

- `FlambdaSimplifySwitchSpec.v`, `FlambdaSimplifySwitchProofs.v`: not

After R1's review of the first request (changes required, two blocking
findings, both about the relations), on top of the second request:

- B1, `Get_tag`. A relation of a type is not the execution of its
  primitive. `relation_holds` for `Get_tag` now also relates a tagged
  immediate to any number: unboxing records the relation of a parameter
  that may be an immediate and passes the tag 0 there. The primitive
  stays undefined on an immediate.
- B2, `Is_null`. The two directions disagreed on poison and on a symbol
  that is not bound. `relation_holds` for `Is_null` now relates every
  value of kind `Value` (1 for `Null`, 0 for any other), which is the
  domain the type of the scrutinee admits and the compiler's reading of
  a constant.
- `Is_int` checked for the same: its two directions agree (tagged
  immediates and blocks), and are unchanged.
- `Relation.of_const`, the compiler's reading of a relation on a
  constant, is in the grammar, with `relation_of_const_sound` (where
  the relation holds of a constant and the compiler gives a number, it
  is that number: what T4 needs for
  `reduce_head_of_kind_naked_immediate`) and `is_null_of_const` (for
  `Is_null` it is exact at kind value). For `Is_int` the compiler's
  reading is wider than the relation, and its two creation paths
  disagree on `Null` (discrepancy T1-2).
- New statements `is_null_coherent`, `is_int_coherent`,
  `get_tag_coherent`: the variable of a type holds a number that the
  relation relates to each value of the type. The three statements for
  the relation types spell the relation out.
- `relation_get_tag` is weaker: the value is an immediate or a block
  with one of the tags. So one reduction of the compiler's `Meet`
  (`reduce_inverse_relations`, the `Get_tag` case) is not sound for
  this meaning: it makes the value a block. For T4 and the human, see
  the open questions.
- Examples: the unboxing case; `Is_null` of poison and of an unbound
  symbol, both sides; mixed and float shapes, with rejection by shape
  from an inhabited type; `closure_types`; closures with at least some
  contents (`at_least_this_closure`, with its statement); a second kind
  of naked number and of boxed number. The claim is now "for each
  constructor but `Mutable_block`".
- Found while checking the unboxing case: unboxing gives types to
  parameters that hold poison (a field, unboxed in turn, of a variant
  that is an immediate on that path). They were not true for the
  meaning here, where a poison value has no type that says what it is.
  Examples `unboxed_poison_*`; discrepancy T1-3. Decided since (D26),
  see "After the first epoch".
- Discrepancies recorded (`middle_end/flambda2/rocq/DECISIONS.md` has
  their titles): T1-1 (`Get_tag` in `reduce_inverse_relations`, with a
  run of the compiler's types library), T1-2 (`Is_int` of a constant),
  T1-3 (types of poison parameters in unboxing).

After R3's review of the second request (changes required, one

- Blocking: a function type of a set of closures made the function

  keeps a function type that one input has, and is then not an upper

- The header says what a meet shortcut of the provers answers about

- Discrepancies recorded: T1-4 (the two joins), T1-5 (what the
