## Found

- **Renaming apart is not equivariant.** On the branch of the corpus
  stream, `rename_apart` gives the binders of a term new names by
  counting up from the largest name in the term. So the names it
  gives the binders of a renamed term are not the images of those it
  gives the binders of the term: the result depends on the numbers of
  names. What holds, with no premise, and what is to be stated: the
  result on the renamed term and the renamed result are the same up
  to bound names (`expr_eqb [] []` accepts the two). Consequence:
  once that pass is in `normalise_before`, that `validate_unit`
  answers the same on renamed terms no longer follows pass by pass,
  because the pass after it that follows aliases does not respect
  "the same up to bound names"; it needs the finer fact that the
  result on the renamed term is the renamed result under another
  renaming, which agrees with the first on the free names. Not a
  discrepancy with the compiler: it is a function of the model's
  validator. N1 and the owner are told.

  N1's ruling: it is the fourth form of `NAMES.md`, section 7 (a
  function that makes a name). What is owed is the finer fact: for
  every `r` and `e` there is `r'` that agrees with `r` on the free
  names of `e` with
  `rename_apart (rename_expr r e) = rename_expr r' (rename_apart e)`;
  the form with `expr_eqb` is its corollary. The pass is to take the
  names to avoid and use `fresh_name`. For `validate_unit` no equation
  pass by pass is needed after it: the two terms get renamings that
  agree with `r` on their free names, and the comparison of the two
  results answers as that of the unrenamed ones by the transitivity
  and symmetry of the comparison, by its accepting a term with closed
  code against its image under a renaming that fixes its free names,
  and by its equivariance. That needs closed code, which
  well-formedness gives. The header of the `Spec` file is to say, at
  that statement, that following aliases does not respect "the same
  up to bound names", which is why the order of the passes matters.
