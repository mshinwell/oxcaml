- **Rewrites that make names** (seven; three definitions to review).

  renaming. `renames_alike r r' e`: two renamings rename the free
  names of a term alike. `equivariant_up_to f`: on a term with closed
  code, the result for the renamed term is the result under a second
  renaming that renames the free names of that result as the first
  does. `keeps_scope f`: on a term with closed code, the code of the
  result is closed and its free names are free in the term. The free
  names are those of the one function for each sort
  (`FlambdaBindersSpec.v`). Stated: a rewrite that commutes does so up
  to the names it makes; the two properties compose and iterate; the
  comparison does not tell apart two renamings of its first term that
  rename its free names alike (`expr_eqb_renamed_alike`), so it
  answers the same when the term that such a rewrite is applied to
  and the second term are renamed (`expr_eqb_up_to`). No rewrite on
  the integration branch is such a rewrite; this is for the pass that
  renames binders apart (below).

## Found

- **Renaming apart is not equivariant**, and cannot be: it makes names
  with `fresh_names`, outside the names of the term. What its file
  states: for every `r` and `e` there is `r'` that does to the free
  names of `e` what `r` does, with
  `rename_apart (rename_expr r e) = rename_expr r' (rename_apart e)`.
  The pass after it that follows aliases does not respect "the same up
  to bound names", so the invariance of the verdict cannot go pass by
  pass through the comparison; it goes through `equivariant_up_to`,
  which composes, and uses the comparison once, at the end. That
  needs closed code, which well-formedness gives, and that each
  rewrite of the validation keeps scope, which nothing stated before.
  Not a discrepancy with the compiler: `rename_apart` is a function of
  the model's validator.
- Nothing else that is not equivariant as it stands: no other rewrite
  or check makes a name or goes by the order of names. The one place
  where names are made from others is `inline_renaming`, which is made
  of swaps. No discrepancy with the compiler was found.

Each is a choice the human may reverse; the cost of doing so is given.

1. **A named predicate for a rewrite that takes only a term**
   (`rewrite_equivariant f`). Cost of changing: one line a statement.
2. **`expr_in_scope` and `inlinable_b` are stated here**, from N1's
   lemmas. Cost of changing: delete two statements.
3. **The action on what a pass carries is defined in my `Spec` file**
   (`rename_known_blocks`, `rename_known_numbers`,
   `rename_naive_typing_env`, `rename_kind_env`, `rename_arity_env`,
   `rename_trap_stack`, `rename_demands`), not in the file of the
   pass, since I do not edit those. Cost of changing: move the
   definitions to their owners' files.
4. **`follow_aliases` is stated and proved here**, though the file is
   of the types library. Cost of changing: move two statements and
   250 lines of proof.
5. **`static_names` is stated with a premise** on the function it is
   given, and again for the two functions used.
6. **`envs_free_of` and `static_fault` are included**, which are tests
   on environments and states and not checks that the validator runs.
7. **The check of kinds is stated here** though no statement of the
   validation has it as a premise: the test tool runs it.
8. **The invariance of the verdict is conditional once binders are
   renamed apart** (the orchestrator's ruling, for the human).
   Options: (a) hold the second series of the corpus stream until the
   premises are proved; (b) land it with the two statements
   deleted and nothing in their place; (c) land it with a conditional
   theorem whose premises are named propositions, a review comment
   for the human, and a header that says the invariance is not
   established. Chosen: (c). Three units of the corpus need the pass;
   the premises are owed and being proved. Cost of changing: with
   (a), the three units stay "not validated" until then.
9. **The verdict is stated to be invariant for a term before whose
   code is closed**, not for every term as before. The proof uses the
   premise: the comparison is known to accept a term against its
   image under a renaming that fixes its free names only where code
   is closed. A well-formed unit has closed code. Nothing is
   established for the other terms, and no counterexample is known.
   Cost of changing: a proof for terms with open code, if there is
   one.
10. **The free names in the three new definitions are those of the
    one function for each sort**, not the older relations (N1 and the
    reviewer asked). Where code is closed the two agree.
