## Found

- **Renaming apart is not equivariant**, and cannot be: it makes names
  with `fresh_names`, outside the names of the term. What its file
  states: for every `r` and `e` there is `r'` that does to the free
  names of `e` what `r` does, with
  `rename_apart (rename_expr r e) = rename_expr r' (rename_apart e)`.
  The pass after it that follows aliases does not respect "the same up
  to bound names", so the invariance of the verdict cannot go pass by
  pass through the comparison; it goes through `equivariant_up_to`,
  which composes, and uses the comparison once, at the end. That
  needs closed code, which well-formedness gives, and that each
  rewrite of the validation keeps scope, which nothing stated before.
  Not a discrepancy with the compiler: `rename_apart` is a function of
  the model's validator.
- **With two liftings or more the verdict may depend on the number of
  a continuation of the unit.** A lifting is made only if what it
  makes is a well-formed unit, and the checks of exception handlers
  and of trap actions go by the name of the unit's exception
  continuation without following rebinding. A round makes names by
  number, outside the names of the term only. Where the term no
  longer mentions a continuation of the unit after the first lifting
  and its normalisation, a binder that a round makes may have its
  number, and the next lifting is then refused; for the renamed unit
  the collision may be absent, or the other way round. So the
  invariance of the verdict was stated with a second premise, and the
  validator could refuse a unit that it could validate (the converter
  numbers the unit's continuations before the names of the term, so
  this was not expected of a unit that the tool converts). No pair of
  terms with two verdicts was exhibited. Repaired since: the round
  after a lifting is given the continuations of the units of the
  liftings and makes no name that is one of them
  (`rename_apart_avoiding`), and the premise is gone.
- **Whoever rewrites the term before with a rewrite that makes names,
  ahead of the validation, is to give that rewrite the continuations
  that the validation is given and the term need not mention**: those
  of the units of the liftings, which a lifting reads, and those
  given for the loops, which `loopify_unit` binds. The second asking
  of the validation (`FlambdaValidateKnowingSpec.v`) rewrites the
  term before so, and the reviewer of that stream found that its
  rounds were given nothing to avoid. Its owner's repair, and the
  statement that the second asking answers the same on renamed terms,
  are not in this tree.
- Nothing else that is not equivariant as it stands: no other rewrite
  or check makes a name or goes by the order of names. The one place
  where names are made from others is `inline_renaming`, which is made
  of swaps. No discrepancy with the compiler was found.

5. **`static_names` is stated with a premise** on the function it is

   on environments and states and not checks that the validator runs.
7. **The check of kinds is stated here** though no statement of the

8. **The invariance of the verdict was to be conditional once binders

   premises have since been proved, so nothing conditional lands).
   Options: (a) hold the second series of the corpus stream until the
   premises are proved; (b) land it with the two statements
   deleted and nothing in their place; (c) land it with a conditional
   theorem whose premises are named propositions, a review comment

   established. Chosen: (c). Three units of the corpus need the pass;
   the premises are owed and being proved. Cost of changing: with
   (a), the three units stay "not validated" until then.
9. **The verdict is stated to be invariant for a term before whose
   code is closed**, not for every term as before. The proof uses the
   premise: the comparison is known to accept a term against its
   image under a renaming that fixes its free names only where code
   is closed. A well-formed unit has closed code. Nothing is
   established for the other terms, and no counterexample is known.
   Cost of changing: a proof for terms with open code, if there is
   one.
10. **The free names in the three new definitions are those of the
    one function for each sort**, not the older relations (N1 and the
    reviewer asked). Where code is closed the two agree.
11. **The verdict is stated to be invariant under a second premise
    where there are two liftings or more**: each lifting after the
    first is applied to a term in which the two continuations of its
    unit are free (`later_liftings_use_unit`). With one lifting or
    none it asks nothing. It leaves out a unit whose term, after its
    first lifting and the normalisation, no longer mentions its
    exception continuation free. The premise is of that statement
    only: the validation makes no such test and its soundness has no
    such premise. Options: (a) the premise; (b) a round that is told
    the two continuations of the unit and avoids them when it makes
    names; (c) checks of well-formedness that follow rebinding.
    Chosen: (a), which the orchestrator allowed so that the series
    that renames binders apart is not held; (b) is planned as the
    next series if its cost is small. Whether the statement fails
    without the premise is not established. There is a review comment
    for the human above the statement. Cost of changing: with (b),
    the round and the two normalisations get an argument, the second
    prover restates two facts about `rename_apart`, and the premise
    and about forty lines of proof go. **Changed to (b) since**, in
    the series after the one that wired the round in: the premise,
