## An unused parameter of a continuation that a call returns to

Simplify removes a parameter that the handler of a continuation does
not use, with the argument that each application gives it. A call
that returns to the continuation gives it its results, which are not
written in the term: Simplify makes the call return to a new
continuation, bound at the call, that takes the results and applies
the old one to those that are left. The two rewrites of the rounds
that remove unused parameters stop at a continuation that a call
returns to, so the term before kept the parameter and the two terms
differed. The source `let _ = if c then f x else 8 in ...` is of this
kind.

- **`FlambdaWrapReturnsSpec.v`** (mine; one statement, to be
  reviewed): `wrap_returns` removes, of a non-recursive continuation,
  one parameter that its handler does not use; the applications lose
  the argument, and each call that returns to the continuation is put
  under a `Let_cont` of a wrapper with the parameters and the kinds
  that the handler had. The wrapper is bound to the name of the
  continuation and its parameters have the names of the handler's, so
  the pass makes no name. It asks that the handler is not an
  exception handler, that its parameters are distinct, that every
  mention of the continuation is an application or the return of a
  call, and that there are two mentions at least. The result refines
  the term under all environments
  (`wrap_returns_refines_statement`); it is not stated as an
  equivalence, as the test of the kind of the removed parameter goes
  where an `Apply_cont` applied the continuation. The proofs are in
  `FlambdaWrapReturnsProofs.v` and, for names,
  `FlambdaWrapReturnsNamesProofs.v`.
- **Under renaming**: the pass commutes with every renaming by an
  equation (`wrap_returns_equivariant_statement`, in
  `FlambdaValidatorEquivarianceSpec.v`) and keeps scope
  (`wrap_returns_keeps_scope_statement`, in
  `FlambdaStaticsScopeSpec.v`).
- **The wiring**, with the word of the owner of the files of the
  validation: `FlambdaValidateSpec.normalise_expr_before e` is
  `wrap_returns` of what it was. No statement changes its text.

## A flag that a loop sets: parameters untagged together

parameter says a tagged immediate, the continuation gets a parameter
for the number, each application gives the number, and the tagged

## Found

- **Renaming apart is not equivariant**, and cannot be: it makes names
  with `fresh_names`, outside the names of the term. What its file
  states: for every `r` and `e` there is `r'` that does to the free
  names of `e` what `r` does, with
  `rename_apart (rename_expr r e) = rename_expr r' (rename_apart e)`.
  The pass after it that follows aliases does not respect "the same up
  to bound names", so the invariance of the verdict cannot go pass by
  pass through the comparison; it goes through `equivariant_up_to`,
  which composes, and uses the comparison once, at the end. That
  needs closed code, which well-formedness gives, and that each
  rewrite of the validation keeps scope, which nothing stated before.
  Not a discrepancy with the compiler: `rename_apart` is a function of
  the model's validator.
- **With two liftings or more the verdict may depend on the number of
  a continuation of the unit.** A lifting is made only if what it
  makes is a well-formed unit, and the checks of exception handlers
  and of trap actions go by the name of the unit's exception
  continuation without following rebinding. A round makes names by
  number, outside the names of the term only. Where the term no
  longer mentions a continuation of the unit after the first lifting
  and its normalisation, a binder that a round makes may have its
  number, and the next lifting is then refused; for the renamed unit
  the collision may be absent, or the other way round. So the
  invariance of the verdict was stated with a second premise, and the
  validator could refuse a unit that it could validate (the converter
  numbers the unit's continuations before the names of the term, so
  this was not expected of a unit that the tool converts). No pair of
  terms with two verdicts was exhibited. Repaired since: the round
  after a lifting is given the continuations of the units of the
  liftings and makes no name that is one of them
  (`rename_apart_avoiding`), and the premise is gone.
- **Whoever rewrites the term before with a rewrite that makes names,
  ahead of the validation, is to give that rewrite the continuations
  that the validation is given and the term need not mention**: those
  of the units of the liftings, which a lifting reads, and those
  given for   the loops, which `loopify_unit` binds. The second asking of the
  validation (`FlambdaValidateKnowingSpec.v`) rewrites the
  term before so, and the reviewer of that stream found that its
  rounds were given nothing to avoid. Its owner repaired it: the
  rounds are given the continuations of the units of the liftings and
  those given for the loops, and the second asking answers the same
  on renamed terms where the code of the term before is closed
  (`validate_unit_knowing_equivariant_statement`, in that file, by
  `validate_unit_renamed_alike`).
- **A rewrite of the term before owes two things, one of the term
  after owes one.** On the term before: its equation of renaming (or
  the form up to the names that it makes) and that it keeps scope,
  since the term is under a second renaming once a round has made
  names, and the comparison is known to answer the same only where
  the two renamings rename the free names of what is compared alike.
  On the term after, which is under the one renaming throughout: the
  equation only. Every wiring since has been read against this.
- Nothing else that is not equivariant as it stands: no other rewrite
  or check makes a name or goes by the order of names. The one place
