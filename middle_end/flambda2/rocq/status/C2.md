## Decided overnight (2026-11-11), for the human to review

- D10, the rest. The question: is a value of another kind than its
  binder's undefined where it is bound (by a `Let`, or as a parameter
  of a handler)? The options: (a) the machine does not test, and the
  check of kinds answers no for such a term; (b) the machine tests at
  each binder. The choice (the orchestrator's, on my recommendation):
  (a) in this series, with the CR in `FlambdaSemanticsSpec.v` before
  `step`; (b) as a separate series after it, since the types need "a
  variable holds a value of the kind of its binder" with no premise.
  Why: (a) changes no statement and lets the three epochs that queue
  behind this one land; (b) touches 71 proofs. What it costs to change:
  the request, section 9 (two tests in the machine, no change to the
  terms; five statements change or gain a premise).
- Tupled functions. The question: what the tool does with code that is
  tupled, which the model has no mark for. The choice: the converter
  refuses it. V2 has a finer rule (refuse a unit with tupled code and a
  call that is not direct), which I agreed to: it replaces mine when
  V2's series is rebased over this one, with the ledger's sentence.
- Partial applications and over-applications. C3 has written, with
  me, the request that puts them in the machine (merged
  since): the tests of kinds and arities are kept, adapted to each
  branch (a partial application is not compared with the result arity
  of the code, and the numbers of parameters differ on purpose);
  `partial_or_over_application` and its two helpers move to
  `FlambdaModel.v`; my statement that such a call is undefined goes
  then.

- The second series, as built (the request, section 12). Each of
  these is a choice that the human may want otherwise; none costs more
  than its own function and proof to change.
  - `thread_switch` asks that the handler it threads through give no
    kinds, and stays an equivalence. The other option was to make
    `thread_switches` a refinement, applied to the term before only.
    Measured: no example loses its validation, so the equivalence is
    kept and there is nothing to choose.
  - `remove_unused_total_lets` and `remove_unused_defined_lets` test
    the kind of what they remove, the second with the kinds of the
    variables in scope, and stay equivalences. The other option was a
    premise "the unit is well kinded" on the validator's theorem, which
    D12 rules out.
  - `inline_known_calls` inlines whatever the arguments are, once the
    arities agree and the code gives as many kinds as it has
    parameters.
  - Three statements of `FlambdaControlFlowSpec.v` gain a premise on
    kinds (the reviewer found two of the three before I did).
  - What is still not tested: a value given to a way out of the term,
    a field of a static block, a value slot (until a `Let` binds what
    is read from it), and a returned value against the result arity of
    the code (it is tested against the handler it returns to).
  - What "a variable holds a value of the kind of its binder" is: three
    statements about the step that binds, with no premise. Not a
    theorem that every state is well-kinded, and not a statement about
    a read, which needs the scoping of the term.
  - The third of them is stated of the place where the body of code
    is entered, and not of an `Apply`: with partial applications an
    `Apply` that has a step need not enter a body with all its
    arguments. The reviewer and the owner of that series asked for it.
    That place is `call_code` since that series landed, which restated
    the statement of it and added `called_closure_at_kind_statement`
    for the closure.
  - The extra arguments of an exception continuation are tested by the
    handler they reach, with the exception, and not against the kinds
    that the call gives them. The other option was a test at the call
    too; nothing in the compiler compares the two annotations there.
  - The machine with a heap of the first step of the heap has the two
    tests, in two definitions of its file, so that it agrees with
    `step`; its statements about rewrites keep their text.
  - The relation between handlers of the rewrites from a predicate
    (`handler_refines_from`) hands over the kinds of the handler, as
    the two older relations do.
  - Two definitions of the passes for loops gain a test, without which
    their statements are false once the machine tests kinds (the
    request, section 12, "Over the passes for loops").
    `shortcut_switch` asks that the handler give no kinds, as
    `thread_switch` does, and stays an equivalence. `loopable` asks
    that the parameters of the code be distinct, and `loopify_unit`
    stays a refinement with no premise. The other option for each was
    a premise on the statement (the unit is well formed, or well
    kinded), which the validator's theorem would then need. No example
    changes what it prints. To change either costs its own function
    and proof.

## The round trip of the conversion

The first two items of the plan's converter stream, small, at the
orchestrator's request and by V2's leave for its tool.

- `tests/tools/flmodel_to_fexpr.ml`: a term of the model printed back
  as fexpr. It is written from the model and the syntax of fexpr, not
  from the conversion. Its header lists what it does not print.
- `tests/tools/flroundtrip.ml`, behind `flsemantics.exe -round-trip`:
  convert, print, read again with the compiler's parser, convert again,
  compare with the model's comparison. For the unit of the file and
  for the unit after Simplify.
- `tests/semantics/roundtrip/`: generated rules for every example. An
  answer that is neither "the same" nor "not converted" fails until it
  is recorded there with its reason.
- Measured on the 99 examples: every unit that converts, before and
  after Simplify, round-trips the same, but one: the unit after
  Simplify of the new `kinds_direct_naked.fl`, written to show the
  known limit of fexpr (a direct call at naked arities: C2-5, R2-12).
- Over the epoch of exceptions the printer writes trap actions, the
  extra arguments of an exception continuation and the sort of an
  exception handler. The printer of before that epoch still compiles
  against the model, and drops the three: with it, 11 answers of the
  seven examples of exceptions stop being "the same" (five differ,
  six are not read back). So the check does find what the printer
  drops when the conversion keeps it.
- Over the primitives added since (the mutability of a load, the
  length of a string, the load of a byte from one, the unary
  arithmetic, the conversions between integer kinds, the negation of
  a boolean, physical equality, the shifts) the printer has a case
  for each. Tried against three mistakes put in those cases (two
  shifts exchanged, equality and inequality exchanged, the two widths
  of a load exchanged): each is reported as a difference.
- Tried against mistakes put in on purpose: the conversion exchanging
  the arguments of a binary primitive, the conversion adding one to
  the tag of a static block, and the printer exchanging the arguments
  of a binary primitive. Each is reported as a difference, with the
  line.
- After its review: a global name of the unit read back is taken for
  a number only if it is exactly what the printer makes of that
  number, and a name that is used and is not one fails the round trip
  ("names not read back"). Before, a symbol `s01` was read as the
  symbol 1. Tried against a printer that writes each symbol used as
  an argument with a `0` before its number: 115 of the 119 answers
  that were "the same" become "names not read back". A static set of
  closures with no symbols is refused by the printer, which looped on
  it. An example with no expected file must give exactly two answers,
  one for each unit.
- A poison constant is printed, with a name in quotes: the printer of
  fexpr writes the name as it is and the parser reads a string there.
  The eight examples made from OCaml sources that have the constant
  after Simplify now round-trip the same, and their records in
  `tests/semantics/roundtrip/` are removed. With the name that the
  compiler gives the constant, which has no quotes, the text is not
  read back: discrepancy C2-14.
- The two terms are compared with the value slots of each set of
  closures in the order of their numbers: the model's list is in the
  order of the compiler's map, which the text cannot keep (the parser
  makes a slot where the text first names it), and the lookup of a
  slot does not depend on where it stands in the list. It is a choice
  of what the round trip tells apart, not a proof of equivalence: a
  closure that is a value still holds its slots in the order of the
  term. Three units made from OCaml sources, in which a
  closure keeps a function of its module and an argument, differed by
  that order alone after Simplify. Tried against a printer that
  exchanges what the first two slots of a set hold: reported as a
  difference.
- What it is not: it shows that what the conversion makes of each
  example is stable under the round trip, not that the conversion is
  one to one. It does not find what the conversion drops and the
  printer does not put back, nor a mistake made alike in the two

  compiler's terms is still to be done.
- The default behaviour of the tool is unchanged, byte for byte: the

## Row 4 of well-formedness: static names in scope

Written for the integrator, who owns `FlambdaWellFormedSpec.v`, at
its request. The check, the theorem and the lines of the lists are
merged; the commit of the conversion (below) and the conjunct of
`well_formed_unit` are not. The files are
`FlambdaStaticsInScopeSpec.v` and `FlambdaStaticsInScopeProofs.v`.

- The check: `statics_in_scope imports e`, a boolean function, by the
  rule of scope of section 3 of `requests/C0-well-formed.md`, with the
  symbols that the unit imports as an argument; `static_names_apart
  imports e`, what goes with it of row 2 (no binding of an import; no
  code id both bound and declared deleted, nor declared deleted
  twice); `interface_conts_not_rebound u e`, that no `Let_cont` and no
  code binds the name of the return or the exception continuation of
  the unit.
- What a state holds: `value_statics_among`, `allocated_statics_among`
  (boolean), `value_statics_bound`, `state_statics_bound`; the fault
  `statics_scope_fault`, a boolean function of a state: its step looks
  up a symbol that is not bound, or code that is neither bound nor
  declared deleted. `imports_bound` says what static environment a
  unit is run from.
- Proved: `statics_bound_in_run_statement`. From a well-formed unit
  that passes `statics_in_scope` and `interface_conts_not_rebound`,
  run with its imports bound: every state of the run holds only bound
  symbols and closures of code that is bound or declared deleted, no
  step has the fault, and the values of an exit hold none either.
- Proved, a lemma for the lifting of code: `deleted_not_bound`. A unit
  that passes `static_names_apart` binds no code id that it declares
  deleted.
- Proved: `rebound_unit_exn_reads_unbound_statement`. A unit that
  binds the name of its exception continuation again passes
  `statics_in_scope`, `static_names_apart` and every condition of
  `well_formed_unit` but the discipline of trap actions, and its run
  reads a symbol that is not bound. Discrepancy C2-13.
- The test tool: the conversion leaves out the declaration that code
  is deleted where it gives the number of that code to the new version
  (the integrator's choice between two); with it every converted term
  passes `static_names_apart`. No expected output changes. That
  commit is the last of the series and lands after the validator's
  wiring, which holds the tool. Until it is in, `static_names_apart`
  fails on terms after Simplify that the tool converts (they declare
  deleted a code id that they bind): the check is not to be wired in
  the tool before it.
- `imports_bound` covers imports that are constants, such as the
  predefined exceptions, and no import that is a closure that can be
  called (the code of another unit is not bound); the comment at the
  definition says so.
- Corrected after it was merged: the check refused a unit that
  Simplify makes. For a match of three cases whose arms are lifted,
  Simplify defines the handler of the inner test above the group that
  binds the symbols of the arms, and that handler, which is not at
  toplevel, mentions them. The rule now has: what the body of a
  `Let_cont` binds on its spine (through `Let`s, `Let_static`s and the
  bodies of `Let_cont`s, in no handler) is in scope in its handlers,
  at toplevel or not, recursive or not; the machine binds all of it
  before the body applies a continuation. `spine_static_names` is the
  new definition. No statement changes text; the proof has one
  weakening more. An example of the shape is checked by computation
  (`nested_test_passes`, `nested_test_returns`). Found by the stream
  of the validator's lifting, on a unit made from an OCaml source.
  The measurement below had no unit of that shape.
- Then one clause in place of two: the handlers of a `Let_cont`,
  recursive or not, at toplevel or not, see all that the body of the
  `Let_cont` binds. It is what Simplify gives a handler: it simplifies
  the body first and adds the constants lifted meanwhile to the
  environment of each handler (`simplify/join_points.ml`,
  `compute_handler_env`; `simplify/simplify_let_cont_expr.ml`,
  `simplify_single_recursive_handler`). The clause is right only of a
  unit that passes row 1 and `interface_conts_not_rebound`, which are
  premises of the theorem already, and the header of the file says so:
  there a handler binds something static only if the body of its
  `Let_cont` can do nothing but apply it or leave the unit, so the
  body has bound all that it binds before control leaves it for a
  continuation that it does not bind. The check no longer reads
  whether a handler is at toplevel, so it loses two arguments, and
  `statics_in_scope` the interface; `spine_static_names` is gone. The
  theorem reads as before but for that argument. Two statements say
  what goes wrong without each of the two conditions:
  `rebound_unit_exn_reads_unbound_statement` (as before) and the new
  `branch_round_binding_reads_unbound_statement` (a unit that fails
  row 1 only, whose body goes round the handler that binds a symbol).
  Examples by computation: a recursive handler that mentions a symbol
  of the body; a handler that mentions a symbol bound in the handler
  of another continuation of the body; and a unit that leaves by its
  exception continuation before the handler that binds a symbol has
  run, which row 1 allows and the check accepts (no handler is
  entered).

## The prefix: what the `.cmx` files say of other units

Step 2 of `requests/C5-imports-with-contents.md`, in
`tests/tools/flsemantics.ml` (`make_prefix` and what is around it). No
`.v` file changes with it, and it is under no theorem of its own: it
is trusted OCaml, and the header of the tool says what is trusted of
it.

Done:

- After Simplify has run, the two terms of a check are given one
  prefix of static bindings, the same in front of both, made from the
  `.cmx` files of the units that the run read and of no other, through
  the compiler's loader and its reduction of the type of a symbol
  (`Flambda_cmx.load_symbol_approx`). It opens no file that the run
  did not read; of those files it may read the body of code that
  Simplify did not look at. It binds the data that the files
  reach, through fields, from the blocks that the two terms ask for (a
  block of values whose fields are constants and symbols; a closure at
  its function slot, alone in its set, with no value slot), and the
  bodies of the code that is asked for. The comment above
  `make_prefix` has the rule, which is that of section 3 of the
  request.
- What is not bound stays an import, or a code id bound to nothing: a
  block with a field of which nothing is said, code with no body in
  the file, tupled code, a body that the model has not got. A symbol
  that the file says is another symbol or a constant is refused.
- The prefix is checked before it is used: each name bound once, none
  that either unit binds, none still among the imports, and the groups
  evaluate from the static environment of the imports. A failure is
  reported as a fault of the conversion. A term after Simplify that is
  well-formed alone and not with the prefix in front is reported as
  that, and not as a term that Simplify made ill-formed.
- It is printed before the two runs, with the compiler's names, for
  reading against the other unit; what is bound without being asked
  for is counted on one line for each block that it is reached from.
  The line of the verdict says of how many units the files were used.
- A unit for which no `.cmx` file was read has no prefix and prints
  what it printed.

The pairs of `tests/semantics/ocaml/pairs/` are checked with it: five
of the seven second units have a prefix, and their expected files
changed with it (`pairs/README.md` says what each shows).

Measured as well, on units that are not in this tree: pairs of units
written by hand, each at the default flags and at `-O3`. A call of a
function of the other unit, at toplevel and in a function, inlined
or not; a function of a nested module; one reached through an alias
of a module; `include` of the other unit; fields read and kept; a
list of the other unit that a loop of this unit goes down: each is
validated with its prefix at the default flags, and the two runs are
defined and the same wherever the body called is in the file. At
`-O3` the last is not validated, for what Simplify does there with
the loop of the second unit itself. A call of code that has no body
in the file is undefined in both runs, and the validation then says
nothing. A module block with a field of which the file says nothing
(a value computed when the module is initialised, a closure that is
the result of a call) is not bound, and the unit is as it was
without a prefix: this is every unit that goes through the module of
the standard library that is opened at the start, until the model
can say that nothing is known of a field.

Most units with a prefix are validated by the comparison that
removes unused static bindings from the head of the term after, or by
a lifting, since the prefix has bindings that neither term uses; one
of the pairs, `lengths_called_at_toplevel`, is the same once
normalised. The first two are not among the verdicts that hold of a
unit with imports. So a unit with a prefix that is validated in one
of those two ways, and has an import left, a predefined exception
say, prints "validated with nothing else imported, given the .cmx of
N" and "not validated with its imports". The cure is not in the
prefix: it is those laws stated from a static environment that does
not bind the names removed.

Controls, not in this tree: a tool built with one deliberate fault in
the prefix for each check (a binding twice; a symbol that the unit
binds; a symbol left among the imports; a variable in a constant; a
closure at a function slot that its set has not got; two closures of
one code in one group). Each of the six is caught, with the message
of its check, and the tool exits with an error.

Decided overnight (2026-11-12), with C5, for the human to review:

- What is asked for. The question: does a block bound by the prefix
  bring with it everything that the files say of its fields, bodies
  included? The options: everything (the whole of a library in front
  of every unit that names it); only what the two terms load, the
  rest left as imports; the data reached through fields, with bodies
  only for the code asked for. The choice: the third, after the
  second was run and left an import in nearly every unit. What it
  costs to change: the walk in `bind_block`.
- A variable that a `Let` binds to another variable stands for
  nothing when a load is read against a block of the prefix. The
  other option follows such a `Let`. It costs a function of the other
  unit left unbound where a term reaches it so; no unit measured
  does.
- Tupled code of another unit is left unbound, and so is a body with
  a call of known arity where one of the two units has tupled code:
  the model has no direct call of tupled code. A call is then
  undefined.
- A fault of the prefix, and a term that is ill-formed only with the
  prefix, end the tool with an error, as a term after Simplify that
  is not well-formed does.

## What the text of a term loses of a call

Not in the integration branch when this was written.

The arity of a result. A call of unknown arity is printed with no
arity and read with one value for its result (label C2-5). Where the
compiler's call returned an unboxed pair or an unboxed number, the
term read has a call that returns one value to a continuation of two
parameters, or of one parameter that is not a value, and the tool's
Simplify stopped on it with a fatal error: `Invalid_argument` out of
`simplify/env/continuation_uses_env.ml`, where `add_continuation_use`
goes down the types of the result and the parameters together, or
"Kind mismatch upon meet" (label X5-12; X5 found it on two units
made from OCaml sources). The tool now tests the text as the parser
gives it (`result_arity_lost`; it goes by the identifiers of the
parsed text and makes no name of the compiler, so no expected file
moves) and, where a call of unknown arity returns to a continuation
in sight whose parameters are not one value, prints "not checked"
with the reason and does not run Simplify. A call is of unknown
arity as the reader has it: no arity written, or the arity of the
result written alone, which the reader drops. In sight means bound
by a `Let_cont` around the call, a name bound again hiding the one
before, or the return continuation of the code that the call is in.
The round trip is given the same reason. The arguments of such a
call are as before: the run of the term read is undefined where the
code called takes other kinds.

Examples, in `tests/semantics/`. The compiler's terms of X5's two
units: `unknown_arity_result_pair.fl`, `unknown_arity_result_int64.fl`.
Ten written by hand for the edges of the test, `arity_text_*.fl`.
Not checked: `no_parameter`; `result_arity_alone` (the arity of the
result written, that of the arguments not); `hidden_by_two_values`
(an inner continuation of two parameters hides an outer one of one);
`return_of_code` (the return continuation of code whose result is
two values); `recursive_invariant` (a recursive continuation with an
invariant parameter). Checked as before, each with a run that is
defined, and validated: `full_arity` (both arities written, two
results); `hidden_by_one_value`; `code_in_handler` (code bound in
the handler of a continuation of two parameters, whose own return
continuation has the same name); `recursive`; `never_returns` (a
call that never returns, beside a continuation of two parameters).

Probes. The conversion refuses a call that is a probe
(`convert_apply`, by `Unsupported`, before anything else of the call
is read). The program runs the handler of a probe only while the
probe is enabled; the model has no notion of that and would run it
always. The refusal goes by the mark that a term in memory has. The
text of a term has no such mark and its reader gives none: a unit
that has a probe of its own is read as a unit with a plain call and
is checked as one, and the refusal does not detect that. A term in
memory may have the mark, and the prefix converts such terms: the
bodies that it takes from `.cmx` files.
Before this change the prefix bound a body with a probe with the
handler's call as a plain call. In the pair written to show it, the
tool of the commit that brought the prefix printed a prefix of six
bindings, the body among them with the call of the handler; both
runs were undefined there (the handler calls a function whose code
has no body in the `.cmx`), so no verdict was wrong by it. That
says nothing of a pair where the runs are defined: taking a probe
for a plain call was a mistake of the conversion, not a harmless
reading. Now the conversion of the body fails, `bind_code` of
`make_prefix` leaves the code unbound as it does for any body that
does not convert, and the unit is not refused for it: the closure
is bound, a call of it is undefined. Where the tool's Simplify
inlines such a body into the unit, the term after has the probe
and the unit is not checked. The pair is in the tree:
`tests/semantics/ocaml/pairs/probed.ml` and `probed_called.ml`,
with `probed_inlined.ml` for the second way. Label C2-17.

Phantom bindings (a later series; not in the integration branch
when this was written). The conversion refuses a binding whose
variables are phantom (`convert_let`, before any of its cases, so
that the bindings of regions and of depths, which are dropped, are
covered too). The program runs nothing for such a binding: the
backend compiles its body alone. The model runs every `Let`. With
`-g` and phantom lets on (`-flambda2-expert-phantom-lets`, which
`-gdwarf-may-alter-codegen-experimental` also sets), Simplify may
keep an unused binding as phantom instead of deleting it; the term
that the compiler gives to Simplify has none. The text of a term
does not say the mode, and the reader gives the normal one: the
refusal cannot detect a phantom binding whose mode a text has lost,
as for probes. A term in memory has the mode: a body taken from the
`.cmx` of a unit compiled with those flags, and the term after when
the tool is given them. Before this change the conversion did not read the mode
and converted the binding as a `Let` that runs. In the pair written
to show it, the body of a function has a phantom load of the first
field of its argument, the code made of it loads nothing, and a
unit calls it with the number 0: the prefix bound the body with the
load, and the run before was undefined at the load, where the
program returns 0. On a run where the term before is undefined, any
term after refines it in the model, so the theorem of a validation
says nothing of the term after there, and this reading could
conceal a wrong term after (which is not to say that the validator
accepts every such pair); the pair did not show one, and nothing
wider was measured. Now the body is left out of the prefix, as any body that
does not convert, and a unit whose term after has such a binding is
not checked. The pair is in the tree:
`tests/semantics/ocaml/pairs/phantom.ml` (compiled with the two
flags, by a line of the source that the rules read, and checked
with them: not checked) and `phantom_called.ml`. Label C2-19. It
was found by reading the conversion against every field of the
terms, which the table below was not.

What else a term in memory has that a text cannot say, and what the
conversion does with it. Two routes bring the conversion such a
term. The term after Simplify is one, and was before the prefix:
where the tool's Simplify inlines a body that it read from a `.cmx`,
the term after has what that body has (`probed_inlined.ml` is the
witness). The prefix is a new and direct one: the bodies that it
binds were never a text. The list is what the comparison of the
compiler's terms with the terms read back found (X5's work, not in
this tree), read against the conversion case by case. "Not read"
means that the conversion does not look at the field, so the term
of the model is the same whatever it holds.

| What a text cannot say | In a term in memory, the conversion | Why that is right, or what is trusted |
|---|---|---|
| That a call is a probe | refuses the call | this series; label C2-17 |
| That a binding is phantom | refuses the binding | the later series; label C2-19. The first form of this table did not have the row, and the conversion took the binding for a `Let` that runs |
| The arity of the result of a call of unknown arity | converts it from the call, with its unboxed products, and checks it against the compiler's own unarizing | faithful; the loss is of the text alone (X5-12) |

So of the things that a text cannot say, two were taken for
something else: the probe, and the phantom binding, which this
table did not list until the conversion was read field by field.
The others are refused, converted from the term, or not read, with
the reason in each row. What this table is not: a reading of every
case of the conversion against `terms/` for terms in memory; that
reading is being made, and is not in this tree.

Decided overnight (2026-11-13), for the human to review:

- The verdict for a text that has lost the arity of a result is "not
  checked", as for a construct that the model has not got, and the
  tool ends without an error. The other option was "not read", which
  the tables use for a text that the parser refuses; this text is
  read, to a term that is not the compiler's. What it costs to
  change: one string.
- A body with a probe is left out of the prefix and the unit is
  still checked, as for any body that does not convert. The other
  option was to refuse the unit. Left out, a run that calls the body
  is undefined, so nothing is validated through it.
- A phantom binding is refused. The other options: to drop it, as
  the backend does, which is what the program runs; or to keep it as
  a `Let` that runs, which is right only if a phantom binding
  promises that its defining expression would be defined. Nothing
  in the compiler says which: Simplify simplifies the defining
  expression of a binding whatever its mode, and the backend runs
  nothing. The refusal takes no side; recommended for the human: to
  drop it. What it costs to change: the case in `convert_let`, and a
  set of variables that may not be used as values, as for regions.
