# C2, kinds and well-formed terms: status

Branch `flambda2-rocq/C2-epoch`: the core change as a series for C0 to
apply, with the request `requests/C2-kinds.md` (third version, revised
for the rebase over C3's epoch and for the review of the series). The
prototype and the experiments on the compiler are on
`flambda2-rocq/C2-kinds`, which is not for merging.

## Done

- Step A: terms carry kinds and arities, and the machine ignores them.
  All the files of the two Flambda 2 libraries are repaired.
- Step B: a call is undefined unless it agrees with the code called
  (three tests in `call_function`); `inline_known_calls` inlines only a
  call that is sure to agree.
- Arities are the compiler's trees (`Singleton`, `Unboxed_product`), in
  `FlambdaModel.v`: the review of the second version showed that flat
  lists of kinds run calls that the compiler makes `Invalid`.
- `FlambdaWellKindedSpec.v`, `FlambdaWellKindedProofs.v`: the check, the
  invariant of a state, nine statements, all proved, with no premise
  about the primitives (C1's two laws), and examples on each side.
- Rebased over C3's epoch. The check has cases for the closure of a set
  of several functions, the projection of a function slot, the
  projection of a value slot from a named function slot, the static
  closure of a set and the two new closure values. C3's four statements
  about a `Let` or a call, its step lemmas and its example of two
  functions that call each other carry the annotations;
  `step_apply_closure` has the three tests as premises.
- The test tool converts kinds, arities and the kinds of value slots,
  prints what the check answers, refuses a direct partial or
  over-application and code that is tupled, and reports a run that
  stops at a partial or over-application, after which it decides
  nothing and attempts no validation. Ten new examples. No example
  there was changes what it prints, but for the new line; the fifteen
  units made from OCaml sources that convert pass the check before
  Simplify and after.
- The trust ledger's entries for what the converter is trusted with,
  and for what the tool does about partial applications,
  over-applications and tupled functions.
- The review of the series (R2), answered in the series: the request,
  section 10, lists the points.
- Nine discrepancies with the compiler recorded and reported, C2-1 to
  C2-9, updated for the rulings and for the review.

## Decided overnight (2026-11-11), for the human to review

- D10, the rest. The question: is a value of another kind than its
  binder's undefined where it is bound (by a `Let`, or as a parameter
  of a handler)? The options: (a) the machine does not test, and the
  check of kinds answers no for such a term; (b) the machine tests at
  each binder. The choice (the orchestrator's, on my recommendation):
  (a) in this series, with the CR in `FlambdaSemanticsSpec.v` before
  `step`; (b) as a separate series after it, since the types need "a
  variable holds a value of the kind of its binder" with no premise.
  Why: (a) changes no statement and lets the three epochs that queue
  behind this one land; (b) touches 71 proofs. What it costs to change:
  the request, section 9 (two tests in the machine, no change to the
  terms; five statements change or gain a premise).
- Tupled functions. The question: what the tool does with code that is
  tupled, which the model has no mark for. The choice: the converter
  refuses it. V2 has a finer rule (refuse a unit with tupled code and a
  call that is not direct), which I agreed to: it replaces mine when
  V2's series is rebased over this one, with the ledger's sentence.
- Partial applications and over-applications. C3 has written, with
  me, the request that puts them in the machine (on its branch, not
  merged): my three tests are made in every branch;
  `partial_or_over_application` and its two helpers move to
  `FlambdaModel.v`; my statement that such a call is undefined goes
  then.

## The round trip of the conversion (on `flambda2-rocq/C2-roundtrip`)

The first two items of the plan's converter stream, small, at the
orchestrator's request and by V2's leave for its tool.

- `tests/tools/flmodel_to_fexpr.ml`: a term of the model printed back
  as fexpr. It is written from the model and the syntax of fexpr, not
  from the conversion. Its header lists what it does not print.
- `tests/tools/flroundtrip.ml`, behind `flsemantics.exe -round-trip`:
  convert, print, read again with the compiler's parser, convert again,
  compare with the model's comparison. For the unit of the file and
  for the unit after Simplify.
- `tests/semantics/roundtrip/`: generated rules for every example. An
  answer that is neither "the same" nor "not converted" fails until it
  is recorded there with its reason.
- Measured on the 99 examples: every unit that converts, before and
  after Simplify, round-trips the same, but one: the unit after
  Simplify of the new `kinds_direct_naked.fl`, written to show the
  known limit of fexpr (a direct call at naked arities: C2-5, R2-12).
- Over the epoch of exceptions the printer writes trap actions, the
  extra arguments of an exception continuation and the sort of an
  exception handler. The printer of before that epoch still compiles
  against the model, and drops the three: with it, 11 answers of the
  seven examples of exceptions stop being "the same" (five differ,
  six are not read back). So the check does find what the printer
  drops when the conversion keeps it.
- Over the primitives added since (the mutability of a load, the
  length of a string, the load of a byte from one, the unary
  arithmetic, the conversions between integer kinds, the negation of
  a boolean, physical equality, the shifts) the printer has a case
  for each. Tried against three mistakes put in those cases (two
  shifts exchanged, equality and inequality exchanged, the two widths
  of a load exchanged): each is reported as a difference.
- Tried against mistakes put in on purpose: the conversion exchanging
  the arguments of a binary primitive, the conversion adding one to
  the tag of a static block, and the printer exchanging the arguments
  of a binary primitive. Each is reported as a difference, with the
  line.
- After its review: a global name of the unit read back is taken for
  a number only if it is exactly what the printer makes of that
  number, and a name that is used and is not one fails the round trip
  ("names not read back"). Before, a symbol `s01` was read as the
  symbol 1. Tried against a printer that writes each symbol used as
  an argument with a `0` before its number: 115 of the 119 answers
  that were "the same" become "names not read back". A static set of
  closures with no symbols is refused by the printer, which looped on
  it. An example with no expected file must give exactly two answers,
  one for each unit.
- What it is not: it shows that what the conversion makes of each
  example is stable under the round trip, not that the conversion is
  one to one. It does not find what the conversion drops and the
  printer does not put back, nor a mistake made alike in the two
  directions. The reading of the conversion case by case against the
  compiler's terms is still to be done.
- The default behaviour of the tool is unchanged, byte for byte: the
  expected files of the examples pass as they are.
